# IV. The Stag, the Cave, and the Cultist
*In which the forest is read more truly, a silence is met, and a confession is torn from the dark*

@audio: bloodrot-wood fade=6
They went back to the wood at @[Darathra](darathra)'s asking — they knew the site of @[Sera](sera)'s death better now than any of her own people, and she wanted whatever the forest could still be made to tell, brought back to her and to no one else. Besides which, the wood was where the answers were bleeding from.

Gwaeron's Rest had not healed in their absence. If anything it had worsened, and this time the party looked at it with clearer eyes and saw what they had missed before: the black ichor was not seeping up from the ground. It was growing out of the living things themselves — weeping from the pores of leaf and bark and stem, as though the plants were producing it, as though the corruption were being made by the very life it was killing rather than poured over it from outside. It thickened as they pressed deeper into the trees and thinned as they looked back toward the town, a gradient with its source somewhere ahead of them, in the heart of the wood where the sickness was worst.

*And there was one place that broke the pattern. The clearing where they had found Sera's body — where she had been slain in that deliberate, ritual way — was not sick at all. It thrived. Healthy green stood there, untouched, lush, an island of life in a forest turning to rot around it, as though something spilled in that spot had pushed the corruption back rather than fed it. The party marked the strangeness and did not yet understand it. For now it was only one more thing that did not fit: the murder site, alone in all the wood, was clean.*

---

@audio: none fade=6
Deeper in, the forest went quiet. Not the ordinary hush of a wood at midday but a true and total silence, the birdsong gone, the small rustlings stilled, the air itself seeming to hold its breath. And in that silence, five hundred feet off through the trees, a stag was standing.

It was enormous, and it was watching them, and it did not move. They went toward it and the distance did not close; it remained always at the far edge of sight, neither fleeing nor approaching, simply present and apart, fixed in place as surely as if it had been painted onto the forest. There was nothing natural in the stillness of it. It was too large, too calm, too aware, and it held itself at exactly the remove of a thing that wished to be seen but not reached — that had come to the edge of the party's world to look at them, and could come no nearer.

*It was @[Corvyn](corvyn) who thought of a way to close a distance that would not be closed. He turned his back to the stag and walked in reverse toward it, step by careful step, while the others held the creature in their gaze and called the range to him — nearer, nearer, there — and when their voices told him he stood beside it, he turned. The clearing was empty. No stag; no sound of one leaving; only hoofprints pressed into the earth where it had stood. He reached for the Weave then, and looked at the ground with a spell's borrowed sight, and the prints answered what the eye could not: they glowed faintly with enchantment. Natural magic, old and deep, the signature of something far greater than a beast. And where the party stepped into those prints, something settled over them — a boon, a quickening. Four of them, Corvyn and @[Amyl](amyl) and @[Sov](sov) and @[Iratius](iratius), felt their senses sharpen and their footing in the world grow surer, as though the forest itself had agreed, for a while, to show them its truths more plainly. It was a gift. It felt like a gift. It felt, though none of them could have said why, like being set right by something that wished them well and could not say so aloud.*

They looked for the stag afterward, out at the far edge of sight where it had held its vigil, and the trees were empty there too. It had watched them exactly as long as it meant to, and gone without a sound, and the party went on with the wood's blessing on them and no idea whose blessing it truly was.

@audio: bloodrot-wood fade=2
The forest did not stay generous. Deeper still, the ichor-sickness rose to meet them in a form that could bite: a swarm of the wood's small creatures — rabbits, squirrels, raccoons, otters — boiling out of the underbrush, and every one of them wrong. The black ichor slicked their fur and clouded their eyes, and they moved with the same not-quite-alive wrongness the party had first seen in the rats beneath the town, driven at the living by something that had unmade what they were. @[Unmoored](the-unmoored), was the word that would come to fit such things. Loosed from their own natures. Made into weapons by a corruption that did not so much kill as rewrite.

The party put them down. But two things stood out from the fight, and both were worth remembering. The first was that the ichor would not cling to them — it slicked the beasts and slipped from the party's skin, and from @[Nik](nik)'s, finding no purchase on any of them; whether because their time in the sick wood had been too brief, or for some other reason, none of them could say. The second was that @[Sov](sov), testing a hunch, pressed the broken Sharran pendant they had taken from Sera's hand against one of the fallen creatures — and nothing happened. No reaction, no recognition, no answer. Whatever bound the corruption to this wood, that little symbol of Shar was not the key to it, or not the whole of the key. The party filed both facts away and pressed on toward the place where the wood had gone worst.

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=5
What they found was not the worst of the sickness but the absence of everything. A clearing with nothing in it — no plants, no ichor, no life and no corruption either, simply bare and silent ground, as though this were the eye of it all, the still point the rot spiraled out from. And at the edge of that dead clearing, a cave mouth breathed faint smoke into the cold air.

*Amyl went ahead in the shape of a ferret, small and low and quiet, and came back with what he had heard: someone was in there, and someone was in pain — a low, pained grunting from somewhere in the dark. And there was one thing more, a thing that Amyl and Corvyn both noticed and neither liked. The presence — the buried, frightened thing beneath Triboar that had followed them at the edge of feeling since the rat-hole — was not here. This close to the heart of the corruption, the thing they had come to carry always at the back of the mind had gone entirely absent, as though it could not reach this place, or would not come near it.*

Inside, at the entrance, a note had been left where it would be found. The hand was the same firm, unhurried script they had drawn from a severed grip a wood and a lifetime ago. It read: “@[The light blinds the veil](the-harvest). —Verity.” And past the note, in the smoke and the dark, they found her: a woman in Sharran robes, one-handed, the stump of her wrist recently and deliberately made. The Maimed Cultist. She had cut the hand from her own arm.

*She did not wait to be questioned. You should not have come here, she said, and came at them — one-handed, burning with a fanatic's disregard for what was left of her own body, and the cave rang with the fight of it. It was in the middle of that fury that @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s charm found her and stopped her like a hand laid flat on a struck bell. The fight went out of the room. Gentled, opened to speaking, she gave them the first true confession of the campaign. She had taken her own hand for Shar's favor — a price paid gladly, for the greater good, she said, and she believed it; she was certain the Lady of Loss was pleased with what she had done. And she had killed Sera. She said it plainly. Sera's death had been a working, a ritual, a thing done for a purpose — and she had been its instrument, and she did not repent of it.*

*It was when the party told her they had come from Verity that the ground shifted under her. She went still, and then afraid — not of them, but of what they had said. That was not possible. It could not be. Whoever Verity was, the Cultist knew something about that name that made the party's easy claim to it impossible, and the certainty drained out of her and left something confused and frightened in its place. And then @[Tirig](tirig), who could wear any shape, wore Sera's — stepped forward wearing the face of the woman this cultist had murdered — and whatever was left of the Cultist's composure came apart entirely. She broke, and fled, deeper into the cave, into the dark past the reach of the party's light, carrying her confusion and her terror down into the earth. The party stood at the mouth of the deep dark she had vanished into. Whatever waited further in, she had run to it rather than face them. They went after her.*
