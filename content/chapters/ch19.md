# XIX. The Thin Place
*In which the rhyme is hunted and not found, and something worse is*

@audio: bloodrot-wood fade=6
The commission changed the shape of the camps overnight. The trial-bowl became a map room; the wardens became surveyors; and the party became, by the strangest promotion of their strange year, the resident experts — because of everyone under the boughs, only they had seen what a murdered place looks like from inside. They knew the rhyme to hunt for. Couplings and collars. Black glass standing in the seams of the world. Cellars of ash-marked worship, liturgy in a devout and careful hand, a stone that drinks. The circles gave them wardens, ladders, lamps, and the run of the deep root-country, and they went looking for @[Triboar](triboar) under the oldest thing alive.

The custodian came to the survey on the first morning with two years of ledgers under one arm and an apology under the other. Her name was @[Berra](berra). She had kept the master's grove since the raising was discovered — logged the mosslight nightly through two winters, and testified against @[Amyl](amyl) with shaking hands, and felt the dead man's peace along with sixty seniors, and had not slept properly since. The apology got as far as “Warden, at the trial I —” before Amyl closed both her hands inside one of his. “You read what you saw,” he said. “So did I. Bring the ledgers.” They were inseparable within a day, in the way of two people who have kept the same dead man, and the party found their arguments about observation intervals strangely restful to fall asleep to.

The rhyme was not there. That was the first week's finding, and it should have been a relief, and it curdled into something else entirely. No cult sign in any hollow. No worked glass in any seam but the vault's one listening pin. No stone, no rites, no ash — the wardens swept a country the size of a barony and found nothing but forest and the forest's sickness. At Triboar the horror had machinery; you could hate it, cut it, burn it. Here the horror was that there was none. And meanwhile the survey's charts filled in, vein by blackened vein, grove by grove, and every line of them agreed: the sickness ran downhill of the world, all of it, converging on one region of the deep root-country on the Tree's north side, where none of the circles' paths went.

They went in on the third day, following the veins home. The root-country there is a landscape, not a floor — roots the size of city walls, valleys of moss between them, canopy so high and total that noon arrives as a green dusk — and as they walked, the green dusk stopped being green. The shadows changed allegiance first: pooling against the lamplight instead of away from it, gathering in the low places like water finding a drain, exactly as they had pooled once in a crypt under Silverymoon, and @[Sov](sov)'s step was the first to slow. Then the day itself began to fail by degrees that had nothing to do with the canopy — a dimming with a border to it, and they crossed the border at what their bones insisted was midday, into a standing twilight that did not belong to any hour.

The wound sat in a clearing of clenched roots, and the record will describe it as carefully as it can, because nothing about it was dramatic and everything about it was wrong. There was no arch, no cave, no door. There was a place where distance stopped working: a standing depth of dusk, perhaps forty paces across, deepening toward a heart of true dark that the eye could not find the far side of — not blackness but fartherness, a hole made of away. Its edges frayed into the ordinary air like wet paper. Cold came off it in the slow, breathing way the party knew from a cave mouth in the Spine and a mirror underground — a cold none of them could name and all of them had met — the cave's cold, the mirror's cold, the cold of their shared gray sleep — a cold with a country behind it. And nowhere — they searched for an hour, dreading the finding, dreading the not-finding more — was there one splinter of machinery. Nothing built. Nothing worked. The thing had not been made. It had happened, the way rot happens, the way a river changes course: the world worn thin past bearing, and giving way at last where the fabric was oldest.

And around it, @[the Tree](the-grandfather-tree) fought. That was the last understanding, and it rearranged every chart they had drawn. The roots ringing the clearing were clenched — grown dense and knuckled into a gripping ring, wood strained visibly against nothing visible — and where they gripped hardest, they were gray. Not blighted: spent. @[Amyl](amyl) walked the ring with his palms on the wood and wept without noticing he was doing it. “It is not being murdered,” he said. “It is dying in battle. Every black vein in this forest is the Tree spending itself into this grip — it has been holding this door shut alone, for a season, and paying for it out of its own body, and we called the cost a disease.”

@[Sov](sov) took his first step toward the dark before anyone saw him decide to — a second step — and @[Tirig](tirig)'s hand closed on his arm, and he stopped, and looked down at her hand as though it belonged to a language he was relearning. He was pale in a way the cold did not explain. What he reported, later, carefully, choosing every word with tongs: it had not spoken, and it had not needed to. Something across that fartherness knew his other half by name. “It did not feel like a threat,” he said, and made himself finish. “It felt like being expected.” Nobody assigned a watch on Sov Thimron that evening. The party simply rearranged itself around him, the way it had once rearranged itself around him in a city, without a word, and he let it happen, which was how they knew he was frightened too.

At the dusk's border they found the rest of the @[black glass](the-glass), and the finding turned the whole case over in their hands. A ring of pins, twins to the vault's, seated at measured intervals in root and soil around the wound — and not one of them driving, not one of them drinking. @[Amyl](amyl) read three in a row with his palms and stood up looking like a man who has misjudged a stranger. “They listen,” he said. “All of them. They only listen.” Someone had fenced the wound with thermometers — and in an oilskin cache beneath a marker-stone, the field-charts to match the vault's: columns by the tenday, widths and depths and the dusk's slow gain, in that same devout unhurried hand, with a line of liturgy at the season's head thanking no god by name that the tide was rising on schedule. The vault's pin, re-read in this light, was no thorn; it was a gauge in a dying patient. The logs were not recipes. They were readings — kept the way a physician charts a fever, or an actuary a life — and the earliest page was dated to high summer: weeks before Harvestide, a season before anyone had heard the word @[Fundament](fundament). It was Amyl who said the thing that resorted everything, quietly, to the pins as much as the people: “At Triboar they cut on purpose. Here —” he turned a chart-page over, and his gentleness had never sounded less gentle — “here they needed only to watch.”

And at dawn the dusk sent them the stag. It came out of the border at first light, straight at the survey camp, and there was nothing of flight in it: a red stag in its prime, or what had been one — @[black threads risen under the hide](the-unmoored) like ink in water, eyes gone to something that had stopped being a stag's business — and it hit the picket like a battering ram with opinions. A warden went ten feet and did not immediately get up. @[Tirig](tirig)'s first arrow took it clean behind the shoulder and the wound closed around the shaft, black and unbothered; @[Iratius](iratius)'s fire turned it, finally, the way fire had turned such things before, and for a hundred heartbeats the camp fought a beautiful animal in the half-dark and hated every second — hooves like mauls, the wrong vitality, wounds that drank themselves shut — and in the middle of it, antlers came up against the dawn sky in silhouette, and for one terrible second every one of them thought of another stag, in a resting wood, in another life: a stag that had walked ahead of them once like a blessing and led them true. Not this one. Never this one. But the shape was the shape, and the shape hurt worse than the hooves.

It was @[Amyl](amyl) who ended it, because it had to be Amyl. He put himself inside the hooves' reach with that unhurried bulk that was its own argument, took the antlers in both hands, bore the ruined head down gently to the frost — and did what he had done for a bear once, and for this forest's smaller casualties all season: the mercy, quick and quiet and his. Afterward he knelt with it a moment and closed its eyes and apologized, in the low voice he used for such apologies, for the world it had been standing in. And @[Saeron](saeron) knelt at the beast's other side when it was done, and said words over it — brief, unasked, in the language of endurance that nobody needed translated by now — because the pattern of him, the party was slowly assembling, made no distinctions that the suffering itself did not make. Berra stood witness through all of it with her ledgers clutched to her chest — the custodian learning, at last, what two years of tidy columns had been counting toward.

The survey went back up to the bowl that day and gave the circles the word the North had not used in an age of the world, and the word went through the camps by evening like frost through cloth: crossing. A door standing open under the @[Grandfather Tree](the-grandfather-tree) — to where, no art of the circles could say, and no heart in the camps wanted answered — grown and not made, wider by a hand-width than the morning's chart allowed. That night the circles of the North — who between them remembered everything — began, for the first time in living green memory, learning something new: how to hold a border against a place.
