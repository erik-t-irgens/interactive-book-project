# XXXVIII. Ascore
*In which a bridge crosses nothing, the sun is one again, and the elder work is asked to keep holding*

@audio: fundament-doubled fade=6
The morning of the departure came with a runner at the door before the bread was cut: a folded note in a muster-hand, terse as a roster. The intake's advance lists are down on my wards this week and I am needed at the tables. Supper stands. When you return. That is an order. — D. The party read it around in silence, and pocketed, along with the note, the small cold question of what an advance list is, and for whom, and how far in advance; and the @[archivist](the-archivist) arrived on the runner's heels with everything else: the Seeker's charts in an oiled case, letters of passage sealed with no name, a cart provisioned to a standard that suggested the city had opinions about its guests returning, and directions to the eastern quarter, where, she said, they would find the bridge. “There is only one,” she said. “The city keeps two doors, and you have used the other. Water behind, sand ahead. It is the kind of arrangement that makes theologians of teamsters.”

@audio: none fade=6
The eastern quarter climbs. The party led the cart up switchback streets through districts still half-derelict — scaffolds and lamplit crews thinning as the houses thinned, the elder stone standing more and more alone — until the last street ended at a gatehouse of the old work, and beyond the gatehouse was the bridge, and the party stood at its head for a while in the particular silence they keep for the elder city's arguments. It runs out from the city's eastern rampart in a single span. There is no far tower visible from the gate; the span simply goes, level and pale and gently humming underfoot, out over the eastern band of the nothing — for the seam has two shores, and the fade the ferries ride is only the western one — a bridge over un-place, railed in stone worn glass-smooth at hand height by centuries of exactly this: people crossing the end of the world with their hands on the rail. Below it, nothing; not depth, not dark; the same canceled stillness, breathing its slow tide. The cart-horse went blindfolded, and went calmly, which said something about the horses of this city that nobody wished to pursue.

They crossed, and the world came back by degrees, and the party met the returning world the way you meet a friend reported dead. First the light began to lean — to lean, to come from somewhere — the flat doubled radiance of the seam resolving stride by stride into direction, warmth, angle; then the pale stone of the span took on gold at its edges; then their shadows, which had walked beside them in twos since a ferry, quietly became one shadow each; and then they walked off the end of the bridge onto sand and stone at the rim of the great desert, under the enormous ordinary sky of the material world, and the sun — one sun, the sun, low and red-gold over Anauroch's rim — stood in it. The party stopped. The cart stopped. Nobody had planned to stop. @[Tirig](tirig) turned her face up into the light with her eyes shut, and stood that way a long time, a huntress letting the one true sky read her for a change; @[Amyl](amyl) put his blistered forearms out into the warmth like a man at a hearth; @[Iratius](iratius) took off a gauntlet, slowly, and held his bare hand in the sun, and looked at it — the light lying on his empty hand, free, unpurchased, owed to no one — and put the gauntlet back on without a word; and @[Sov](sov) stood at the bridge's end with the faint winter of the seam in his chest and the whole warm obvious world in his face, and laughed — once, short, astonished — the first laugh this book has been able to report of him since a cellar. They had been out of the world since the Feast of the Moon. The world, it transpired, had kept their places.

@audio: triboar-hearth fade=8
@[Ascore](ascore) is a day's ride northeast along the desert's edge, and the ride was almost more than the party's discipline could bear, because it was pleasant. Hard ground, honest distances, a wind with temperature in it; hawks; once, far off, a line of wild camels the charts had no opinion about. They camped that night in the lee of a dune with a driftwood fire that crackled — crackled; @[Corvyn](corvyn) fed it splinters just to hear it — and above them stood the stars, the whole extravagant commonplace vault of them, and no one said anything for the first hour, and @[Aelendir](aelendir), who has walked under those stars for four hundred years, was heard to hum. In the morning they raised Ascore.

@audio: ascore-hush fade=8
The dead city announces itself the way dwarven work always does: by refusing to fall down. Brass-brown ramparts standing out of the sand at the desert's rim; gate-towers with their doors long gone and their lintels immortal; streets drifted to the knee and colonnades drifted to the shoulder, and everywhere the deep-cut geometry of Delzoun, the old Northkingdom, squared and proud and empty these thousands of years. The party went in on foot with the charts, through avenues where the wind has been the only citizen for an age. The silence of the place deserves its own sentence, because everything that happened next happened inside it: a hush with a floor and walls, thousands of years deep, the kind of quiet that is not an absence but an accumulation — and the party, without discussing it, had begun to walk softly, the way you lower your voice in another faith's church. And for a while, Ascore rewarded the manners. They gave themselves an hour before the work — the charts furled, the cart left in shade — and wandered: a market square where the stall-stones still stood in their honest rows under three feet of sand; a hall of friezes where dwarven smiths hammered at dwarven forges in relief, patient as the day they were cut; a fountain bowl scoured mirror-smooth by ten thousand years of wind, in which Corvyn set one pebble ringing, softly, and then apologized to the square at large, and meant it, and the silence took the sound and folded it away. It was, all seven have said since, one of the gentlest hours of the whole long year — the calm laid down so completely that what happened next happened to people who had stopped expecting anything to happen at all.

The first tap came from nowhere and belonged to nothing. A single concussion, enormous and distant and directionless — stone struck under stone, somewhere, everywhere — rolling down the drifted avenues and returning off ten thousand years of walls until the sound had no source left at all; and the party stood in the middle of it turning slowly in place, hands half to weapons, looking aimlessly up and around at the empty city like farmers reading a clear sky for thunder. Then two more: TAP. TAP. Closer. Under. Nobody named it, because nobody could. And then the street at the square's far end opened, and it came up through the paving the way a whale breaches — masonry parting around it like water and closing behind it barely disturbed — three men high and headless, stone flesh smooth and blind and gray as everything it swam through, hands broad as temple doors, and where a face should never be, in the belly of it, a great fanged mouth, working. It stood in the ruined street, tasting the air. The party did not decide to hide; hiding happened to them, seven bodies finding colonnade shadow in one heartbeat of pure animal consensus. And the thing turned its blind bulk once, all the way around, and slid back down into the ground like a diver, and the paving closed over it, and the silence came back entire — seamless, ten thousand years deep — as though the city had dreamed it.

The second breach chose a target. One tap — a different quarter, behind them now — and the ground at @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s heels opened without ceremony, and a hand the size of a temple door came up out of the flagstones, reaching not for him but for the pack on his back, where a @[small bottle](the-shining-bottle) had been shining its honey-and-morning shine into the dark of the canvas since the Hells; and Corvyn Stonemason's-son survived it by a hand's width and someone else's reflexes — @[Amyl](amyl)'s staff hooking his collar and hauling him clean out of his own footprints as the stone fingers closed on the space he had been, took a fistful of drifted sand and a century of paving, weighed the handful, and poured it out, disappointed. The arm withdrew. The ground closed. And into the ringing stillness @[Aelendir](aelendir) shouted one word from behind his column — “@[Thaalud](the-thaalud)!” — and got no further, because the third breach was already choosing, and it chose @[Sov](sov), or rather the pale blade slung at Sov's back; and as seven people scattered across the square in seven directions, diving down stairwells and behind fallen lintels, the whole of an old elf's learning compressed itself under pressure into the only three words that mattered, flung across the chaos at the top of his voice: “Magic! It wants magic!”

What followed was a quarter hour that the party spent as the ruins had taught them by example: silent, patient, dispersed, and moving only when the moving could not be heard. They learned the rules the way you learn ice — by weight, wincing: that it rose to sound and to shine and was blind to all else; that its stops were worse than its passages, the whole square holding its breath around a listening; that the cargo had to go apart and go dark — @[Sov](sov) wrapping @[Bayne](bayne) in his coat and holding it to his chest like a fevered child, smothering the hum of it; @[Iratius](iratius) laying scabbarded @[Volcaris](volcaris) flat in a drift and stepping away from his family like a man leaving a lit fuse; @[Tirig](tirig) sliding the length of a gallery one heartbeat before a hand swam through the wall where she had been and withdrew, trailing powdered stone, unhurried, certain there was ore in this seam somewhere. It was @[Saeron](saeron) who ended it, with the Seeker's second-best resonance rod and the best throw of his life. They chose the sacrifice by triage at twenty feet and in total silence — @[Aelendir](aelendir)'s fingers moving over the instrument case, selecting the one whose loss the readings could survive — and on the next passage, in the full grinding roar of the thing's transit, Saeron stepped from cover, turned twice like a hammer-thrower, and put the charged rod in a long flat arc over the eastern wall, out of the city entire, far into the open dunes. The tapping stopped. The whole world stood still around the absence of it. And then the thaalud turned — ponderous, total, a landslide changing its mind — and went after the brighter vein: through the wall, through the rampart's root, down; the taps dwindling under the earth, fainter, fainter, like weather leaving a country; until the last of it faded below anything but the soles of their boots, and then below that too.

The hush of Ascore came back down over the streets the way the desert comes back over footprints — seamless, unhurried, as though the city had merely cleared its throat after ten thousand years and resumed — and the party came up out of stairwells and shadows one by one, bruised where masonry had shed on them, breathing like swimmers, and gathered their scattered shining things, and only then, with the square quiet and the cart-horse being talked down from its opinions, did @[Aelendir](aelendir) give them the rest of the word he had shouted. Thaalud. Tomb tapper. Netheril built them, in the deep arrogance of the old magocracy, to mine — to smell out magic through solid rock, ton by ton, and dig it home to their masters' floating cities: tireless, deathless, loyal as arithmetic. “Netheril has been dust for seventeen centuries,” he said, repacking the instrument case and entering the rod's loss in his precise clerk's hand as instrumentation expended in the field, which was true. “The cities fell. The masters burned. Nobody told it. It has been down there ever since, working its shift, waiting to be paid.” And the party looked out at the drifted, patient, workless city with its ten thousand years of hush, and heard the silence differently for the rest of the day.

They went on after that, quieter than they had come, keeping to the colonnades' shade out of an instinct none of them examined too closely; and the deeper they went the older it got, and then — in the city's heart, where the charts drew their sigil — the dwarven work stopped. Not ended: stopped, and stood back. The great flagged plaza at Ascore's center is dwarven at its rim and is not dwarven at its middle; the paving changes; the proportions change; the stone goes pale and seamless and loses its tool-marks; and the party stood at that join — @[Corvyn](corvyn) crouched and ran his thumb along it, old work meeting older the way a ship's deck meets the sea — and understood what they were looking at, because they had been living inside its sibling for a week. The same hand. The same unattributed elder hand that raised @[Fundament](fundament)'s bones had built here first — or last; or always — and the dwarves of Delzoun, coming upon it in whatever dawn of their kingdom, had done exactly what the Consensus would do ten thousand years later, and what the swallows do in cathedrals: they had built around the thing they could not explain, and kept it, and asked it no questions in writing. And the last word on the subject was written in the ground itself, for anyone who read ground the way @[Tirig](tirig) reads it: the deep glassy bores of the thing that had hunted them — some fresh, most older than kingdoms — webbed the whole dwarven city underfoot; and every one of them, every single one, arriving at the pale seamless paving, turned. Seventeen centuries of blind hunger with a nose for power, circling the greatest concentration of the old work left standing in the North — and it had never once crossed the join.

At the plaza's center, in a shallow round basin of the pale stone like the socket of a vanished hill, stood @[the Mooring](mooring-stones). The party had prepared themselves on the ride for a monument, and it is not a monument; that was the first correction the elder work administered. It is a crystal — a standing crystal the height of a watchtower, rooted in the basin and rising in one clean unfaceted sweep, the pale warm not-quite-color of dawn through ice — and it is @[kin](the-heart-of-winters-veil), and every one of them knew it at fifty paces, to a small cold stone that had once hung at a huntress's throat and leaned north in a closed fist. @[Tirig](tirig) stopped walking. The party stopped with her. “It is the same,” she said, very quietly, with her hand at her bare throat. “Grown great, or mine was grown small — but it is the same stone.” And as they came on into the basin, they heard it: a hum below hearing, a fullness in the sternum rather than the ear, patient and continuous and — there was a long argument later about the right word, and the right word won — kind; and the air of the whole plaza was gentler than desert air has any business being, the heat eased, the light steadied, the way the air is different in a house where people love each other. The instruments came out of the oiled case. The party did not need them. The stone was holding. You could feel it holding. It had been holding, alone, unthanked, in an empty city at the edge of an empty desert, for longer than the desert had been a desert, and something in every one of the seven — they compared accounts that night and the accounts agree — wanted, absurdly, before anything else, to tell it that they were there, and that it could lean on someone for an hour.

The readings, once taken, said in numbers what the party could already feel at the edges of the plaza's gentleness: a labor grown too heavy. The @[Seeker](revelation)'s instruments drew the harmonics as a long steady line with a tremor in it — a shear, arriving in slow waves out of the west, out of the seam; and @[Aelendir](aelendir), cross-checking the charts against the tremor's timing with the concentration he once spent on courts and contracts, laid the conclusion out flat for the fire that evening, because it deserved plain words. The Mooring was not sick. The Mooring was overworked. Every crossing at the seam, every ferry, every intake, every ton of the righted city's new timber, hangs its weight from the old agreement between the far anchors — and the far anchors are fewer than they were, because under @[Triboar](triboar) one of their number was corrupted into a winch and then broken, and at a town called @[Nesmé](nesme) another working died mid-pull, and the party themselves, in the arithmetic's cold honesty, had removed fouled anchors without replacing true ones, because there was nothing else to be done; and now Ascore held what the missing held, alone, and the enemy's city grew heavier every week. “They are burning the beam that carries their own roof,” Aelendir said. “And the beam is the last honest thing in the house.”

How do you maintain a stone that holds? Not the way the Sharrans fed theirs; every instinct in the party arrived at that door and refused it together. They walked the basin's rim through the afternoon with the question, and it was @[Amyl](amyl) — of course it was Amyl, who reads sickness with his hands and once ended a battle by walking toward the thing everyone else was fleeing — who found the grammar of it. The stone was not empty, he said, palms flat against the warm pale sweep of it, eyes shut. It was hungry the way the tired are hungry, not the way the starving are; it did not need to take anything, because taking was the one thing it had never once done; it needed — and here he stood a long time, listening through his hands — to be given. “It holds feeling,” he said. “That is what it is. That is what they all were, before the copies. It has been holding the same feeling for ten thousand years, and spending it, and nobody has ever put anything back in.”

And @[Corvyn Stonemason](corvyn)'s-son, who had answered this exact question once before in a gray glade with a borrowed talent, stepped down into the basin with a wooden box in his hands and answered it again with nothing borrowed at all. He tuned for a long time. He was afraid — he said so, plainly, that night: afraid the way you are afraid the first time you cut stone with your own arm and no master's hand over yours. And then he set his feet the way masons set their feet, and played @[the tune](the-tune) — their tune, the rope-line, the fellowship's song — alone, honestly, imperfectly, three wrong notes and all of them kept; and the party, without anyone deciding it, came down into the basin around him and carried the melody the way they have carried it across four countries and two deaths — @[Aelendir](aelendir)'s voice low under it, @[Saeron](saeron)'s palm keeping time against his knee, @[Tirig](tirig) humming with her hand flat on the crystal, @[Sov](sov)'s breath falling into the meter the way it did in an aisle of glass — and the Mooring drank. There is no other verb. The hum below hearing rose to meet the tune and settled into it, chord finding chord; the warmth of the plaza deepened by one degree, like a house when the fire is fed; and on the Seeker's unattended instruments, jittering on their tripod at the basin's rim, the long line's tremor eased, and eased, and lay down. Not healed. Fed. The shear would return with the seam's next burdens; the arithmetic had not changed; but the stone had been given, for the first time in ten thousand years — given freely, by seven people and a wooden box, the one currency it was built to hold — and every account of that hour agrees on the strangest particular: that when the last note ended, the plaza was not silent. The stone held the tune. Faintly, below hearing, in the fullness of the sternum where its voice lives, it was still playing — is, for all this book knows, still playing — the way stone holds warmth after sunset: because it was given, and what is given, it keeps.

@[Tirig](tirig) stayed at the crystal after the others climbed out of the basin, and the party let her have it. She stood a long time with her hand flat where she had hummed, her bare throat, her thefts and her winters and her socket in a mountain wall all standing around her in the failing light; and the instruments at the rim — @[Aelendir](aelendir) was repacking them, and swears to this, and no one doubts him — swung their needles gently after her when she finally walked away, and settled only when she had left the basin, the way a compass settles after iron passes. She said nothing about it. She has said nothing about it since. It goes in this book because everything true goes in this book, and because some chapters are owed their questions a long time before they are owed their answers.

They rode back the next day with the sun on their right shoulders and the argument in their baggage, and the argument weighed more than the cart. @[Aelendir](aelendir) gave it its final shape at the last camp, quietly, to the crackling driftwood: the originals hold. They hold by being given feeling and keeping it faithfully, at their own cost, for ten thousand years — and the copies take; the whole black art of the enemy, from a cave under Triboar to a mirror under Nesmé to whatever waits at the seam's own heart, is the elder work photographed in the dark: stones that were built to be fed, rebuilt by people who only knew how to feed on. “Their entire city,” he said, “stands on a stone that holds by giving. And their entire method is stones that take. That is not an irony. That is the argument — the whole of it, the one we could not find a shape for in any court or session — and it was standing in a desert the whole time, and they sent us to it themselves, and we are carrying it home.” The fire cracked. Nobody disagreed. Far to the west, past the world's rim, the doubled glow of the seam stood low on the horizon like a second dusk that would not finish, and the party rolled into their blankets under the one true sky and slept their last easy sleep for a long count of chapters.

The bridge took them back over the nothing in the morning, their single shadows failing by degrees into pairs; and @[Fundament](fundament) received them the way the city receives everything, with lamplight and manners and a ledger already amended; and that evening, before the captain's house and the supper and everything the supper would begin, the party went down to the harbor quarter and followed a laugh. It was not hard to find. It came through the doorway of a low tavern on the water — an honest loud room full of ferrymen and net-menders — riding over the noise of the place from behind the bar, where a broad half-orc woman with her sleeves rolled was setting down four tankards with one hand and running the room with the other, and the party stood in the doorway unnoticed for the length of a breath, watching @[Mishka of the Northshield](mishka) be entirely, unbearably alive. It was @[Amyl](amyl) she saw first. The tankard she was holding did not drop; she set it down — landlady to the last — with terrible care, both hands, watching them the whole time the way you watch a thing that will vanish if you blink; and then she came around the bar like weather, and the room's noise parted for her, and she reached them, and stopped, one pace short, and looked at all seven, one at a time, counting, her chin beginning to go. “You got out,” she said. Not a question; a verdict she had been appealing for a season. “They told us — the fire took — we said your names into the basin. All seven. I paid for the candles.” And then the pace closed and the party disappeared into her arms in twos and threes, and the tavern, understanding that something was happening, cheered without knowing what for, because it is that kind of room, and she is that kind of landlady, and this city, whatever else is true of it, has that kind of night in it.

She fed them, of course. She sat them at the family table by the kitchen door and fed them off the menu and off it again, and stood over them with a cloth on her shoulder demanding the road — and they gave her the true shape of it with the names of countries sanded off, and she took the sanding in stride, because a landlady knows a curated story when she pours for one and lets it keep its dignity. And in return, unasked, between the stew and the honey-cakes, she gave them her own arrival, plainly, the way she does everything. “I'll tell you how it was, since nobody here likes to say it. I woke up on a quay with a blanket on me and a stranger saying saved, and I did not believe a word of it. Not the fire, not the rescue, not the kindness — kindness is free the first week anywhere. I kept my back to walls. I counted knives. I asked for the Northshield and watched every face that answered.” She wiped the table once, out of habit, and looked out over her loud warm room. “I didn't believe a word of it for a month. Then the month ended.” A shrug, enormous and simple. “The work was real. The pay was real. The people stayed kind after it stopped being free. A town's a town, loves. I have buried one and I am not in a hurry to bury another. So I keep the taps and I say the responses at the shrine and I don't pick at the seams of it.” And then, quieter, with her eyes still on the room: “Most days.”

They walked home along the harbor with the ferries' paired lamps going out across the fade, and nobody spent the evening's last coin of talk, because tomorrow was the captain's house, and every one of them could feel the shape of the days beginning to close, the way you feel weather before the glass falls. They had done the enemy's maintenance, and it was the truest work they had done all year. They had fed the last honest stone in the enemy's arithmetic, and learned the argument that unmakes the whole ledger. They had watched a woman they mourned pour for a room she loved in a city built on her stolen grief, and heard her say most days, and pocketed it beside an advance list and a bowed-to chair. The city glowed above them in both its lights, beautiful, diligent, working late. And out past the eastern rampart, a day's ride into the honest dark, a stone that keeps by giving stood alone in its drifted plaza under the one true sky, still faintly playing their song.
