# XVII. The Sick Wood
*In which a prisoner sets the pace, and a forest declines to be well*

@audio: heartsilver-eve fade=4
The last morning at the Heartsilver house was a heavy-plated, quiet affair. Nobody spoke of the crypt, and everybody spoke around it; @[Sov](sov) came to table with a sword slung beside the mace for the first time, and nobody asked, which was its own kind of embrace. The business of the day assembled itself over the bread: the rite-day had come — the seventh — the wardens' column would form at the western gate by midmorning, and @[Amyl](amyl)'s hearing waited under the boughs a week's road west. The party had spent the seven days becoming, by the standards of any city law-house, alarmingly literate in circle-law; Theron had drilled them on the oath-friends' conduct like a tutor before examinations — stand when the eldest stands, speak only if spoken to by the court, and vouch for nothing you have not seen — and Lady Mariel sent them off with traveling fare, two letters of character, and instructions to return victorious or not at all, delivered in a tone that made it genuinely unclear whether she was joking.

They left @[Silverymoon](silverymoon) on the seventh day by the western gate, and the city gave them a strange present at parting: the Moonbridge held steady all morning, bright and unflickering, as if a patient had rallied to see visitors off. Nobody said what everyone thought, which was that the bridge had begun to have good days and bad days, and that this is a phrase you use about the dying.

The oath-friends rite was sworn at the gate itself, because circle law is portable and Theron Veil had done his work. Companions of an accused, he had found in a book nobody had opened for a century, may stand at a rite-day as oath-friends, if vouched for by a member of the circles in standing — and it was Neth who stepped forward to vouch, chin level, before her seniors' raised brows. “On my standing,” she said. “Such as it is.” They swore one by one on a living branch carried for the purpose — the same oath @[Amyl](amyl) had sworn, sized smaller — and the eldest warden watched the seven of them bind themselves to a heretic's trial with an expression that had stopped being surprise somewhere around Silverymoon and was becoming something more dangerous, which is interest.

Theron stayed — his war was the bridge, and there was a black corner of altar-stone on his workbench that had begun, he reported with a scholar's terrible cheerfulness, to hum at moonrise. He saw them to the gate anyway, and shook every hand, and got the composition of the company wrong in his farewell by one — counting Neth — and nobody corrected him, least of all Neth.

---

The road ran west and then south of west, three days through river farms and open moor with the Rauvin dwindling behind. The column moved at the wardens' pace, which was @[Amyl](amyl)'s pace, which was unhurried — a prisoner sets the pace when the prisoner is the point — and the big man used the hours the way he used all hours: mending a wheel at a farmstead they watered at, splinting a shepherd's dog, apologizing to each in turn. By the second evening two of his escort had stopped pretending not to help him. The feathers came west too. One waited on a milestone that first dusk, laid straight as ever, and @[Sov](sov) — quieter since the crypt in a way the others were still learning the borders of — picked it up in front of everyone, turned it once, and pocketed it without a word, which from him was a proclamation.

On the fourth morning the horizon grew a shadow that did not resolve into hills, and by noon they understood that they were looking at the High Forest: a wood the size of a realm, older than every crown in the North, running unbroken from here to legend. The circles are its only law — and it is worth saying what the circles are, because no city person ever quite believes it. They are not a church, though they keep rites; not a nation, though they keep borders; they are an agreement, older than either, between people and places — and their seniors, who answer to nothing with walls, were assembling now from every green country in the North to sit judgment under the oldest thing alive.

Under the eaves the light went green and swimming and the sound of the open world shut off behind them like a door. The wardens' posture changed at the threshold — reverence, the party thought at first, until they learned to read it better. It was reverence the way a nurse's gentleness is gentleness: laid over worry, and practiced at hiding it.

The sickness introduced itself gradually, which was somehow worse. First small things: sap running black at old axe-scars; birdsong thinning to the wrong kind of quiet. Then, on the second forest day, a grove the column detoured around without a word of explanation, and through the trunks the party saw why: trees bare-branched in high summer, their bark veined in black — and the veins ran downward, rootward, every one, as if the trees were not dying of something but being drunk by something. “It was not like this in spring,” Neth said, once, and then did not talk for a while. And somewhere in that second day @[Amyl](amyl) stopped apologizing and started studying — kneeling at root-collars, smelling bark, pressing a palm flat to soil with the absorbed, professional attention of a physician reading a pulse — and his escort found that this bothered them far more than fear would have.

---

The paths began to braid together, and the braid filled with the circles' folk: moor-shepherds with crooks and dogs; mountain-speakers smelling of goats and juniper; a sea-circle woman with kelp dried into her braid; old men and young women in homespun the colors of every land they kept. All of them going the same way. Most of them finding somewhere else to look when the column passed — and some not: warding signs half-hidden behind backs, an old man spitting carefully leftward, a mother turning her child by the shoulder. The accused walked through it all bareheaded and unbound, wearing the looks the way a big man learns to wear rain, and Neth's station drifted, mile by mile, until she was walking at his shoulder with her staff-butt striking the ground rather harder than the path required.

The last day was spent among giants — trunks the girth of towers, in the deeps the circles themselves call the Elder Forest — and the party spent it making the same mistake every pilgrim makes: they kept taking the @[Grandfather Tree](the-grandfather-tree) for a hill. The horizon had been the Tree for a full hour before understanding arrived, all at once, with a sensation several of them later compared to a dropped stomach: the hill was a trunk. The wooded ridgelines were roots. The green sky beyond, with its own weather moving in it, was canopy. They came in under it at dusk, and even the wardens' bare feet seemed to apologize for each step, and nobody spoke above the hush, because the Tree enforced nothing and required nothing and the silence happened anyway — the natural courtesy owed to something that was already old when the gods last rearranged themselves.

The circles' trial-ground lay in a bowl of root-ridges on the Tree's south side, camps ringing it like settlements around a harbor. And even here, at the heart of everything the circles kept, the wound had arrived first: one great root, broad as a road, ran blackened along its crown for half a mile — a burned vein in the oldest arm in the world — and every camp in the bowl was pitched facing away from it, which is how you know, among woods-folk, that a thing is not merely feared but grieved.

It was in the last light, crossing the bowl to their appointed fires, that they passed the warded grove. A ring of hazel hurdles, knotted cords at every gap, and wardens at its gate who stood without leaning on their staffs — and @[Amyl](amyl) stopped mid-stride, the way a man stops on hearing his own name across a crowd of strangers. He did not ask what was kept there. Nobody told him. He knew it the way you know a hand laid on your shoulder in the dark, and the party watched the knowledge go through him and settle, and take something with it.

They had brought his master to the trial. Or — the thought arrived with a root's patience and could not afterward be dug out — the trial had been brought to his master. @[Amyl](amyl) sat up late that night by a small lamp, not reading, hands quiet in his lap; and for once in his gentle life he did not apologize for the light.
