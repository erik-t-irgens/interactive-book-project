# XIV. Seven Days
*In which a garden thrives, a library keeps its silences, and someone watches*

@audio: moongleam-arch fade=8
It began with a petition. On the first evening of the wait the party walked out to the grove-hall — a ring of standing oaks beyond the east walls, older than the walls, where the circles kept their law when their law came to the city — and asked audience of the eldest warden, and made their case for a different keeping. They made it each in their own register. @[Corvyn](corvyn) made it beautifully: seven days of a good man's company was evidence no tribunal should deny itself. @[Tirig](tirig) made it bluntly: “Your oaks can hold him. They cannot teach him what he is. We might.” And @[Saeron](saeron), who had said nothing the whole audience, made it last and quietest, and it was the one that landed: “You are trying a man for what is in him. Let the court see what he does with it when he is free to choose. That is the only testimony that means anything.”

The eldest warden heard it all with her weathered stillness, and it was @[Neth](neth) who tipped the scale — stepping forward out of her own rank, uninvited, to volunteer the watch: every hour of it, her staff and her standing, her name on his conduct. Her seniors looked at her the way institutions look at their young. The eldest looked at her rather longer, and at Amyl, and at the seven sworn oath-friends crowding her grove like a hedge, and said she would give her answer in the morning.

The circles' mercy, when it came, wore oak leaves. On the second morning the eldest warden stood in Lady Mariel's forecourt and read the tribunal's terms aloud: the accused would not be held at the grove-hall after all, but paroled to the city — bound by an oath sworn on a living branch, the walls of Silverymoon his cell, his own word the lock — on the condition that a warden of the circles shadowed him every hour of it. @[Amyl](amyl) swore the oath with both hands on the branch and thanked them so sincerely that the eldest warden, a woman weathered past surprise, blinked twice.

The shadow they assigned him was the youngest of his escort — the one who had trained a summer under his old master, worlds ago — and she took the duty with the fierce correctness of the junior handed something important. Neth, she gave eventually, when @[Amyl](amyl) asked her name, in the tone of someone unused to being asked. She walked three paces behind him for one full day. By the second she walked beside him. By the fourth she was arguing with him about soil, and losing, and bringing cuttings to the argument, and Lady Mariel's neglected kitchen beds — which Amyl had adopted within an hour of his parole, on the grounds that a garden in that state was the true crime under discussion — had begun, scandalously, to thrive.

What he did not do was speak of the trial — except once, to @[Saeron](saeron), in Mariel's garden at dusk, with a small dried mushroom cap turning over and over in his enormous fingers. The party knew that cap. It had come out of a rat-hole under Triboar, off the walls of a chamber where a @[buried vastness](the-quiet-thing) had brushed against three minds and left the doors ajar — and @[Amyl](amyl) had carried it every day since, the way other men carry a coin or a saint's medal. “They are not wrong that something is in me,” he said. “It reached me, under the town. And I reached back — that is the part they would burn, and it is also the truest thing I have ever done. @[Corvyn](corvyn), too; it touched him the same night, though he wears it more quietly.” The cap went still in his fingers. “They are only wrong about its name. I should like to know what I am before they tell me.”

@[The feathers](the-raven-queen) kept school through all of it. One waited at a graveside where an old woman read her dead husband's letters aloud — only the openings, my dear, my dearest, my own — because, she said, he had never once managed a decent ending and she wasn't going to embarrass him now. One lay on the rail of a vigil where a tired priest spoke the names of the year's unclaimed dead into the church's great empty hall — the nave — every name, unhurried, as if the emptiness of the room were a slander the speaking refuted. Each site was a death kept well. Each feather lay straight as a tutor's chalk-tick beside the lesson, and @[Sov](sov) had stopped pretending he was not enrolled.

And while the others mined law, @[Sov](sov) drifted — the way he had drifted through Ormath's cemeteries as a boy — to the Vault's funerary wing — the North buries more than it philosophizes, an archivist told him, with the shrug of a man quoting his own epigram — and Sov spent two afternoons in it hunting the owner of the feathers, and came away with scraps that were somehow worse than nothing. A folio of northern grave-customs that mentioned, in passing, a @[Queen of ravens](the-raven-queen) whom scholars could not agree existed. An account of Uthgardt sky-burials where black feathers marked the dead the spirits had accepted. A monograph on psychopomps — the escorts of souls, the ferrymen of every faith — whose final essay, the one addressing the raven cults directly, ended mid-page: the concluding leaves torn out, cleanly, long ago, and never recopied. He stood a while over the torn edge. It was becoming a familiar shape of absence.

The @[Doomguide](the-doomguides)s received him more warmly than his history deserved. They are Kelemvor's order — priests of the Judge of the Dead, whose whole discipline is the honest death: ease the dying, settle the estate, walk the soul to fair judgment, and suffer no undeath and no lies about endings — and @[Sov](sov) knew their liturgy the way you know a childhood house, because a Doomguide named Thuin had half-raised him in the cemeteries of Ormath, teaching a strange-eyed boy the rites of passage and the balance they serve. Their Silverymoon chapel kept the finest acoustics in the city and the plainest altar, and Sov went twice, telling himself it was for the quiet. The second time, an old priest with hands like driftwood looked past the workmanlike mace at Sov's hip entirely — and let his gaze rest, a breath too long, on the wrapped length slung across the visitor's back: the blade Sov owned and never wore, carried through every mile of this book bundled like a debt. The old man studied it the way an archivist studies a book missing from his own shelves, and the seconds landed like coins on a scale, because the blade was @[Bayne](bayne), and Bayne had come out of a Kelemvorite reliquary in Ormath in a sack, at night, in these very hands. The priest said only: “The dead trust some men on sight. It is not common, and it is not comfortable, and the church has uses for it. Doors open, should you ever knock.” No accusation. An offer — which is to say, one more claim, laid politely on the pile of parties who wanted Sov Thimron for something, and the pile had begun to require both his pockets.

It was on the fifth evening that the watching began — or that he began to be able to feel it, which is not the same thing, and the difference kept him up. A crow sat the finial above the chapel lane for one full hour without shifting its feet, and was not there when the bells rang, and no one had seen it leave. A figure occupied the high gallery of a bathhouse across from Mariel's gates, at the one angle that saw her garden door; when @[Tirig](tirig) took the long way around to the gallery stair — climbing the back of the building to come up behind whoever kept that post — she found nothing but cold air and a smell like snuffed candles. Nothing was taken. Nothing was left. And it was @[Saeron](saeron) who put the useful fact on the table, in his spare way: in all the watching, no one had once seen the crow and the figure at the same time.

The others closed ranks around Sov without discussing it — nobody said guard, nobody said danger; they simply arranged themselves so that he was never alone on a street again — and their week ran on underneath, much of it back up the Vault's western rise, because Amyl's defense meant law, and law meant the stacks. They went in force, Theron herding them like an academic sheepdog through a literature nobody in the party had believed existed: circle-law, recorded on bark-paper in hands that plainly resented the invention of writing. It was there, in a book unopened for a century, that Theron surfaced with treasure — the accused's companions may attend a rite-day as oath-friends, if vouched for by a member in standing — and @[Corvyn](corvyn) and @[Aelendir](aelendir) spent Mariel's salons like coin toward the same end, until by the fifth day there were letters: introductions, characters, one from a white-haired archdruid's cousin that the Lady extracted over cards with the ruthlessness of a woman playing for something other than money. @[Iratius](iratius), between library shifts he attended under protest, mapped the purveyor's circuit by roadhouse and rumor while @[Volcaris](volcaris) swung its warmth like a weathervane in an argument. @[Tirig](tirig) charted the @[crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil)'s lean from the roof of the guest wing with a sailor's patience — not at the Spine, she reported; at something in the Spine, and the something did not move. And @[Saeron](saeron) said little, that week; but the folded page from the amphitheater came out each evening and was read again, and refolded smaller, as if he could press it below the threshold of being true.

One more pattern surfaced in @[Silverymoon](silverymoon), and the record files it beside the cords and the folded page: @[Saeron](saeron)'s wages evaporated. Reliably, entirely, within a day of payment — Lady Mariel's stipend, the Vault's small per-diem, a purse won fairly at cards he had not appeared to be trying to win — gone by the following sunset, every time, with nothing to show. It was @[Corvyn](corvyn), professionally offended by unexplained cash flow, who finally solved it, and then wished he had not been so pleased with himself: the infirmary by the south gate, it turned out, knew Saeron by name. So did the almshouse behind the Doomguide chapel. He had been in the city seven days.
