# XI. Moongleam
*In which the counting ends, a leash comes off, and the roads are chosen*

@audio: moongleam-arch fade=8
@[Moongleam Tower](moongleam) had stood over Everlund for a long time, and its marble had received many strange arrivals, but the @[Harpers](the-harpers) who kept it would say afterward that it had never received a sadder one. Some forty came through the arch on Harvestide night, scorched and silvered and carrying children who would not put their arms down from around necks. The tower did what towers cannot and made room.

For the party themselves, the arrival had the quality of a conjuring trick played on the body. One stride: a burning autumn night, glass rising in the streets of home. The next: cold marble, harp-quiet, and the smell of beeswax and paper — the private hall of Moongleam Tower, seat of the Harpers in Everlund, a city none of them had ever entered and were now standing in the middle of, a hundred miles and more south-east of Triboar as the raven flies. The portal at the top of @[Darathra](darathra)'s tower had been there, it emerged, all along; the Lord Protector's last secret turned out to be a door. From the tower's high gallery you could see the Rauvin valley silvered under stars, and the dark line of the Evermoor Way running back north-west toward everything they had just lost — a distance it would have cost a hard week to walk, crossed in the time it takes to fall through a doorway.

The Harpers themselves received the catastrophe the way their order receives most things: with music stands pushed aside, kettles on, and a fierce, practiced gentleness. Quiet folk in traveling clothes came and went at all hours; instruments hung on the walls beside message-tubes and map-cases; small gray birds arrived at the window-slots through the night, were read, and were sent out again. It was a house built for exactly this, and the party, who had not slept in what felt like a geological age, were fed, warmed, and firmly put to bed.

The days after were lists. Names taken at the door, names matched against the wagon-trains arriving from the west by twos and threes, names that stayed unmatched and were moved, gently, to a different page. The arithmetic of the saved is a crueler business than anyone admits: every name confirmed alive rang like a bell, and every bell made the silence after it more particular. The party learned the number of the living the way you learn a language — a word at a time, with mistakes.

Somewhere in the counting, a name surfaced that had no page to go to. @[Darz Helgar](darz) had walked into Gwaeron's Rest before the end — walked in and not out, as far as any list ran, west-wagon or arch. The party asked; the Harpers wrote it down with everything else; and the wood, which keeps its own census, said nothing. It sat wrong with all of them, in the low-grade way of unfinished things, and it would go on sitting wrong for a while.

@[Gregory](gregory) was not on any list, because Gregory had come through the arch with them — carried, sleeping, and sleeping still: the deep, walled-off sleep of a boy whose mind had spent a night as a battlefield. The Harpers gave him a small room with a west window and took turns sitting with him, and @[Aevren](aevren)'s people sent word east along their lines, to a wagon on the far roads, that a son was alive in Everlund and would follow when he could travel. He woke rarely, ate obediently, said almost nothing — and once, asked whether the @[big quiet thing](the-quiet-thing) was all right now, and accepted “yes” the way children accept only true answers, and slept again.

Moongleam's dawns, the party discovered that first tenday, belonged to @[Saeron](saeron). He was up before the tower's own bells, every day, at the same east window — and every day, the same small ritual: the crimson cords came off wrist and waist, were drawn once through his hands, and went back on, re-tied, thirteen knots, no more, no fewer, with the unhurried exactness of a man winding a clock that keeps something other than time. He never explained it. Nobody asked — the party had learned which of one another's doors were locked — but the book sets it down here, in the accounting chapter, because it belongs in every accounting of him. And among the hurt of the ragged forty he could be found at the low beds in the evenings, one at a time, saying words over them — brief, level words in a language of endurance that nobody asked him to translate — and the hurt, it was noticed, slept better on the nights he came.

And one mercy came east to meet all the westward grief: a Harper rider's report from the Evermoor road. A tiefling family — three of them, the only other tieflings Triboar had ever held — out and away before the ash, wheel-ruts eastward, unpanicked, provisioned. Gregory's people, gone ahead of the end and traveling well. The party chose to read “traveling” as “alive,” and for once the reading held.

And two names came off the worry-list the same week, because somebody in the fall's last hour had opened the cells. The order was never traced, and the book suspects it never needed tracing — it had the shape of a Lord Protector about it — but the fact stood: the Tower's prisoners had gone out with the retreat, and among the ragged forty at Moongleam were Tovric Hale, jaw still locked around the name he could not say — the working, it turned out, had outlived the worker — and Lethis Dorne, thinner and quieter and alive. Tovric gave the Harpers his testimony the only way left to him: a splinter, a tabletop, and two letters pressed slow and deep enough to be read by touch. @[M.T.](merris) The party could put a face and a smile to the initials, and did, and Aevren wrote the name into her files in full, in ink, where workings do not reach.

They worked, because working was the only room with the lights on. @[Corvyn](corvyn) played the small chambers where the rescued slept, tunes with no cleverness in them, the musical equivalent of a hand on a shoulder. @[Saeron](saeron) sat with the ones who wept and the ones who worried him more, the ones who didn't. @[Tirig](tirig) ran the wall-walks at dawn until the cold outpaced whatever was chasing her. @[Sov](sov) wrote letters for people who did not know where to send them; the Harpers have a term for such mail, and a drawer for it: to be held.

@[Aevren](aevren) came up the stair on the fourth day, still in her road dust. She had left Triboar when she left Hyuth's manor — Harper business pulling her west through the gate in the small hours after the masquerade, one single day ahead of the end of the world — and that margin was the whole story of why she was alive, and she plainly knew it, and had plainly decided not to discuss it. She asked one question — the @[ledger](the-silent-ledger)? — and when the black book was put into her hands she weighed it like a founder weighing ore. “Coded,” she said, turning three pages. “Properly. This wants a key and a while. Keys I have. Whiles I make.” Then, softer, to no one: “You beautiful, careful monsters.”

Her first question after the ledger was @[Darathra](darathra). It was not a Harper's question; the two women had traded a decade of dry letters and dryer favors, and Aevren asked it looking at the map table rather than at anyone's face, which is how you ask when you already know the shape of the answer. They told her what they had seen: the Tower emptied, the streets given over, and the Lord Protector, at the end, going down into the rising glass like a woman going down to answer her own door. Aevren was quiet for the length of one full breath. “Of course she did,” she said at last. And that was the eulogy, and it was a good one, and nobody has improved on it since.

She had brought dispatches, too, and read them aloud over the map table because Harpers believe information is a form of food and should be shared at meals. Two mattered. The first, in silver ink from a colleague in the Silver Marches, reported that the @[Moonbridge](the-moonbridge) of @[Silverymoon](silverymoon) — the arc of solid moonlight that has crossed the Rauvin for as long as the city has had a name — had flickered twice in a month, and that the city's official position was that this was normal, seasonal, and fine. The second dispatch was greener and stranger: the druid circles of the North were convulsing. Every grove from the High Forest to the Coldwood had felt Harvestide like a wound in the world's side, and the circles were counting their own. At the center of the convulsion stood the center of their world: the @[Grandfather Tree](the-grandfather-tree) — the oldest living thing on any map the Harpers keep, a tree like a hill with a canopy like a county, mother-grove to every circle of the North, far west in the deeps of the High Forest — and beneath its boughs, the dispatch said, the seniors of the circles were assembling, and asking hard questions of anyone whose craft touched the gray edges of the natural order. Aevren read that one twice. Nobody around the table understood yet that it was aimed at them.

Below, in a warded room with good light and no velvet anywhere, @[Hyuth Kolstaag](hyuth) came unbound. It happened on the sixth morning, without ceremony: whatever stitching had held his tongue for a season rotted through — perhaps because the town it had been sized to no longer stood on this side of anything, perhaps because such contracts have terms of completion, and the work was complete. He asked for parchment. He wrote, in a hand that shook, I can say it now. And then he wept, at length, because there was so much less to say than he had hoped.

What he could give, he gave, and it was worse for being concrete. How a mark was chosen: a man already halfway to yes — debts, a sick wife, a warehouse one bad season from the bank — that was the whole science of it. Then the manufacture of gratitude: a loan called in early by some stranger, and Hyuth arriving, distressed on the mark's behalf, to pay it quiet; introductions to better custom; a daughter's apprenticeship smoothed. Three favors given before one small thing was ever asked. Then assurance — his word, their word — protection, formalized: a modest sum against fire, theft, spoilage, the misfortunes of a hard country, and the mark never once suspecting that the misfortunes were on retainer too. And then, only then, the paper: terms of service dressed as terms of friendship, and the door closing politely behind. It was a manual, and the party memorized it, because manuals describe the future as reliably as the past.

But when he reached for the principal — the face at the top of his ruin, the voice on the far side of the broker — the name came to the door of his teeth and would not cross. Not fear. He was past fear. Clause. Somewhere a contract held that one word the way glass holds a fly, and Hyuth beat on it from the inside, and it did not care.

What to do with him occupied one whole supper and divided the table honestly. Some argued the road: hand him to whatever justice Everlund kept for men whose crimes had been annexed by a catastrophe. @[Iratius](iratius) argued arithmetic, briefly and coldly, and was talked around by @[Saeron](saeron) with four sentences nobody wrote down. In the end it was @[Aevren](aevren) who settled it: @[Hyuth](hyuth) would stay at Moongleam, in Harper keeping — witness, asset, and, she observed, given the Clause in his mouth, possibly a loose end his old master might one day come to trim. “He is safer as our guest than as his master's unfinished business,” she said. “And more useful, and considerably more miserable, and I find I can live with all three.” It was not a resolution so much as a shelving, and everyone at the table knew it. The book will find him again.

On the seventh day they spread the map and laid their pulls on it like evidence, and the pulls stood up all at once, like iron filings when the magnet moves. North: a @[crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil) that leaned in a closed fist toward the Spine, patient as a sleeping pulse. South and east, or east, or nowhere: a @[sword](volcaris) whose warmth would not hold a bearing, as if the thing it wanted kept moving — and a fiend's parting promise, delivered smiling at a masquerade, that more than one of its targets remained. West: the green dispatch, the convulsing circles, the @[Grandfather Tree](the-grandfather-tree). Near — two days up the Rauvin — the Gem of the North, with a flickering bridge, a scholar's card that said find me, and an invitation in a silver hand. A folded page riding in a monk's robes, describing a town being prepared — though only one man at the table knew what it rhymed with, and he was not talking. And on a certain windowsill, on the first morning, where no bird had been seen to land: a single black feather, laid straight, as though placed.

The invitation had come from the Lady Mariel Heartsilver of Silverymoon — patroness of music, fixture of the northern salons, and @[Aelendir](aelendir)'s keeper-of-engagements since longer ago than most families last. It renewed, with the delicacy of an invoice, an engagement catastrophe had interrupted: at her card table at Boar's Rest, the note observed, a performance had been promised against her coin, and she had every intention of collecting, and considered the end of a town an inadequate excuse. Her six lines closed with two the party would quote at each other for a season: Music after grief is not disrespect. It is defiance, and I pay defiance well. Aelendir read it aloud in the voice of a man who had heard her say such things across several centuries and had never once won the argument that followed.

So: Silverymoon first. They chose it the way sensible people choose — because every road they might take next ran through it, because a lady had asked, because funds and standing are also weapons — and beneath all that, because a bridge was going out like a lamp, and lamps going out had lately become their business.

No one said anything about the @[black feather](the-feathers) that had appeared on a certain windowsill, laid straight as a compass needle, on their first morning in the tower. @[Sov](sov) had picked it up before the others woke, and it rode in his breast pocket the whole week, and he told no one, which was itself a kind of telling, though only the room noticed.

They left Everlund under a gray sky, the tower's arch cold at their backs and the Rauvin road unrolling ahead. Two days to Silverymoon. It rained the first day and shone the second, and afterward they would argue about which of the two had been the omen.
