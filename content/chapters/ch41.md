*In which a boy is kept, an empty stable is measured, and a song is found taken apart*

The trouble began before @[Gregory](gregory)'s eyes had finished closing, because an anomaly in a hall of procedure is a wound, and wounds draw attention. The nearest wardens were already converging — gently, correctly, with the unhurried closing-in of people whose forms have a box for everything — and a clerk was already discovering that his forms did not: no bier number, no file, no manifest line, an arrival with his eyes open in a system built on the premise that no such person can exist. Observation was proposed. The physicians' wing was mentioned, and then, more quietly, between two wardens who did not know how far a monk's hearing reaches, a different wing was mentioned. And @[Amyl](amyl) stood up from the floor with the sleeping boy in his arms and said, to the hall at large, in the voice he keeps for tribunals: “He is ours. Kin, of the North, of the same town as nine hundred and forty of tonight's saved. He sleeps under our roof.” And @[Darathra](darathra) was there by the end of the sentence — ward-captain, intake-mustered, entirely within her authority and radiating it — with the paperwork already half-composed in her mouth; and the wardens looked from the boy to the seven to the Lord Protector of nowhere, and wavered; and then the floor's own weather changed, and the @[archivist](the-archivist) was with them, and the hall held its breath to see what the small gray-eyed woman would file this as.

She looked at @[Gregory](gregory) for a long moment. That is the whole of what happened, and the party will carry it further than any threat the city ever offered them: she looked at the sleeping boy — at the road-worn boots, at the eyes moving under their lids, at the face of the one person in the history of her tender terrible apparatus to walk through its door awake — and the filed kindness went very still, and something older looked out through the gray for the space of three heartbeats, assessing, weighing, entering him somewhere; and @[Darathra Shendrel](darathra) stepped — bodily, unmistakably, with a soldier's placement — between the look and the boy. And the moment closed like water. “The household of the seven,” the @[archivist](the-archivist) said, mildly, to the clerk, exactly as she had said it once before at a customs desk; and to the party, with the small courtesy that ran everything: “The city is glad of its guests' kin. Rest him well.” And she went back to her biers; and nobody exhaled until the stair; and @[Sov](sov) said, on the climb, very quietly, “She did not ask one question,” and let the observation lie there, doing its work, the whole way up.

@[Gregory](gregory) slept a day and a night and most of a second day, and the party kept the watch in shifts they did not discuss. He slept the way he had arrived: not like a sick child; like a spent instrument — profoundly, economically, his breathing slow and deep and metered, his eyes moving always, always, under the lids, reading something at great length. @[Corvyn](corvyn) sat the longest watches and hummed the three wrong notes at the cot the way he had hummed them over a town, and swears the metered breathing kept his time. And on the second evening, between one breath and the next, with no flutter of waking, Gregory's eyes were open; and he looked at the ceiling awhile, relearning the idea of ceilings; and then he said, hoarse, to the room: “Is there bread? Walking through is hungry work.” And the lodging fell on him with soup and bread and seven kinds of relief, and the boy from the rat-hole ate like a woodcutter and let himself, for one hour, be twelve.

He was not twelve. That was the thing the party understood by degrees across the soup and the days that followed, and this book will let it arrive as it arrived, in pieces. He was taller, yes; browner; but the change was not in the inches. It was in the pacing of him — the way he listened all the way to the end of every sentence before answering; the way his answers came back level and finished, like stones set rather than thrown; the way he sat with silence, comfortably, the way old men and old trees sit with it. A year of the link had run through him like a river through a valley, and the valley was the shape of the river now. And some of what the river carried had lodged. He knew things — not facts recited; knowings, that surfaced when the ground of the talk passed over them: the age of the elder stone, offered mildly while @[Aelendir](aelendir) was still framing the question; the party's own route through the gray country, sketched with one finger on the table, right, including the turning they had not taken. And once — only once, early — he answered @[Amyl](amyl). Amyl had not spoken. “It isn't hurting me,” @[Gregory](gregory) said, to the worry in the room's quietest head, “you can stop weighing it”; and heard what he had done in the silence that followed; and had the grace to look, briefly, twelve again. “You think loudly when you're worried,” he said, to the table's stares, by way of apology. “Most people do. I'm learning not to answer.”

His account of the year came out over two evenings, plainly told and full of holes he declined to decorate. Moongleam first: the Harpers kind, the small room with the west window, the tower's hum — he screwed up his face at the memory — wrong, in a way nobody there could hear but him, a machine-noise where a home-noise should be. Then the morning the calling turned. “It had been a direction the whole time. Like a stream somewhere in the woods. Then it was a going-away.” He would not stay after that; the party knew this part from a careful hand's postscript; two good cloaks to Silverymoon, and east of it, gone — following, he said, the only thing he has ever followed: the road the @[quiet thing](the-quiet-thing) was being moved along. How does one move such a thing? The party asked it the way you ask children practical questions, and got back the only sentence of the account that @[Aelendir](aelendir) wrote down that night word for word: “Slowly. At night. In pieces of attention — they make everyone who watches own a piece, so nobody ever sees all of it. You move a mountain by owning everyone who looks at it.” He followed the going-away east for a season, feeding himself — people feed you if you split wood — and came, at last, in the first hard cold, to a timber town on a river, where the going-away stopped. And waited. And he waited with it, in the woods above the town, because by then he could feel the shape of what the waiting was for.

Of @[Jalanthar](jalanthar)'s last night he said little, and the party did not press, and this book will honor all three silences — his, theirs, and its own. He was in the wood above the town. He watched the lights go out, street by street, in perfect order, like a person snuffing candles down a hall. “It was very quiet,” he said. “That's the part I'd tell the Session, if the Session asked me things instead of filing them. It hates the quiet they make it make. It's a loud thing. It's supposed to be loud.” And when the town was dark and the working was done and the door in the world stood open for the files of sleepwalkers, @[Gregory](gregory) of Triboar came down out of the wood, and walked in among his sleeping countrymen — and here the account arrived at the place the party had been waiting for, and he gave it to them simply, with his hands around the mug, in the way of a boy explaining a thing he has thought about so long it has become obvious: @[the door cuts](the-glass). That is what the door is for; that is why nobody remembers; it shears what you are carrying loose, the night of you, and keeps it somewhere in the toll. “But it carried me,” he said. “I rode inside where it keeps me — I've lived half in there since the rat-hole; there's a me-shaped place in it now. The door can't cut what it's holding. So I came through whole.” He looked around the table at the seven of them, unhurried. “It did that on purpose. It hasn't got many ways left to say things. I'm one of them.”

What was coming, he knew only the way rivers know weather. The lists were done — he said it as fact, not report: done, finished, the last town gathered; the going-away had already begun again behind him, westward now, slow as ownership; and what waited at the end of the road was the harness's real work, the thing all the small works had been rehearsal for, at — and here he frowned, translating from something without a calendar — “the dark of the year. When the night is longest. It thinks in tides and it can feel that one coming the way you feel a big wave through a boat.” He did not know what the work was. He said so plainly, twice, and the party filed the honesty as the most frightening thing in the account: the creature that had carried a boy whole through the shearing door, the oldest and largest attention any of them had ever felt, did not know what it was for. “It only knows it's @[the engine](the-quiet-thing),” @[Gregory](gregory) said. “Nobody tells an engine where the wagon is going.”

They went down again on the third night, because the boy asked to — stood up from supper and said, in the level finished voice, “I need to see where they're going to put it. It wants me to have seen” — and because @[Darathra](darathra)'s intake credentials had not yet been rescinded and the party had learned this city's first law: paperwork is a country, and they held passports. The processional stair took them down through a @[Fundament](fundament) sleeping off its gratitude, and the receiving hall at the bottom of the world lay at rest — biers folded and racked, lamps trimmed to embers, the Crossing standing dark and still at the far heart like a sea with its back turned — a cathedral between services; and their steps and their one lantern were small in it, and nobody spoke above the hush, because the hush was the almost-right hum, closer down here, everywhere down here, and the party's trained ears could not stop hearing the one wrong note under the world.

@[Gregory](gregory) led them off the nave's flank, down galleries the intake files never used, along stairs that were elder work unrepaired, trailing one hand on the stone the way he had once walked a farm at night; and the water-sound rose to meet them, and the last gallery opened out, and they stood at a rail above @[the stable](the-stable). The party had no other word for it afterward and stopped looking for one. A vault below even the cathedral, vast past lantern-reach, floored in black still water — and empty. Empty the way a thing is empty that has never yet been filled: down out of the unseeable ceiling hung the couplings, black-stone roots of an inverted tree, dozens of them, hanging dead and patient over the dark water with their sockets open; cradle-works of elder stone stood ranked along the flood like the ribs of an unbuilt ship; channels ran seaward — seamward — toward gates the lantern could not find; and everything in it, every socket and rib and channel, was sized to one tenant. The party stood at the rail and measured the emptiness the way you measure a gallows, by what it is for; and Gregory put his palm flat on the gallery stone, and left it there a while, and said, in the smallest voice the account had used yet: “This is where it will be sad.”

The door found them on the way back up, or they found it; the party disputes this gently and the book declines to rule. At the head of the stable's galleries, where the charts of the under-city showed only wall, a portal of the pale seamless stone stood shut — no handle, no seam, no keyhole — and it did not open to hands, or to iron, or to Aelendir's careful old words; and @[Corvyn](corvyn), standing before it with his lantern up, out of patience and something better than patience, hummed at it. Three notes. The wrong three, kept. And the elder stone sighed back into its own thickness, the way the basin at Ascore had taken its first color, and let them in; and the party stood on the threshold of a room the size of a temple's heart, perfectly proportioned, achingly plain — and used. Racked along one wall, in ranks, in padding, with the neatness of a beloved arsenal: @[black stones](mooring-stones), hand-high to waist-high, each humming its wrong dead hum, each one — the party knew the original now; had fed it; had heard it hold their song — a copy. Mirror-plates in slotted frames, dark and waiting, the antechamber instruments' larger kin. Braided cabling run with a craftsman's love along the ancient walls to a socket set in the floor at the room's center — empty, waiting, and sized, every one of them saw it at once, to the couplings hanging over the black water below. And a long @[worktable](the-workroom), clean as an altar, squared as a muster, with papers on it.

The papers were music. The party stood over that fact for a long time before anyone could work with it: the inmost room of everything — the working heart their whole year had been walking toward, from a cave under Triboar to a town carried through a door — and the table held no grimoire, no black book; it held @[scores](the-song-of-unraveling). Staved paper in a modern, devout, painstaking hand, page upon page, annotated in the margins in the vocabulary of field documentation and liturgy — sung thus at the threshold; the unbinding descends here; the choir holds the lattice while the operator ascends — and @[Corvyn](corvyn), who cannot read a room of powerful people but can read anything with staves on it, went down the top sheet with one finger, and stopped, and made a sound the party had never heard from him: a small wounded musician's sound. “It is @[the tune](the-tune),” he said. And then, turning pages, faster, his finger skidding the inversions: “It is the tune taken apart. Every phrase inverted. Every resolution refused. The whole of it run backward against itself, like” — he set the page down with exaggerated care, and finished the sentence anyway, because this company finishes its sentences — “like a butcher's diagram of a beloved animal. Somebody took the kindest thing I know, and arranged it to cut.” What it would cut, and from whom, and to what end, the pages did not say and the party did not know; the margins spoke of thresholds and lattices and an operator in the grammar of the devout, which is the grammar of the deliberately unclear; and the party copied what could be held in memory, and understood perhaps a tenth of it, and understood entirely that a tenth was already too much to carry quietly.

And beneath the working copies, in a folio of stiffened leather — protected, the party marked, as nothing else in that devout arsenal was protected — lay @[one sheet](aelendirs-sheet), alone. Old. Truly old: ink browned, paper gone soft as cloth at the folds and mended along every fold with a conservator's reverence. Notation, in an older convention, in an elegant unhurried hand: the tune — entire, right way round, the three notes and the whole architecture they climb toward, set down complete by someone who had once had it whole. @[Aelendir](aelendir) did not touch it for a long time. When at last he lifted it, he held it the way the ferrymen had taught them all to hold something of their own; and the party watched an old elf stand in the enemy's music room reading his own handwriting, four centuries old, out of a country whose name he lost with everything else; and what he said, he said without looking up, and the book sets it down as the hinge of the chapter: “I wrote this. I remember the ink — I ground the lampblack for it. There was a fellowship; there was a wall we raised; there was a rope in a blizzard; it is coming back to me the way a shore comes back through fog, and I do not yet know what stands on it. There was a woman. I set the song down whole because she asked me for it — for a working, she said, and I was young, and proud of the commission.” His hands were very still on the old paper. “I knew her name. I said it for years the way you say the names of people you love. I can feel the shelf where it sits in me. And it will not cross my tongue — not lost; held; like a word on the far side of glass.” He looked up then, at the racked copies, at the socket, at the butchered scores, and said the sentence the party carried up out of the depths in place of every answer they had come down for: “It is not gone from the world, children. It is kept. And someone in this city has my song, and has had it a very long time, and has been taking it apart.”

They put the room back the way they found it, to the last squared page, because Darathra's discipline had become the company's spine — you do not spend a reconnaissance on a gesture — and the cost of the withdrawal was physical and the book records it: @[Iratius](iratius) standing over the racked copies with his empty hands opening and closing on nothing; @[Tirig](tirig) at the mirror-plates with her knife-hand flat against her own thigh; @[Corvyn](corvyn) squaring the unraveled scores to the table's edge with his jaw set like a mason's line. They took nothing but what memory could hold — and one thing more, and the book will not call it theft. @[Aelendir](aelendir) folded the old sheet along its mended creases and put it inside his coat, over his heart, where @[Saeron](saeron) keeps a key; and when Tirig's eyebrow asked the tradecraft question, he answered it in four words, in the voice of four hundred years: “She stole it first.” And whether she was the woman he could not name, or the small gray-eyed woman who could not be surprised, or both, or neither, nobody said on the long climb up — though Tirig walked the last flights with her hunter's stillness on, and Aelendir walked them with his hand flat against his coat; and above them the city slept its grateful sleep, and below them the empty stable waited at the bottom of everything, sized and socketed and patient; and somewhere east of the world, slow as ownership, the engine was coming home.
