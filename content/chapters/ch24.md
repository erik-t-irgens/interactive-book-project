# XXIV. Through the Socket
*In which the party is loud in a quiet country*

@audio: bright-country fade=8
The gifts were waiting for them at the camp's edge when the too-many-colored stars gave way to a morning the color of good news. Every one of them: the white fur cloak cut to @[Saeron](saeron)'s shoulders; the red berries, fresh as the moment they were refused, steaming faintly although nothing here was cold; the gold ring in its casing of clear ice — all of it bundled in a netting of frost that had no business existing in that warm air and existed anyway, patiently, the way a signature exists. @[Gorvach](gorvach) hauled himself up on one elbow against every medical instruction in the camp, looked the bundle over with a gatekeeper's eye, and delivered the ruling that let everyone breathe: “Given. Not lent.” A pause, while he turned the frost-netting in his fingers. “Keep them. Refusing twice insults.” For what was returned, the frost wrote along the netting as it melted, at last, having outlived its errand. It counted.

Two of the gifts were not repetitions. Pressed into the netting's fold lay a cake of blue-white rime that Neth, after one touch, carried straight to @[Gorvach](gorvach)'s wound — winter's balm, she called it, for lack of anything in any herbal she had ever read — and it sank into the spear-hole like frost into turned earth and knit what it found there, and by evening the orc was standing, and by the second morning he was auditing the watch rotation. And beside where @[Sov](sov) had slept, alone, apart from the bundle, a patch of frost persisted on a warm stone through the whole of that first day, spelling itself over and over in a hand the party had last seen on a silverwood bole: Yours was the costliest. It is not forgotten. He did not tell the others what he made of it. He was not sure the making was his to do.

The ring, for the record, fit @[Iratius](iratius) — the one hand in the company that had left no gift at any fork in the white forest — sized to the finger as though the country had measured him while he glowered. Nobody said anything. He wore it after a day and a half of conspicuously not wearing it, and the record has chosen to find that funny rather than moving, because Iratius would prefer it.

---

The country was loud about them, and they learned quickly that the loudness ran both directions. This was a land built of the very currency they carried — @[Aelendir](aelendir) named it on the first evening, quietly, the way you name a sleeping bear: the @[Feywild](the-feywild) — and in the plane of feeling, the seven of them blazed like lamps in a mirror shop — the seven; Neth and Gorvach burned beside them candle-honest and human-sized, and the difference was visible to anything with eyes in that country, which was everything. The grass leaned toward them as they walked. Flowers turned to follow, unhurried, an audience finding its seats. Where @[Iratius](iratius)'s temper banked and flared, the dew browned in a ring around his boots; where @[Corvyn](corvyn) delighted — and he delighted constantly, helplessly, it was his condition — things budded out of season and showed off. And at night, faint but no longer deniable, the party could hear @[Tirig](tirig): the singing behind her sternum, audible now at an arm's length in the hush, patient as a second pulse. She took to sleeping a little apart. Nobody let her take to it far. The lesson under all of it assembled by the second day and Aelendir said it plainly, once, for the record: “In this country, being interesting is a form of shouting. We are very interesting. Walk accordingly.”

@[Gorvach](gorvach) met the Feywild the way a hammer meets fog. His grammar — literal question, literal answer — had been the party's rock through two seasons, and here the country itself declined the format. How far is home? he asked @[Aelendir](aelendir), the first walking day. “That is not a distance here,” the elf said, gently. Gorvach considered this for the better part of a mile. “Bad country,” he ruled at last, and then, with the air of a professional making the best of a posting: “Good watches, though. Everything announces itself.” He checked the gifts each morning like a quartermaster, read the ground like scripture, and treated the impossible meadows exactly as he had treated the mouth of a cave at the top of the world: as a gate someone had to keep, wherever it happened to be standing.

Time was the country's one weapon they could not watch for, and @[Aelendir](aelendir) made sure they feared it properly. Days here, he warned, are not always honored elsewhere; the Feywild keeps its own books and settles them when it pleases; travelers have danced one night and come home to their grandchildren's funerals. @[Saeron](saeron) did not say anything to that. He took out the folded page — the one with a date written in its margin, the date the whole year now leaned toward — and looked at it for a while, and put it away, and that evening the party began cutting a notch for every dawn into the haft of @[Amyl](amyl)'s staff, and counting them aloud at the fires, all together, like a prayer with arithmetic in it.

It was on one of those counted nights, on a shared watch, that @[Amyl](amyl) finally paid a debt of curiosity he had been carrying since a grove-hall. Why, he asked Neth — quietly, over the sleeping camp — had she staked her name on him, her standing, her whole young ledger, for a stranger on trial for abomination. Neth considered the too-many-colored stars for a while, in the way of a woman who answers questions properly or not at all. “Because the eldest was going to be right slowly,” she said, “and the Tree did not have the time.” Which was true, and warden-shaped, and complete. And then, some minutes later, into the dark, in the voice of a person adding a footnote they had decided the record deserved: “Also — the first morning of the survey, before anyone was watching, you apologized to the moss where the ladders had bruised it.” A pause. “Nobody had ever apologized to my moss before. I decided to see where a man like that was going.”

---

The country escorted them. Flowers followed them for most of a day in a slow rearguard nobody caught moving. A fox-shaped something with seasons for eyes fell in beside the column one morning, walked with them until dusk, accepted one of the fey berries from Neth's hand with enormous ceremony, and left — the Feywild's version, @[Aelendir](aelendir) said, of being sniffed. And once, on a high meadow, Aelendir himself stopped walking. Far off, blue with distance, a treeline stood against the stars in a shape that meant something to him, and he looked at it the way the party had learned to recognize — the way Sov looked at feathers — and said only, “I lived a life once within sight of those trees. It is one of the ones that were taken off the shelf.” He walked on before anyone could ask which shelf, or whose.

The violin led them. It had not stopped playing since the doorless hillside — soft, inside its case, patient, a sound like a memory practicing — and it played louder along one bearing and duller along the others, and @[Corvyn](corvyn) followed it like a man being reeled in on a very long line, with an expression the party had never seen on him: a performer walking toward the one audience he was afraid of. On the third evening he told them why, unprompted, which for Corvyn was a wonder in itself.

He had been eight, he said. A stonemason's yard in a town nobody sings about; a family of masons going back past counting, and one small gnome who drummed chisel-rhythms on the cut blocks and heard music in the mortar-lines, and was going to be a mason like everyone before him, because that was the shape of the world. And there had been a lady in the yard one evening — small, moth-winged, kind past sense — who had watched him drum and asked him if he wanted the thing he wanted. “I asked what it would cost,” @[Corvyn](corvyn) said, to the fire, in the voice of a man who has rehearsed the sentence for a lifetime and never once said it aloud. “She said: 'Nothing you will miss.' I was eight. Nothing you will miss.” And the morning after, his hands had known a violin the way water knows downhill, and every year since, the same question in the same eight-year-old voice, under every stage and every ovation: was any of it ever mine? Nobody at the fire answered him. The fire had learned, by now, which questions were on their way to being answered by the road.

There was a second half to the confession, and he gave it to the fire in a smaller voice. The morning his hands first knew the violin was also the morning his family's house woke up one child short. His brother — gone in the night, no track, no note, the town out searching for a tenday and the search finding what such searches find, which is winter. And an eight-year-old with brand-new impossible hands had done the arithmetic a child does in the dark and never shows anyone: nothing you will miss. He had believed, every day since, that he knew exactly what the gift had cost, and who had paid it. Every performance of his life had been dedicated — silently, under the applause, where nobody could hear the debt being serviced — to a boy whose name he did not say at the fire, because some coins you do not spend until you must. His parents had blamed him quietly, the way parents do; and forgiven him long ago, the way parents do; and @[Corvyn](corvyn) had kept his guilt anyway, polished, close, and used it as the only proof he would accept that they had not.

They crested into his remembered country on the fifth notch — he knew the ridgelines, the stream-braid, a leaning dance-stone he had once been measured against — and stopped on the rise, all of them, because the valley was wrong. Below, where the land's green ran rich in every direction, there was a hole in it: a coin of gray, exact-edged, half a mile across, like a burn in a tapestry — not dead, they could see growth in it even from the rise, but gray, the way the party had learned one particular country was gray — and at its heart, a single hollow tree. The violin, in its case, rose from playing to something with an edge of voice in it. And @[Corvyn](corvyn) stood looking down at the glade where his gift had been bought with an expression the record declines to summarize, and said, “That is her tree. It was the greenest thing I ever saw. What happened to it?”

One more thing happened on that rise, and the record sets it down because @[Corvyn](corvyn) set it down, later, himself. His mirror, seasons ago, had shown him a vision — the string, yes, humming; but behind it a wall: endless, seamless, stone-cut past mortal cleanness, runes flickering in the mortar-gaps, pulsing to a heartbeat he did not feel in his chest. All the long descent into the gray valley, some part of him kept waiting for the wall. The wall did not come. His door was opening here, in a grayed glade in his own bought history — and his vision, alone of all of theirs so far, pointed at some other place entirely, further on, still waiting. He filed the fact where he filed all his sharpest things: behind a joke he did not make.
