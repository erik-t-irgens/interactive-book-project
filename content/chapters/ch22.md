# XXII. The Owned North
*In which the North belongs to someone, and says so*

@audio: owned-north fade=8
They went north the long way, because there is no short way. From the High Forest's northern marches the land opens into the great cold shoulder of the world — moor and taiga and stone — and somewhere beyond all of it, white against white, the Spine of the World: the wall the world built against itself, mountains that end maps. It was a hard tenday of road, and the year spent it fast: autumn died somewhere along it, quietly, in the night, and the company came out of a morning fog into winter and simply kept walking.

There was a morning, early in that road, when Theron's borrowed maps put them level with a certain latitude, and everyone seemed to know it without consulting the maps at all. Somewhere east of their track, over a horizon of moor, lay the place where @[Triboar](triboar) wasn't — the roads they had walked all summer, the gates, the graves, the glass. Nobody proposed the detour. Nobody spoke for a mile or two. @[Corvyn](corvyn) hummed something low that was not the tune, and let it end unfinished, and that was the funeral they could afford at the pace the winter was setting.

The season had a name on it, up here, and the name did work on the party that none of them expected. The North was leaning toward the @[Feast of the Moon](the-feast-of-the-moon) — the old festival at winter's threshold when the North sets a table for its dead: candles in windows for the ones who are gone, names spoken over thresholds, the night when, the fireside wisdom runs, the veil between the living and the dead wears thinnest of the whole year. Villages they passed were already laying in the candles. And @[Saeron](saeron) asked, once, in a voice arranged to sound idle, exactly which night the Feast fell on this year — and wrote the answer in the margin of a certain folded page, and refolded it, and did not explain, and nobody made him.

---

The dominion introduced itself by paperwork. At the first big ford stood a way-fort so new its timber still bled sap, and on its post-board, weathered under wax, the law of the land: hunt-orders for beasts and men; levy-schedules for the clans; a schedule of licenses required of “walkers” — and every notice signed the same way, no name, no seal, a single title written out in full, in a plain hand that never hurried: —Conviction. The party stood at the board in the blowing snow and felt the grammar land. They had seen it once before, on a scrap of liturgy in a root-vault, a virtue signing itself whole. “Certitude farmed a root,” @[Corvyn](corvyn) said, quietly, reading down the levy columns. “This one annexed a horizon.”

The clans told the rest without saying a word. The road threaded their winter camps — hide-towns in the lee of hills, smoke and dogs and watchful children — and in camp after camp the same absence: the fighting-age men gone, levied south or north to garrisons; the old totems still standing but overlaid, each one, with a fresh brand burned at eye height — a @[tusk](the-hollow-tusks), hollow at the core. That mark the party knew. It had come through a smuggler's sluice at them on the last night of a town, on the shields of a warband. The Hollow Tusks had been raiders once, an enemy you could face. Somebody had promoted them. “They were a warband,” @[Iratius](iratius) said, at the third camp, in the flat voice he saved for professional assessments. “Someone has turned them into a tax.”

The tax collected them at a bridge on the eighth day: a Tusk patrol, six strong under a scarred sergeant, wanting walking-papers the party did not have and could not have known to have. It went, in the end, without blood, and afterward nobody could fully account for why. @[Aelendir](aelendir) did something unhurried with his posture and his voice that made the checkpoint feel, briefly, like a formality between colleagues; @[Iratius](iratius) stood in visible reach of @[Volcaris](volcaris) and radiated the price of error; @[Corvyn](corvyn) produced papers — they were Lady Mariel's letters of character, upside down — with such administrative confidence that the corporal actually stamped one. But it was the sergeant who decided it: an orc gone gray at the temples, old truce-scars laddered up both forearms, who looked too long at the company — at @[Amyl](amyl)'s warden-green, at the @[crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil) at @[Tirig](tirig)'s throat — and then stood aside and tilted his head north, once, a motion his own troopers could not see. They walked on. “Some gates in this dominion open the wrong way,” @[Saeron](saeron) said, a mile later, “if the gatekeeper remembers an older oath.” It felt less like an escape than a handing-on, and it was.

---

For two days after the bridge something hunted them anyway — horns behind, twice, at dusk and dawn, and once the far-off belling of dogs — the dominion's slower, official curiosity, following its own paperwork north. It never closed. On the third day the first true storm of the winter came down off the Spine like a decision, and the North, which belonged to someone, belonged for thirty hours to the weather instead; and the weather, older than every title ever posted, hid them thoroughly and asked for nothing.

Below the snows they made their last provisioning, at a trade-hamlet that called itself, without irony, the last post of the world — and spent Lady Mariel's traveling purse on the North's own answers to the North: furs sewn for wearing over furs; bone snow-eyes against the glare; tallow and wax for skin and steel; a light sledge for the gear that outgrew their backs. The circles' parting gifts came into their own here too — warden-wool cloaks that shed cold the way oilcloth sheds rain, one apiece, Neth's doing — and Neth herself proved a ruthless quartermaster, auditing every pack and confiscating vanity by the pound. Only @[Tirig](tirig) needed less than the ledger said she should. The cold's old courtesy toward her held even here, at the cold's own front door, and by now the company simply planned around it, the way sailors plan around a member who does not get seasick.

The last post gave them one more thing, and it came on wings: a Harper bird, improbable and indignant and iced to the leg-tube, that had chased them up half the North to deliver four lines in @[Aevren](aevren)'s hand. The @[Ledger](the-silent-ledger)'s cipher is old. Older than it has any right to be, and vain — it wants to be read, which is how I will beat it. Keys I have. Whiles I make. — A. @[Saeron](saeron) read it aloud twice and was seen to smile at vain, and the bird ate half their hard cheese, insulted the sledge, and went south. The book had not forgotten the Ledger. Neither, it was good to learn, had the woman holding it.

They climbed for a week after the storm, into a country that dispensed with pretenses: stone, ice, sky, and the Spine filling half of it, close now, its glaciers hanging like held breath. @[The crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil) changed as they climbed. It stopped leaning and started pulling — @[Tirig](tirig) woke on the ninth morning with her fist already raised off the bedroll, wrist cocked northward like a compass needle finding its pole — and it had gone cold in a way that was not the mountain's cold, a cold with intention in it, though never toward her: the frost that furred every strap and buckle of the company's kit continued, as it had in a cell in Triboar, to decline entirely to touch her. Nobody remarked on it anymore. Everybody noticed.

The others carried their own weather up the passes. A single @[black feather](the-raven-queen) waited for @[Sov](sov) at the mouth of the high valley, laid on the snow's crust, straight as a drawn line, pointing the way they were already going — approval, he read it, or direction, or nothing, and he was honest enough by now to know the reading was his. @[Amyl](amyl) and Neth botanized the treeline like two people committing a country to memory, arguing happily about lichens in the thin air. And @[Iratius](iratius) walked north with his jaw newly set, because the alignment had broken at the mountains' foot. The whole road up from the Tree, the two pulls had agreed — crystal and sword, two rulings on one bearing — and then, a day below the high valley, @[Volcaris](volcaris) began to lean away: east first, then south of east, the warmth swinging off their heading like a hound leaning at a fork, the mark — if it was the mark — turning some wide circuit of its own back down toward the low country and the moors. He said nothing about it for a full day. Then, at the fire, only: “It moves again. Whatever it is.” And he kept north with the party, which cost him more than he showed, and did a private arithmetic on his fingers at the night fires that he never shared — days, the party guessed, against some ledger with a date on it. He caught @[Saeron](saeron) watching once and said only, “I am owed a debt before winter's end. It will keep.” It was not clear which of them he was reassuring.

---

The Veilmark sat where the high valley narrowed to a throat: a camp of hide and stacked stone below a cliff, and in the cliff, a cave mouth — and the party knew it for their door before anyone spoke, because the cave was breathing. Outward. A slow, steady exhalation of cold that never once inhaled, stirring the camp's banners toward the valley all day and all night: a door that exhales. Beside the mouth, cut deep into the living rock and older than any hand in the camp, ran the mark the place is named for: a single long line, broken at its center by a gap the width of a palm — the world, and the place where the world isn't. The camp itself held perhaps forty souls: clan remnants, gray-scarred keepers of the old truce, two families with children — the unlicensed of the dominion, gathered at the one gate the dominion's paperwork could not seem to reach.

It was @[Saeron](saeron) who put words to what the cliff-mark was doing to everyone's back teeth. He stood before it a long while that first evening — the long line, the gap driven through its center — and then said, in the voice he kept for exact things: “There was a mark on @[Sera](sera)'s pendant. The second one. A line, with a chevron struck down through it — like a veil pierced from above.” He drew them both in the frost on a flat stone, side by side: the pierced line; the parted line. Kin, everyone agreed, leaning in — two words in one alphabet, written by hands that never met — the same script and not the same word, and nobody could take it one syllable further than that. The record notes, for those keeping their own margins, that it can take it no further either. Yet.

@[Gorvach](gorvach) stood at the cave mouth as though the intervening season had been an afternoon. Broader than they remembered, in furs now instead of chains, the burn-scarred palm wrapped in leather — and the frost-whispers that had haunted his cell were gone from around him, though the party noticed he stood in the mouth's cold breath as other men stand in sunlight. He looked at them without surprise, the way he did everything without surprise. “You came,” he said. Flat. Literal. True. It was, the party realized, the warmest greeting they had ever heard him give.

They had learned his grammar in a cell, and it held: literal questions, literal answers, and the door marked why kept shut. How did you come here? “Walked.” What is this place? “The mouth. I keep it.” For whom? “For the ones who kept me.” And then @[Gorvach](gorvach) did the thing none of them was prepared for: he asked a question — he, who had bled rather than answer one — and the question he asked was the forbidden kind. He looked down at @[Tirig](tirig), at the small @[crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil) burning cold at her throat, and said: “Why do you go in?” The camp's wind moved the banners. Tirig, who volunteered nothing on principle, stood in the question a long moment — and answered it true, and kept it to five words, and the record has always suspected she surprised herself: “To give something back.” Gorvach held her eyes. Then he inclined his head — not to her; to the crystal, the way one bows to a superior — exactly as he had once done through the bars of a cell, and stood aside from the mouth he kept.

They spent the night as the camp's guests, and spent it asking literal questions. What is inside? “White. Trees. Two skies.” What lives there? “What is left of a court.” What should we fear? “The one who keeps the ice.” @[Corvyn](corvyn), carefully: The Lady beneath the ice? And @[Gorvach](gorvach) was quiet for the length of two breaths of the cave's cold, and then delivered the correction with the terrible precision of a man who has met her: “She is not beneath it.” A pause. “She is it.” Nobody had a follow-up. And as they banked the fires he gave them the mouth's one law, unasked, which from Gorvach amounted to a speech: “Inside — do not take. Anything. It counts.” His eyes did not go to @[Tirig](tirig) when he said it, with such deliberateness that they might as well have.
