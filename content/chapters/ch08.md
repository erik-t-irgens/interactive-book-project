# VIII. Unmasked
*In which the party proves it was never there, a host is thrown down his own stairs, and the party escapes into a town already on fire*

@audio: masquerade-gilt fade=4
It began as a quiet exit and became a fight for the door, and by the end of it the party understood that the whole evening had been a stage set that was already being struck around them.

Before it turned, there were a few last quiet moments. @[Saeron](saeron) studied Grey Thimble's mask and found it disturbing for what it wasn't: utterly featureless, recognizable as nothing and no one, a blank where a face should be. He shared a bottle with Boren Barasque, who was in high, careless spirits despite having gotten precisely nowhere all night. Boren needed armed men to guard his caravans — caravans hauling ingots, he let slip — and when Saeron asked why a caravan master would come shopping for mercenaries here, of all the strange places, Boren's gaze drifted to Grey Thimble and stopped there. If you don't know, he said, I probably shouldn't tell you.

@audio: mirror-glass fade=1
@unlock: boars-rest.2
*And then the evening asked its question, and the question landed like a dropped stone. Who still has their weapons? The party looked up, and the hall was empty. Not emptying — empty, entirely and at once: every chair, every table, every candle and plate and guest simply gone, as though no masquerade had ever happened here at all, as though the whole gilded crowd had been a thing painted on the air and someone had wiped it clean. There had never been a party. There had only ever been the party — the eight of them — standing in an empty hall, and the trap closing.*

---

When @[Tirig](tirig), still wearing Darathra's face, moved for the exit, the guards barred the way without a word. And @[Hyuth](hyuth)'s voice came down out of the empty air to fill the room: I don't think I can let you leave yet. Do you really want to make a mess of things?

A gargoyle dropped from the ceiling in a grinding shriek of stone. @[Sov](sov) cut down two guards in the first breath of it and slipped out through the gap he'd made — breaking, as it happened, for the other half of the night's disaster before the party even knew there was one — and the rest of them turned to fight, guards and gargoyle both, in a hall that had a moment ago held a hundred masked revelers and now held only enemies.

*Only when the guards and the gargoyle were dealt with did @[Hyuth](hyuth) deign to join the battle himself, apparating into the hall — and as he did, the entire house seemed to wake around him and enter the fight on his side. This was the thing the party had half-felt all evening and could now not deny: @[Boar's Rest](boars-rest) was not merely Hyuth's manor, it was in some sense his, an extension of him, and it lashed at them with a will of its own between his blows. He opened, tellingly, not with a paladin's steel but with a gout of eldritch force — and missed. It was the first plain confirmation of what he truly was: not the holy warrior he played at, but something that drew its power from a darker contract.*

@[Saeron](saeron) ended the argument the direct way. He charged up the stairs, closed with Hyuth on the ledge above, and shoved him bodily off it — the two of them taking the fall together, stone rushing up to meet them both. The house convulsed with lair actions of its own, hurling what it could at the party between exchanges. But Hyuth, grounded and battered, came down fast. And when the party stood over him ready to finish it, he begged — pleaded for his life, all his host's grandeur collapsed into a frightened man on the floor of his own hall — and the party stayed their hands.

With Hyuth subdued and pleading, the company divided. Some stayed to secure him and to reach the vault and the shrouded thing Saeron had glimpsed. And the others — following Sov, who was already gone — raced out and down toward Darathra's Tower, because word had begun to reach even into Boar's Rest that something was very wrong in the town below. What follows happened in two places at once.

---

## In the Streets

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=4
@[Sov](sov), @[Corvyn](corvyn), and @[Amyl](amyl) broke for the Tower through a Triboar that was coming apart at the seams. A disheveled woman begged them for water. Doors hung half-open on dark rooms. People ran in the streets with the particular panic of those who do not know what they are fleeing, only that they must. Word moved through the chaos in fragments, and the fragments disagreed: the lower levels of the Tower had been locked; there was fire below; orcs had gotten in — though no one who said so had seen one — and the Lord Protector herself was down there, some said fighting, some said trapped, all of them certain and none of them able to say how they knew.

*Another woman was frantic over her boy. Over @[Gregory](gregory) — the tiefling child the party had pulled from the rat-hole in the very first days, the boy who had never been quite right since — who had run off into the unraveling town claiming he could hear voices calling him. And beneath the party's feet, low and deep and felt in the bone more than the ear, a faint rumble rolled through the ground, as though @[something vast](the-quiet-thing) were shifting in its sleep far below the streets.*

@[Corvyn](corvyn) took hold of the panic the way a bard can. Gregory would have to wait — there was no finding one boy in this — but a fire could be fought, and a crowd could be turned to fight it. Smoke was threading up out of the Tower's lower slits by the time they reached it, and Corvyn turned the terror of the street into a bucket-train aimed at it: frightened people set to useful work, a chain of hands running water down toward the smoke, while the party pressed on toward the woman somewhere beneath it all.

## In the Vault

@audio: mirror-glass fade=3
@unlock: hyuth.2
The party that had stayed turned their captive host to account. They convinced @[Hyuth](hyuth) to surrender fully — though he could barely speak. Something outside him physically stopped his tongue when it neared certain matters, a compulsion laid on him that seized his throat when he tried to say too much. What he could give, he gave in pieces.

*His bag held a silver instrument etched with fine symbols. @[Aelendir](aelendir) knew them on sight for what the party had not: musical notes. He played them — and nothing happened, not on their own; the tune wanted something to be played into. @[Saeron](saeron) remembered the locked study in the basement, and the book and the shrouded statue behind that door, and understood that the instrument and the room were two halves of one lock.*

@unlock: darz.2
@[Hyuth](hyuth) confessed what he was able to. He did not know what was unfolding at the Tower, he said; he knew only that he had been instructed to delay the party, to keep them here and occupied while it happened. And then, haltingly, fighting his own throat for every word: I set @[Sera](sera) and @[Darz](darz) up together. He had engineered their love — the meeting, the notes, the whole doomed romance — and when the party pressed him on why, the compulsion took him and he could not say. He only knew that he had done it. Someone had made him do it, and taken the reason out of his reach.

## In the Streets

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=3
The three pushed on through the failing town toward the Tower, the rumble beneath them, the fires guttering up behind, the word of orcs in the deep growing more certain with every panicked mouth — the way rumors do when no one can check them. Somewhere below, Darathra was in the thick of it, whatever it was. Somewhere out in the dark, a boy who could hear voices was running toward the thing that called him. The party ran, and the ground muttered under their boots, and Triboar came apart around them a little more with every street they crossed.

## In the Vault

@audio: mirror-glass fade=3
With their captive in tow, the party descended to the locked door and the shrouded shape beside it. @[Aelendir](aelendir) set the silver instrument into the keyhole and played the song through it — and the magical barrier fell. @[Saeron](saeron) checked the book for traps, found what he could, and seized it. And as he lifted it, the cloth over the statue stirred.

*He asked Hyuth what the thing under the veil was. The answer was a single word: a gargoyle. They did not stay to test it. They took the ledger — the @[Silent Ledger](the-silent-ledger), the hidden record of every assurance Hyuth had sold and every order he had followed — and they ran, and slammed the gate behind them, and behind the bars the veiled thing settled back into stillness and let them go.*

And as they fled, a voice followed them out of Boar's Rest — the bartender's voice, the velvet-knife voice, the voice of the thing that cast no shadow: You've made an enemy this day. Not a threat so much as an announcement, a line drawn, a thing that had been playing a game deciding to stop pretending it was only a game. Whatever @[Veyran](veyran) was, it was no longer content to pour drinks and watch. It had declared itself.

---

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=5
The two halves of the party found each other again in the wreck of the night — the vault group with the Silent Ledger and its silver key and a subdued, half-mute Hyuth in tow; the streets group with the town burning behind them, a bucket-train working water down into the Tower's smoking lower floors, and the @[Lord Protector](darathra) somewhere beneath it all with rumor thick around her. They had gone to a party to gather intelligence, and they were leaving it with a fiend's enmity, a captive who could not explain his own crimes, a ledger written in a code they could not yet read, and a home coming apart behind them.

And under all of it, still, the rumble. Deeper now, or nearer, or both. Somewhere out in the dark of the failing town a boy was following voices down toward the thing that made that sound — @[Gregory](gregory), who had gone into a hole full of frightened rats a lifetime ago and come back listening to something no one else could hear. He was out there now, running toward it. And whatever was @[rousing beneath Triboar](the-quiet-thing), patient and vast and finally stirring, the party could feel that they were almost out of time to reach it before it woke.
