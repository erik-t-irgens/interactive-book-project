# VII. Old Friends & Gilded Promises
*In which promises are made that should not have been, a song opens a door, and a bartender who casts no shadow tells a paladin the truth*

@audio: masquerade-gilt fade=6
The masquerade wore on, and as it deepened it sharpened, because now the party knew there was a downstairs.

The evening had not ended — it had only turned, the way a long night turns past its bright hour into something more candid. The masks and the murmurs thickened as the hall settled into itself, and where the early evening had been reconnaissance the later hours became a hunt: coins on the mind, the private tasting as the goal, and the certainty growing that the whole gilded affair was arranged to conceal whatever waited in the cellar the party had not yet been permitted to enter.

---

@[Saeron](saeron) had come to the masquerade wearing no mask at all — the only bare face in the hall. The ushers tried twice at the door and were outlasted, both times, by a man simply standing still; and when a glittering guest finally asked him, midway through the evening, where his mask was, he answered, “Faces are already masks. One is enough,” and the guest laughed, and moved on, and was seen to look back twice. His plate from the long tables went, entire, to the boy who cleared it — this happened three times before the staff began inventing reasons to pass near him — and Saeron, working the floor with that same monk's stillness, marked two guests who moved alone through the crowd: Mistress @[Aevren](aevren), the courier out of Everlund, and Grey Thimble, the quiet merchant-patron — a soft-spoken figure behind a mask of plain, unadorned pewter, who smiled often and calmly, in the particular way of people whose calm is the most dangerous thing about them. Grey took neither food nor drink the whole evening — a small abstinence that meant something, though not yet what. And when Saeron offered Aevren help, her poise sharpened, as though help were a word she had reasons to be careful around.

*@[Tirig](tirig), still wearing Darathra's face, went to @[Hyuth](hyuth) at the hearth. He was cordial and he gave away nothing, and one hand kept straying to a leather satchel at his side, protective, the gesture of a man guarding something small and valuable. What was in it the party would glimpse only later: coins, and a little silver implement whose purpose none of them could name, and the ordinary sundries of a wealthy man's pocket. But it was the silver tool the eye kept snagging on, without yet knowing why.*

---

Hyuth's game that evening was called Three Promises, and it was a courtier's amusement with real teeth: an exchange of vows, lightly made, that bound the makers all the same. @[Tirig](tirig), as Darathra, opened with a promise calibrated to charm a man like Hyuth — a pledge to turn a blind eye to a little tax fraud — and Hyuth could not help the blush and the delighted grin of a man who has just been told his sins are safe. He offered protection in return, protection whenever she might need it; but Tirig asked instead for a coin, one of the tokens that bought a seat below.

*And Hyuth named his own price for it, the third promise, the binding one: we do not interfere with each other. Tirig agreed. And Hyuth carefully drew a coin from the guarded satchel and passed it across, and a bargain was sealed between the host and a woman who was not the woman he thought she was — a promise of non-interference given under a false face, to a man whose whole enterprise the party had come to interfere with. It was a clever piece of theater. It would also, in ways no one at the hearth foresaw, come to matter.*

---

@[Sov](sov), meanwhile, found @[Darz](darz) again — still drinking, still grieving, brittle as thin ice. And when Sov let @[Sera](sera)'s name fall into the conversation, something in Darz went rigid. He had not given anyone her name. He said so, the confusion curdling toward alarm: I don't remember telling you her name. He spiraled, groping after a thread that would not come, and then caught himself and apologized and rose and left, carrying his grief and his new unease out of the hall. Amyl's owl familiar tailed him toward Gwaeron's Rest and lost him in the dark. A later pass of the familiar showed something else worth noting: Darathra's Tower, which should have been shut and guarded, stood with its doors open and a soft smoke curling from within.

---

It was @[Iratius](iratius) who found the key, though he did not know it was a key. He sought out @[Aelendir](aelendir), the bard, and hummed for him the @[fragment of melody](the-tune) that had haunted all of them since the beginning — the tune Corvyn had played that first night in the inn, the one they all somehow knew. And Aelendir, given the fragment, completed it. He performed the song — not on lute or voice but on the instrument he was never far from: the @[shallow bowl of fey-wrought metal](the-resonance-bowl) from his back, a handpan whose struck tones hung in the air a breath longer than sound should, as though each note were resonating somewhere other than the room it was played in.

@audio: gray-dream fade=2
*The whole hall went still. Not politely quiet — enthralled, every masked face turned, every conversation stopped, Lady Heartsilver frozen mid-departure at the door as though the music had reached into her and held. And in that hush the gray came again, the flat cold interstice, the circle of the eight of them standing in the colorless country — and far off, at the edge of it, the eighth figure, distant and turned away, present and unreachable as ever. The song had opened the dream in the middle of a crowded room, and the party stood inside it a moment, and came back to a hall full of people slowly remembering how to breathe.*

@audio: masquerade-gilt fade=3
The party marked who had truly listened — who had leaned into the song rather than merely fallen silent under it. Veyran, at his bar, smug and certain, as though he had expected it. Grey Thimble, intent. Mistress Aevren, keen. Three people in that hall had heard something in the music that the rest had only felt, and all three would matter.

---

@audio: mirror-glass fade=4
*@[Tirig](tirig) went to the bar, and @[Veyran](veyran) poured her something he made out of sight and set down with a flourish: the Heart of Winter's Veil, he called it, an icy aurora-bright thing, all snowmelt and cold light, a drink he promised would clarify — inconveniently. And as he said it he watched Tirig with a knowing that had no business in a bartender's eyes; he spoke to her winter-cool nature as though he had read it off her like a label, named the two skies she stood between, warned her that choosing badly would close them both. He knew too much. And as Tirig studied him back, she caught the thing that turned the whole evening cold: Veyran cast no shadow. The lamplight fell on him and stopped there. Whatever poured her drink and smiled its velvet-knife smile, it did not throw a shadow on the floor like the living do.*

@[Aevren](aevren) drifted near and probed, gently — were they all going to the tasting? — and after a brief, odd exchange with the false Darathra she slipped away, leaving a whisper behind her: before descending to the cellar, find me on the second floor.

---

They found her upstairs, and @[Amyl](amyl) laid a Zone of Truth over the meeting so that nothing said in it could be a lie. Within that ring of honesty, Aevren confirmed what she was: @[Harper business](the-harpers). You're the ones helping with the Sharrans, she said, and it was not a question. She could not go down to the tasting herself — she was not, she admitted, officially invited, and she was uneasy about the open door at Darathra's Tower. But she offered what she could. If you need anything decoded, she told them, I'm your woman. The party had found their cipher, though they did not yet have the thing that would need deciphering.

---

@audio: masquerade-gilt fade=4
The coins bought their way down at last. @[Veyran](veyran) reappeared behind a second bar in the cellar — of course he did — and @[Hyuth](hyuth) welcomed the chosen few, and made a point of calling out the presence of Darathra among them, the Lord Protector come to his little gathering. Some stayed upstairs and did not descend: Theron Veil the scholar, the twins Irska and Fenn, Boren Barasque, and Grey Thimble, who watched the whole affair rather than partaking. Below, the room hummed with the real commerce of the evening — protection packages, guard rotations, quiet guarantees traded in low voices among people who could pay for certainty in an uncertain season.

*In the murmur, the party caught two conversations worth carrying away. Boren Barasque, the caravan master, was all but begging Grey Thimble for help — the terms unclear, the tone urgent, a man reaching for something he badly needed from a merchant who gave nothing away. And @[Aelendir](aelendir), trading airy gossip with the outfitter twins, gathered a rumor worth more than gossip: that some disruptive new magic was abroad in the world, and that Hyuth's great wealth might be thinner than the gilt suggested — a rich man's fortune propped on something less solid than it looked.*

---

@audio: mirror-glass fade=4
And @[Iratius](iratius) went to @[Veyran](veyran), and staked a promise of his own. He would share what bound him to this strange company, he said, if Veyran would tell him what he had lost — the memory with the hole in it, the thing he could not reach.

Veyran was happy to talk. The knot that binds you, he told the paladin, was tied with defter hands than mine — disclaiming the making of the party's bond even as he confirmed there was one. But of Iratius himself he knew a great deal, and he gave it back in pieces calculated to wound. He remembered Iratius coming to him, once, desperate for revenge. He remembered the assurances Iratius had made in that desperation. He remembered that the location of the quarry — the one Iratius hunted — had been part of the deal between them. And when Iratius pressed on the matter of betrayal, of who had wronged him, Veyran supplied the name as lightly as a garnish: @[Volcaris](volcaris), of course. And added, with the same amused precision, that more than one target still remained.

@unlock: veyran.2
Iratius flicked a coin at him — whether in payment or contempt — and the coin passed through Veyran and fell to the floor beyond, through the body of a thing that stood where a man appeared to stand and was not a man at all. A bartender with no shadow, who knew a paladin's forgotten bargains, and through whom a tossed coin fell as through empty air. Iratius had come to the bar for the truth of himself and gotten a portion of it, and the portion was worse than the not-knowing had been.

---

*@[Hyuth](hyuth) found the false Darathra again, and his cordiality had grown a needle. No interruptions, he reminded her — the vow from the game — really? And then, with a smug and pitying air, he touched the wound of Sera and Darz, watching for the flinch. He let fall hints of a history the party could not place: you don't remember when we hosted the Twelve? And of Darz, with a proprietary fondness: he invited himself. We go way back. Every word was a small assertion that Hyuth knew more of the town's true shape than the woman governing it — or more than the strangers wearing her authority, which amounted, for his purposes, to the same thing.*

Elsewhere Theron Veil drew @[Amyl](amyl) aside with a scholar's excitement. There were rumors, he said, of an illithid colony that had once burrowed beneath this land — mind flayers, long gone — and the great hole that had opened in Triboar might be nothing so novel as it seemed, might be only their old leftover wound, an ancient excavation uncovered when giants disturbed the earth above it. It was a theory, and it lingered. Find anything, Theron told him, and bring it to me — I'll be in Triboar a few days yet, then on to Silverymoon. And he pressed into Amyl's hand a card with his direction on it, a thread with a city at the end of it.

---

The evening wound toward its close and the party gathered its takings. Amyl's owl reported once more: the Tower door still open, the smoke still curling within, a wrongness that would not resolve itself from the air. @[Saeron](saeron), working the edges as the staff he was pretending to be, tried a locked study — and found it too well secured to crack, though through the gap he glimpsed a book, and beside it a statue draped in cloth, shrouded from view. He could not reach either. He diverted what food and supplies he could toward the refugees who needed them, and stayed on to keep working while the others made ready to slip away for a rest they had earned and would not get.

But it was @[Tirig](tirig) who got the evening's last and coldest word. She asked @[Hyuth](hyuth) plainly, dropping for a moment the dance of it, why Darathra had needed to attend at all — why her presence had mattered so much to him. And Hyuth looked at the Lord Protector who was not the Lord Protector, and smiled, and gave an answer that meant far more than the party could yet unpack. It seems like you already know.
