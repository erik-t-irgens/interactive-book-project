# XXV. The Stone Door — The Bought Glade
*The Stone Door*

## Corvyn

@image: images/corvyn.jpg Corvyn

@audio: gray-dream fade=6
The glade's gray took them at the tree-line like a change of law. Color did not die inside it — that was the first horror, and the subtler one: the grass grew green and read gray, the stream ran bright and sounded like paperwork, the berries on the bramble tasted, @[Corvyn](corvyn) reported, of the idea of berries. Nothing was dead. Delight was dead. The party's own blaze, which had made flowers turn for days, guttered at the boundary like lamps carried into bad air, and Neth said the true thing first, in her flat warden's voice: “This was not weather. This was harvest. Years of it. To the stubble.”

@[The fairy](the-fairy) lived at the heart of it, in the hollow tree, and she was the second horror, because she was lovely. Small, moth-winged, faded the way a beloved dress fades — washed too many times, kept too long, worn still — and her joy at the sight of @[Corvyn](corvyn) was total and terrible: “My Corvyn!” she sang, gray to the wingtips, in the gladdest voice in the saddest place any of them had stood, and drew him in to see her treasures. The hollow tree was furnished with him. Playbills in six languages. A carved button he had lost at nine. A snapped string from his first violin, kept in a walnut shell lined with moss. She had followed his whole career the way tides follow the moon, from a glade going gray around her, and she was so proud, and she had no idea, and watching Corvyn understand that she had no idea was the hardest thing the party had watched since a custodian held a gate.

He asked her what had happened to the glade. She made a small gesture, wing and hand together, that took in the gray as though it were a spell of rain: “It went quiet. Gray weather. It will pass, sweetness. Everything passes.” It had been going quiet, the party's eyes agreed silently over her head, for about as long as one gnome had been performing. And when @[Corvyn](corvyn) asked — gently, greenly, in the voice of a man opening a door he has kept shut for decades — whether he might see @[the paper from the night in the yard](the-gift-contract), she brightened, and fetched it from an acorn case, folded impossibly small, and handed it over with the pride of a grandmother producing a christening gown. “I never read it, sweetness. Reading is for the suspicious.”

@[Saeron](saeron) and @[Aelendir](aelendir) unfolded it together, on a flat stone, with the care of men defusing something. The gift-clause was four lines and generous. The rest was schedule. A tithe of joy, it read — in a clerk's hand so fine it seemed to apologize for itself — upon every performance of the granted craft, assessed against the audience thereof, collected by the grantor's assign; the residence of the guarantor standing surety for uninterrupted supply; product to be conveyed to — and here the fine hand went finer still — @[the appetite](veyran). Aelendir read it twice and set it down. “Every audience you ever played,” he said, because @[Corvyn](corvyn) deserved it plainly, “was skimmed. A tithe off the top of every joy you caused. And her glade stood collateral, and her glade has been paying.” The gray, in every direction, was suddenly an itemization. Corvyn stood in the middle of his life's receipts and said, in a small voice, “I have been pickpocketing everyone who ever loved me,” and the fairy, who had understood at last, at worst, all at once, said nothing at all, and her wings went still for the first time since they had arrived.

At dusk, something moved at the glade's edge. Foliage first — a patient rustle, unhurried, the sound of someone small picking a path — and then a lantern, bobbing at the height a lantern bobs when a child carries it, and the party's hands went to hilts and then slowly came off them, one by one, as the light stepped out of the ferns. @[Corvyn](corvyn) saw the fiddle before he saw the face. An eighth-size fiddle, strapped across a small back — a shape he had not seen in decades and could have drawn blind — and above it, unchanged, unaged, exactly as the stonemason's yard had last held it, a face. The boy looked at the gnome by the fire for a long moment, head tilted, working something out. “You look funny,” he said at last, in a voice Corvyn had buried. “You got old.” And Corvyn Stonemason's-son, who had been devastated in front of kings and never once shown it, did the bravest performance of his career: he smiled, and kept his voice level, and said, “Somebody had to,” while everything behind his face fell down.

The story came out of the boy in pieces, over supper, in the maddening sideways way of children and the fey-kept: he had woken the night @[his brother](corvyns-brother) left home and heard the music going away down the road — the new music, the impossible music — and he had followed it, because he missed him already, and the country had opened for a child following a song the way it does not open for armies. He had been walking toward the fiddle ever since. That was all. That was the whole of it. And @[Corvyn](corvyn) sat very still while a lifetime's arithmetic came apart, because the fairy had never taken anything; the ledger he had serviced under every ovation had never existed; the price had not been his brother — the price had never been his brother — a boy had loved him and followed him and been lost by loving him, which is a grief, but it is not a debt. He excused himself from the fire, briefly, and stood at the glade's edge in the dark, and came back with his face arranged, and taught the boy a scale. The party pretended, expertly by now, not to have counted the minutes.

They understood the rest by degrees, gently, around the fairy's fire. The brother had walked some days, danced at a harvest thing, helped a badger-faced fellow mend a cart. A kind season. He was hungry for news of home in the way of a man three weeks gone. And he had been in the country so long that the country had kept his name — the Feywild collects what you do not watch, and no one watches their own name — so that he answered to brother and to friend and to nothing else, and did not seem to miss it, which was the worst part, and the party watched @[Corvyn](corvyn) decide, visibly, in real time, not to tell him yet how long it had been, or what that length had probably cost at home. The record respects the postponement. It will not last past this book's third part, and Corvyn knows it.

@audio: seam-of-winter fade=3
They slept that night in the green-gold patch, all of them, at the boy's insistence and nobody's objection. The Feywild's noon came up the next day like theater lights — a sourceless golden blaze that made every color in the country louder — and the glade demonstrated, in full sun, exactly what had been done to it: the gray did not argue with the noon. It simply declined it, the way a debt declines a compliment. And into that lit and unlifted quiet came sound: bells first, small and cold and many, like icicles keeping time; then hooves — hooves on rime, crisp and ringing, where no rime had been a breath before — the winter of the riders arriving a minute ahead of the riders themselves.

The Court arrived the following noon, and it arrived as paperwork does everywhere: with riders. Six of them, on white elk whose breath frosted the warm air, in tabards of gray-silver, bearing wands of office like folded winter — the @[Winter Court](the-winter-court)'s excise — its collectors — riding the furthest demesne the Court calls its own — and they reined at the glade's edge and read the charges to the air itself, as though the glade were the defendant, which in their law it partly was. Unlicensed trans-planar commerce in distilled sentiment. Contraband instrumentality, one, stringed. Guarantor resident and liable. Schedule of seizure to follow. “The Court does not judge,” their captain said, dismounting, in a voice like a door in winter. “The Court prices.”

@[Aelendir](aelendir) translated, low, at the party's shoulder, while the riders dismounted in their own private winter — not the words, which were plain enough, but the world under the words, which was not. The Feywild, he reminded them, is emotion made into country; and the Courts are what that country has instead of laws of nature — regulators, gardeners, dam-keepers of feeling. Nothing in Faerie is owned. Everything is owed. Every gift is a debt wearing a ribbon, every favor accrues, and value here is not gold but obligation — which is why the Court does not judge and does not punish: to them a wrong is not a sin, it is an unpaid amount, and justice is arithmetic. “What they have said,” he finished, “is this: the fiend's trade in bottled feeling was smuggling; the violin was the smuggler's instrument; the fairy vouched for the original bargain and is therefore liable for all of it. And now,” as the captain unrolled a scroll of frost, “they will name the price.”

Two things happened at the riders' arrival that the record files under later. Their captain, passing the party's line, stopped before @[Tirig](tirig) — stopped wrongly, too suddenly, like a man walking into glass — and inclined his head one careful degree, and said, “Keeper,” and moved on, and would not meet her eye again, and Tirig stood there wearing the word. The office, it appeared, traveled faster than the officeholder. And at @[Gorvach](gorvach), the captain paused a second time, gaze going to the burn-scarred palm as though reading a posted notice, and said, “Your term ended, gatekeeper. The mark is cold. Why did you still keep?” And Gorvach looked back at the folded winter of him and gave the whole of his philosophy in one word: “Habit.” The captain considered this, nodded once, satisfied — fey law recognizing sufficient answer when it hears one — and moved on.

The bargaining ran the afternoon, and @[Corvyn](corvyn) advocated — for the fairy, for the violin, for a glade that could not speak in its own defense — and it went as pricings go, which is to say against him, item by scheduled item, until he stopped arguing in words. He took out the violin — contraband, one, stringed — and before six wands of office could object, he played the phrase. Eight bars. @[The tune](the-tune) — the one from a taproom at the end of the world, the one the forest of two skies had kept humming for an hour, the one that had been leading them across a plane by voice alone — and the assessors' frost-quills stopped on their slates. The Court had priced joy all season; joy was commodity; there were columns for it. There was no column for awe. And a court that prices everything must price what it is shown or adjourn, and the Winter Court has not adjourned in ten thousand years. The captain stood in the ringing after-silence, recalculating the afternoon.

The settlement, entered into the frost-slates and witnessed by the glade, came in three clauses, and the record will lay them out plainly, because fey clauses are built to be admired and misread. First: the instrument, ransomed back against consideration received — marked, however, with a ferrule — a slim silver band riveted at the neck — the Court's sign that a thing has been paid for but remains on the books — and the violin has been a half-ounce heavier ever since, and @[Corvyn](corvyn) swears it plays the truer for it. Second: the fairy. She had vouched for the original bargain, which under Court arithmetic made the whole smuggling debt hers, and the remedy for a guarantor who cannot pay is seizure — they would have taken her, as an asset, into winter, for as long as a debt takes at Court rates, which is long. Unless someone of standing offered to answer for her instead. Corvyn did not let the clause finish being read. He put his own name into it — Corvyn Stonemason's-son, surety for the fairy who had bought his life — meaning that if she ever defaults again, the Court comes for him. The arrow of the oldest debt in his life, reversed in one signature: the boy she had purchased now stood purchase for her. He signed without being asked twice, and the captain regarded him the way an auditor regards a man paying a stranger's taxes, and made a small notation nobody could read. Third: the commerce itself — the tithe, the skim, the appetite's whole supply-line — declared contraband within the demesne, its agents subject to seizure on sight; which is to say that somewhere down a long cold ledger, a creditor the party had not yet met acquired, that afternoon, an enemy court.

“The Court thanks the instrument,” the captain said at the last, mounting, with the first and only degree of warmth in the whole proceeding. “First audience of any performance in this demesne is claimed in perpetuity.” And before the party could decide whether they had just been threatened, @[Aelendir](aelendir) supplied the plain of it: whenever Corvyn plays anywhere in the Court's country, forever, the Court hears it first — before any other living ear, a private audience owed off the top of every performance he ever gives here. “It is almost a compliment,” the eladrin added, dry as the frost. “It is also a leash. With them it is generally both.” They were gone the way winter goes, all at once, leaving the warm air rubbing its wrists.

@audio: door-threshold fade=4
The Court rode out as it had ridden in, on its own weather, and the glade let out a breath nobody had noticed it holding. The evening after paperwork has a particular quality anywhere in the worlds — loosened, tired, slightly silly — and the party had it by the fire: the boy asleep against the fairy with the eighth-size fiddle in his arms, the first green-gold of the ground going silver at the edges under the rising moons, the new color looking, in that light, heartbreakingly easy to lose. It was into that softness — the fed fire, the sleeping child, the fragile first inch of a healed place — that the night's true business arrived. @[The Hungry](the-sorrowsworn) came, because of course it did: seven vessels of kept feeling, blazing — and their companions' smaller warmths beside them — camped in the middle of a half-mile of starvation. It came up out of the gray the way want comes up in a body — gradually, then entirely — a gauntness the size of a haywain, made mostly of mouths, every mouth a different size and none of them closed, moving with the horrible economy of something that wastes nothing because it has nothing. Steel taught them the arithmetic in the first exchange: it ate the blow. Effort, fury, the heat off @[Iratius](iratius)'s summoned fire — everything they spent, it banked, and grew by exactly what they gave. The glade had been harvested to the stubble, and the harvest had come home, and it could not be fought, because fighting was food.

And it worked on you worst of all, mason's son, because this is your chapter, and the record turns to you at the fire's failing edge. Every mouth was singing, low, in the register of a yard at evening, and what they sang was the ledger: bought, bought, bought — your hands bought, your ear bought, your name on playbills bought, the delight of everyone who ever loved you skimmed at the door — and up out of the oldest room in you rose the eight-year-old's question with its answer finally attached: nothing of you is yours to give, and it never was. And you knew — the way Sov had known, and Amyl, and Tirig in the white — that the flood was being poured: something the gray had left in you was being played, string by string, by a thing whose whole nature was the emptiness where your gift's price went. It circled you with its mouths down, waiting for you to agree, and drifted — patient, certain — toward the smallest blaze in the camp: the brother, who had followed a sound across a world, and the fairy, who had loved a child so well she beggared her home for him.

You did the thing the schedule had no column for. You stepped inside the reach of the mouths — the party closing around you in a fighting hedge, @[Amyl](amyl)'s bulk and @[Bayne](bayne)'s pale line and @[Gorvach](gorvach)'s spear-length buying you bars, two, four, eight at a time against a thing that grew on their spending — and you fed it. Not fury. Not fire. You set bow to string inside the ferrule's new weight and you played @[the tune](the-tune) — the whole of it, unhurried, the way it wants to be played, the way she plays it on her lips in the silent country — and gave the starving glade the one joy in your possession that no tithe had ever touched. The mouths went still by ranks. Then they began, one by one, unhurried as dusk, to close — like windows being shut by someone walking home through a house — and the gauntness folded down and down, smaller and smaller, until what remained sat at the music's edge the way a dog sits at a door it has finally, finally been called through. When the tune ended, it was not there. Steam off soup. And where the music had pooled thickest, between the fire and the hollow tree, the glade took its first color back: one wash of green-gold, small as a rug, absolute as dawn.

Do the arithmetic the party did, standing in that impossible patch of gold. The fiend's schedule had skimmed every joy @[Corvyn Stonemason](corvyn)'s-son ever caused — decades of it, audiences beyond counting — and the Hungry, which was that skimming's orphan and residue and proof, had starved beside a fortune. But the tune fed it. @[The tune](the-tune), which he had carried since a taproom at the end of the world; which the forest of two skies had kept for an hour like a coin it meant to treasure; which no line of the fine print had ever collected on — because some things are not on the schedule, and they are not on it because they cannot be. The craft was bought; the record will not soften that, and neither will Corvyn. The music was always his. Both true. Both, now, finally, in writing.

The fairy wept at the edge of the green-gold like a woman at a christening. The brother stood in it with his boots planted, looking at color the way you look at a face you had begun to forget. And @[Corvyn](corvyn) folded the contract along its old creases — small, smaller, smallest — and put it away against a future fire, and caught @[Iratius](iratius) watching him do it, and the two of them held one of those looks the record has learned to bank. “We are going to need a very particular fire,” Corvyn said. “I know where one is kept,” said Iratius. Neither of them explained, and nobody asked, because the year was long and the Feast was coming and some appointments make themselves.

They slept that night half in gray and half in green-gold, seven blazes and their company, and the gray came for the seven in the smallest hour the way it always came — exact, as it has always been exact, in its count — and the door it opened was tall and bright and loud with work, and it smelled of stone dust and morning. This is what they watched.

## A Remembrance

*of Euphoria*

*— she is perhaps five-and-twenty —*

@audio: bright-country fade=10
@unlock: the-remembrances.2
*There is sound. Faint — as through deep water, as through a wall of years — but real, the first sound in all these silent countries, and it is the song. Whole verses of it, in many voices, rising and lapping and rising. The party stands in it the way one stands in warm rain, and more than one of them stops listening for clues and simply listens.*

*A wall is being raised in a green cut of valley — scaffolds and rope-cranes, dozens of people at work, and every single one of them is crisp. Every face kept: the fat mason and the widow with flour on her sleeves and the twin boys carrying one hod between them. Where loneliness kept a market of fog, joy keeps everyone. That is the accounting of this memory and it needs no translating.*

*The work itself is the marvel. Masons and mourners labor in pairs: a mourner leans close to a brick in a mason's hands and sings to it — sings a memory into it, lips shaping a story — and the brick takes light, softly, the way a coal takes breath. Set into the course, the light stays: a seam of glow along the mortar-line. And in the faces of the stones, at the right angle, small scenes move. A wedding under rain. A dog leaping in snow. A child riding shoulders through a fair. A toothless old woman laughing at something forever. The wall is a library of best days, and it is being built higher by the hour, and the party walks its length reading it like a shelf of unbearable books.*

*A mason's chisel slips and opens his palm. He laughs — the party hears it, water-far and unmistakable — binds the hand with a rag, and keeps carving, the handle slick with what joy costs and does not count. One of the watchers stares at that chisel a long moment, and the long moment stares back.*

*And she is the center of it, sleeves rolled, mortar-dust graying her dark hair by degrees — the party marks it, the gray arriving early as a joke the memory does not know it is telling. @[She laughs](the-girl-at-the-grave); she directs; she presses her forehead to an old mourner's forehead; she takes a turn on the rope-crane and nearly loses it and laughs harder. They have seen this face at thirteen, and at sixteen, and at fifty on a threshold night with the tools set down, and seeing it happy is very nearly the hardest thing the gray has asked of them.*

*Night closes it. The workers sleep where the nave will be, bundled among the tools, and she alone is awake — standing at the newest course with her palm flat to a stone that is still warm with singing. In it, small and bright, a scene flickers: a hearth, a chair, a shape made of warmth, telling a story to a little girl. She put her mother in first. The light of it moves on her face for a long time, and the far voices sing, and the gray — gently, and taking its time for once — comes.*
