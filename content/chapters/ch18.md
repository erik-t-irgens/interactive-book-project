# XVIII. The Rot Door — The Trial Under the Boughs
*The Rot Door*

---

## Amyl

@image: images/amyl.jpg Amyl Rachis

@audio: door-threshold fade=6
They came for him at first light, and he walked to the boughs with the mirror for company. The vision replayed as it had replayed on every important morning since the cave, unbidden and exact: the musk of rot, damp leaf and fungus closing over his senses like a broad hand; the pulse of roots below the soil; the deep green dark, and far down in it something enormous being patient — being, he had never found a better word, kind. A season ago it had felt like an accusation. This morning, walking to trial across the greatest roots in the world, it felt like a character witness that nobody would be permitted to call.

The bowl held some sixty of the circles' seniors by sunrise, ranked on the root-ridges in homespun the colors of their countries, and behind them everyone else the forest had gathered. The oath-friends were placed near the accused but not beside him — near enough to be seen swearing, far enough to be no shield — and Neth stood in the wardens' rank with her staff planted and her face doing the work of two loyalties at once.

The eldest read the charge, and her voice was trained under trees and needed no raising. @[Amyl Rachis](amyl), novice under watch. Workings of the Blighted path. Congress with an abomination. Complicity in the blight at Triboar, whose wound is in every grove from the ice to the sea — and here her arm rose, slow as a branch, toward the blackened vein on the Tree's great root — and now in the sickening of the Tree itself. She did not name the penalty for Blight-work. It has not changed in a thousand years, and the bowl's silence recited it for her.

---

The morning went to witnesses: the Silverymoon wardens on the seizure; a plains speaker who described, in a level voice that was worse than weeping, what Harvest night had felt like inside every grove she kept. Then the eldest said, “Bring the evidence,” and they brought his master.

What came through the hazel-gate walked patiently between two wardens and needed no chains, and @[Amyl](amyl) knew him before the crowd did, because Amyl had known him longest. His old master. The man who had taught him every green thing he knew — dead these two years, and these same two years in the circles' own custody: taken from the little grove where the raising was discovered, warded, watched, logged nightly by custodians who never learned what to call him, and carried at last to the Tree because the trial required its evidence. He came gray as undyed wool, moss grown neat at his collar like a second collar, moving with the slow economy of something conserving a finite store of itself. His eyes were fogged glass. They passed over the crowd without snagging — and found Amyl, and stopped, and though the dead face could not change, something behind it changed, plainly, the way a dark window changes when a lamp is carried through the room beyond. Every senior in the bowl felt it. That, of course, was why he had been brought. Amyl was on his feet without any memory of standing; sat again by his own strength; and apologized, for the first recorded time in the whole of this record, to no one.

The custodians' logs made the Circle's case, and it was a good case, which is what made it terrible. Two years of nightly observation of the fragments — the flickers of awareness the raised man still made: a faint mosslight that rose under his skin on some evenings, dim and green as foxfire in wet wood, steady as the moss it resembled, season upon season, unchanged. Except once. On @[Harvest night](the-harvest), at the hour the wound tore into the world's side, the light had blazed — tenfold, the custodian testified, her hands shaking on the page — lucent through skin and clothing, bright enough to read by. And he had spoken. One word. The only word in two years of keeping. He had said: “Breathing.” The custodian read it out and could not make it sound like anything but what the Circle had decided it was: “The abomination fed, Speakers. Twenty-five hundred souls went into the dark, and this thing lit up like a hearth. We watched it gorge.”

And in the oath-friends' rank, @[Amyl Rachis](amyl) went very still — not at the accusation; at the shape closing in him, quiet and total, like two halves of a broken bowl finding each other in the dark. The @[vastness under Triboar](the-quiet-thing). The vastness holding his master upright. The same patience. The same green. The same hand — and around both of them, he would have staked the trial on it, the same harness.

---

So @[Amyl Rachis](amyl) stood, and asked the assembled circles of the North for leave to commit his crime in front of them.

Communion with the raised — the forbidden act itself, the heart of the charge — performed here, now, publicly, the channel opened outward so that every linked senior in the bowl might read what the defendant read. The uproar lasted as long as uproars last under that canopy, which is not long. The eldest sat through it with her eyes shut, and everyone watched her have the argument with herself, and when she opened her eyes she stated its terms aloud with a fairness that cost her visibly: “You ask this court to acquit you of an act by commissioning it.” A breath. “The forest judges truths, not appearances. The court will feel this, or the court will sentence in ignorance, and I will not lead the second court. Do it where we can feel it.”

But before he opened the channel, @[Amyl](amyl) gave the court @[Triboar](triboar), as plainly as he owned it. The rat-hole under the town, and the vastness sleeping at the bottom of it. The little dried cap — he produced it from his robes and set it on the root-floor before the tribunal, an exhibit the size of a coin — grown from that darkness, and the doorway it had opened in him the night @[the Presence](the-quiet-thing) brushed three minds and left them ajar: his own; a sleeping boy's; and one of his oath-friends', here present — and @[Corvyn](corvyn) stood where he sat and inclined his head, confirming, and sat again. Amyl told them what he could remember of his years with his master, and told them honestly where the memory failed: a breadth in it he could not reach, a season of his own life that turned its face away when he looked at it directly — as though the why of him had been carried off and only the how left behind. And then, because the circles judge truths, he gave them his: “Whatever raised my master was not summoned by him and does not serve him. It is vast, and it is green, and it is held — harnessed, against its will, by the same hands that harvested a town, and made to work their rites. You felt one hour of its slack and called it feeding. And if what stands before you is abomination, then it is an enslaved one, and a gentle one — a third state between the living and the dead, which your law has no name for and my heart calls a mercy. I mean to see the thing that grants it freed.” The bowl took that in absolute silence, which is what courts do when a defendant raises the stakes on himself.

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=6
They cleared the bowl's floor to bare root. @[Amyl](amyl) crossed it alone and stood before his master, and asked him — asked, aloud, the way you ask a person — and the dead man's hands rose a little at his sides, palms opening, which the custodians' logs had no category for. The seniors knelt where they sat and laid their palms to the root-floor. Neth knelt with them. And Amyl took his master's hands and opened the door in himself that he had kept shut and shameful through every mile of this book, and turned it outward, and let the whole forest in.

What the court felt, in the order the court felt it. First: peace. Not absence of pain — the settledness of a made bed, of a tool cleaned and hung; what the dead man mostly was, under everything, was grateful, and sixty seniors of the circles learned it simultaneously and were not, all of them, equal to it dry-eyed. Second, beneath: an ache. Old, worn smooth with wearing — the sensation of harness. Somewhere far away something vast was bound and chafing, and the raised man hung from that vastness the way a lantern hangs from a beam, and the ache in the beam came down the rope; and the wearing on that wound was years deep — years — old beyond any rite worked at Triboar in autumn. The timeline arrived in sixty minds at once and quietly disassembled the prosecution. And third, last, the night itself: @[Harvest](the-harvest), re-felt from the inside. Not feeding. Slack. A harness slipping — the vast thing drawing one free breath in years — and the dead man blazing not with a meal but with relief: the hand that held him up had, for one hour, stopped hurting. Below all of it, at the channel's floor, @[something green and enormous and unnamed](the-quiet-thing) turned its attention upward toward the linked court, gently, the way a season turns — and some of the seniors snatched their hands from the roots, and some pressed them flatter, and both were correct.

The eldest's face was wet and her voice was dry. She did not make a speech. “The charge of Blight-work fails,” she said, and let the procedural thunder of that settle — and then she looked at the big man standing in the bowl still holding a dead man's hands, and delivered the sentence nobody had seen assembling: “Novice. You read that through a dead man across a hundred leagues of the world's rope. Read our Tree.”

---

@[Amyl](amyl) put his palms to the great root at the vault's heart and read, and was quiet a long time, and when he spoke his voice had the flat gentleness the party had learned to fear more than shouting. “Something is being done to this Tree,” he said. “It is being drunk. Every vein in the forest runs downhill to one place — patient, unhurried, thorough. Sickness wanders. This has intent in it.” He did not say whose. In that vault, over those columns, nobody needed to guess twice.

@unlock: the-grandfather-tree.2
They found the seat of it in the vault's deepest fold: a @[pin of black glass](the-glass), long as a forearm, seated in the heartwood with a jeweler's care — and the party knew the workmanship on sight, because they had seen it before, in collars and couplings across a buried god under Triboar. Tucked beside it in oiled cloth: tally-sheets in a devout, deliberately vague hand — the veins' widths and the blight's gains logged in neat columns by the tenday — and a @[scrap of liturgy](the-harvest) that thanked no god by name for the progress of the work, signed with no name either. Only a title, written out in full, the way its owner plainly always wrote it: —Certitude. The eldest read the scrap twice, and when she spoke it was to the vault as much as to anyone. “@[The Tree](the-grandfather-tree) holds a door shut. It is what the oldest living things are for.” She looked at the pin, seated in the heartwood like a thorn in a hand. “And this is how they are prying its grip loose.” Nobody said Triboar. Everybody heard it.

One more thing happened at the pin, and it happened in @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s hand. He had carried a knife since the early days of this record — @[obsidian](the-obsidian-knife), pried out of the jaw of a ruined bear under a town that no longer exists, kept because a gnome keeps his trophies — and standing at the heartwood he drew it, for no reason he could later give, and held it beside the seated pin. Night-glass, both. The same night-glass — the knife older-made and honester-shaped, a tool where the pin was an instrument, but the same black, drinking the lamplight the same way, kin the way the party was learning things in this story were kin: without explanation, and not by accident. Corvyn looked at it for a long moment, and put it away, and said nothing, and started wearing it on the outside of his belt.

@audio: door-threshold fade=2
It was when @[Amyl](amyl) closed his hand around the pin that the vault went thin. The air acquired a quality the party knew from a cave and an amphitheater — a membrane worn to tissue — and something came through it sideways, unfolding out of an angle the lamps could not account for: @[gaunt past reason](the-sorrowsworn), arms long by half a life, one limb ending in a barbed hook like a dockyard's grief, and a wail that was not a sound so much as a subtraction. Each cry made the vault larger. The lamps grew foreign. The distance between any hand and any other hand increased without anyone moving. And the creature rose over the party with the hook drawn back — and before Amyl, only before Amyl, it went still, and the hook lowered by one degree, exactly as something once had in a burning street in Triboar; and now, as then, there was no mistaking it: it knew him.

It took @[Corvyn](corvyn) first — the hook, the drag, the gnome hauled into the dark beyond the lamps with the vault's new distances yawning around him — and the fight that followed taught its one lesson brutally: steel passed through the thing like oars through fog; it could be hurt only in the moment of its taking, and it took whoever stood furthest from a friend. And it worked on you worst of all. This is your chapter, and the record turns to you now, because the vault had begun doing to you what the creature was: your friends' voices arriving as if across water; the lamplight becoming a coastline, receding; and up out of the old ground of you, rising like damp, the lesson your whole life had taught and your whole craft had argued against — that closeness ends. That everything is on loan. That the reaching itself is the mistake. And you knew — even from inside it, the way Sov had known in a crypt a week before — that this flood was not weather of your own making: something the gray had left in you was being found and played, string by string, your oldest grief amplified past nature, made ally to the thing that fed on it. The creature circled you with its hook down, singing your own conclusion at you in your own inner voice, and waited for you to agree.

You did the one thing a lonely thing cannot survive. You put your harpoon-logic down — all of it, the flinch, the ledger of losses, the habit of arm's length — and you walked into it with your arms open, willing. The embrace that shears the struggling closed on a willing man like a door coming home. It drank at the distance between you and found none; adjacency, chosen, starved it faster than any blade; and the wail turned over once into something almost like a question, and it stilled against your chest the way the burning street's creature had almost stilled — and then it came apart, not violently, like fog deciding against its shape, and the vault was just a chapel of roots again, and your friends' voices arrived at their true distance, which was close, which had always been close, which had never once in this whole book been the thing that was far away.

The verdict, when the bowl reconvened, took less time than its consequences. Cleared — and the words novice under watch struck from the rolls; and then the eldest, in front of sixty seniors and the whole gathered green North, offered @[Amyl Rachis](amyl) not reinstatement but a warden's standing, and a chair among the seniors whenever he should consent to fill it. The big man thanked them the way he thanked everyone, completely, and asked leave to earn the fit of it first — and the bowl, which had come to burn a heretic, laughed, and the laugh was the sound of a door opening somewhere in an institution. Neth was released from her minder's duty that evening and used her first free breath to request, formally, assignment as his second. “Someone must carry the cuttings,” she said, to her seniors' faces, daring them.

Of his master, @[Amyl](amyl) asked only time. Not mercy yet — he would not name the mercy question, and no one made him — time. What he told the party, later and quietly, was the thing he had not opened to the court: that whatever holds his master upright is holding something else, far away, against its will; that the rope has two ends; and that both debts will come due together. He said good night at the hazel gate. The dead man's stillness warmed, the way a dark window warms. It was all, and it was enough, for now.

That night the gray came for all seven of them, and the door it opened stood in morning fog and smelled of festival bread and wet stone. They stepped through together, unseen as always, into the fullest silence yet. This is what they watched.

## A Remembrance

*of Loneliness*

*— she is perhaps sixteen —*

*A market square on a festival day — the fullest place the gray has ever shown them, banners and stalls and a crowd shoulder to shoulder. And every soul in it is fog. Faceless, half-finished, smeared in motion: people rendered as weather with elbows. The party turns, looking for anything the memory bothered to keep, and finds it up the hill past the square — the graves. Every headstone crisp to the chisel-mark. Every patch of lichen. Every name legible, save one that slides.*

*@[The girl](the-girl-at-the-grave) — older now, the same stillness — moves through the festival, and the crowd parts around her without once looking at her. It is not dramatic. It is worse: a bubble of empty space, always the same radius, traveling with her like something she wears. A fog-mother draws a fog-child gently out of her path. A vendor takes her coin in a way that never lets their hands touch.*

*Once — only once — the fog produces a person. A peddler's boy angles toward her, and as he comes he comes into focus: freckles assembling, a chipped tooth, a real face, because for three steps she is paying attention, daring to. Then something — her hands, or something over her shoulder that the party never sees — turns him, and he smears back into the crowd mid-stride, features dissolving in retreat. The party watches a human being pass out of mattering, and understands that they are seeing the market the only way she kept it: as the place where that happened.*

*Up the hill, then, where the fidelity lives. It is her birthday; the little honey-cake says so, cut into many small pieces. She lays one piece on each of the nameless soldiers' graves, and here — among the stones — she becomes the most animated the party has ever seen her: gesturing, tilting her head, pausing as if listening, nodding at answers, laughing once, silently, at something no one said. She is hosting a party, and supplying both halves of every conversation at it, and she is visibly, unmistakably at ease — easier here than she was among the living below. That is the whole of it. No one weeps in this memory. The party does the weeping; that is what they are for.*

*She hums to the graves as she tidies the moss from them. Nothing crosses the silence. Her lips shape the notes, and the watchers supply the @[melody](the-tune) out of their own chests, each of them privately, all of them the same.*

*The memory closes on dusk: the girl lying down in the grass beside a mound, fitted against it the way a child fits against a sleeping parent's side, her breath slowing. The graves stay crisp to the last. The world stays fog.*
