# The Bell Under the Hill

@audio: threshold fade=4

The town of Harrow's Ferry kept one law older than its charter: nobody dug beneath the bell-hill. Children learned it before they learned their letters, chanted it skipping rope, forgot they had ever needed to learn it at all. The hill stood at the town's north edge, green and unremarkable, and under it — everyone agreed without ever saying so — was nothing worth the trouble.

@[Silda](silda) came to Harrow's Ferry on the last barge of autumn, carrying a satchel of blank ledgers and a letter of appointment nobody remembered sending for. She gave her name at the ferry office, paid for a room above the chandler's, and asked, in the mild voice of someone asking about the weather, who currently held the keys to the archive.

The clerk laughed. Harrow's Ferry had no archive. It had a damp cellar under the courthouse where the tax rolls went to rot.

"That will do to start," she said.

---

@audio: undercroft fade=6

The cellar was worse than promised. Water had gotten in some winter long past and made a single grey brick of an entire shelf of records. But at the back, behind a rack of broken chairs, Silda found a door that the courthouse plans did not show, and behind the door, stairs.

She stood a long moment at the top of them, listening. Later she would write in her ledger — the first entry of thousands — that the darkness below did not feel empty. It felt *catalogued*.

The stairs went down further than the hill was tall. That was her first note. The second was that the air, which should have been foul, was dry and cold and moved gently past her face, downward, as if the deep rooms were breathing in.

@unlock: undercroft.1

She named the place the @[Undercroft](undercroft), for lack of anything written on its lintel, and she went down.

---

@audio: vigil fade=3

Sixty-one steps below the cellar she found the first shelf, and on the shelf a single object: a hand-bell of black metal, no larger than a teacup, its clapper bound in wax-stiffened cloth. Someone had gone to great care to keep it from ringing. Someone had gone to greater care to keep it from being *found* — and had, for reasons Silda could not yet guess, failed to simply destroy it.

@unlock: bell.1

She did not touch it. She had been an archivist too long to touch the first thing an empty room offered her. Instead she noted its position, its binding, the fine grey dust that lay on the shelf everywhere except in a ring around the bell — as if the dust itself kept a respectful distance.

From somewhere far below, deeper than the stairs went, something answered her lantern-light with a sound. Not a ring. The *memory* of a ring, the shape a bell leaves in the air the moment after it has stopped.

@unlock: fundament.1

Silda wrote one more line in her ledger before climbing back to the light. *The hill is not a hill. It is a lid.*
