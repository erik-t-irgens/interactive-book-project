# XXIII. The Winter Door — The White Forest
*The Winter Door*

---

## Tirig

@image: images/tirig.jpg Tirig

@audio: seam-of-winter fade=6
She walked into the mountain with the vision for company, and the vision, which had replayed on every important morning for a year, replayed one last time as the passage swallowed the dawn behind them: the white forest, silver to the sky's edge; the two skies over it, one bright, one dark, sharing a heaven; the frozen lake; the ache, always the ache, arriving with the images the way weather arrives with wind. The passage breathed its cold against them, turned, descended, turned — and then the walls fell away, and the vision stopped replaying, because it was no longer a vision.

The white forest stood inside the mountain, or beyond it, or beneath it — the geometry declined every preposition the party tried on it. Silver-barked trees to the limit of sight, bare and blossoming at once, snow lying blue in the hollows. And overhead, exactly as the mirror had promised her: two skies. One bright — a pale gold winter daylight without a sun to blame for it — and beside it, sharing the same heaven along a seam like a watermark in paper, a second sky, blue-black and thick with stars. Neither yielded. Light fell doubled; every tree cast two shadows, one gold-edged and one silvered, aimed in different directions; and the party stood at the threshold for a long moment doing what everyone does at that threshold, which is checking each shadow against the other and finding both of them true.

The country was off, and it was off in ways that never once resolved into a rule. Their footprints filled behind them with snow that fell from no cloud. Distances lied — a tree an hour away arrived in ten steps; the lake, visible from the first ridge, took until the light changed to reach. Their breath-clouds drifted against the wind. Sounds came unmoored from their causes: a branch's crack arriving a heartbeat before the branch moved, laughter — or something shaped like it — crossing the forest with no throat anywhere. And @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s @[violin](corvyns-violin), cased and slung across his back, hummed. One string, untouched, faint as a held breath, all day — and he said nothing about it for hours, and then said only, “It is not out of tune,” in the voice of a man reporting a problem, “It has never been this in tune,” and put a hand on the case the way you quiet a child in church.

---

The forest began offering them things before noon of the first sky. A bush of red berries, impossibly ripe, steaming faintly in the snow, exactly at hand-height. A cloak of white fur hung waiting on a branch at the path's turn, cut — they could all see it — to @[Saeron](saeron)'s shoulders. A gold ring cased in clear ice at the lake-path's edge, sized, @[Aelendir](aelendir) observed without touching it, for precisely one finger present. Each gift was fitted to the hand that found it, and each was beautiful, and @[Gorvach's law](gorvach) stood over all of them like a warden: do not take. Anything. It counts. @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s fingers made it to within an inch of the berries — hunger and habit and a performer's faith in his own luck — before Neth's staff cracked across the path like a gavel, and he withdrew the hand and thanked her, later, twice.

It was @[Amyl](amyl) who found the country's other ledger. He stopped at the path's fork, took a cutting from his pack — one of the healed Tree's own, green as a promise — and planted it at the fork's foot without a word, tamping the snow like a man paying a toll he considered fair. The path on the far side of his gift stopped lying. They tested it the length of that day and it held: the forest counted both columns. @[Saeron](saeron) left a strip of crimson cord at a cairn. @[Aelendir](aelendir) sang eight bars to a leaning tree and left the melody there — they all heard the forest keep it, faintly, behind them, for an hour. And @[Sov](sov), at the lake path, stood the longest, and then took one @[black feather](the-raven-queen) from his breast pocket — one of hers, one of the collection, given away for the first time since the collecting began — and laid it on the snow, straight, the way they had always been laid for him. The distances stopped lying entirely after that, as though the country had counted them honest; and if the forest understood what that particular gift had cost, it kept the understanding to itself.

---

They reached the lake in the changing light, when the gold sky dimmed and the starred one leaned closer, and the lake was the vision's lake, and it was not empty. The ice ran clear as cathedral glass and deep past reason, and the deep was not dark. Something pale stood in it — stood, upright, at a depth no light should have reached: the suggestion of a woman, vast, her outline running into the ice the way a figure runs into fog, so that the eye could not say where @[the Lady](the-lady-of-winter) ended and the lake began, because — they understood it all at once, the way Gorvach's grammar had tried to teach them — there was no such border. She was not beneath the ice. The ice was the least of her.

Her voice arrived as cold against the cheek, and as frost, writing itself in quick crystal patterns across the surface at their feet, and it addressed one of them. You crossed my country once before, thief. @[Tirig](tirig) did not deny it. She had stopped denying things somewhere around a cell in Triboar, and the habit held. What the Lady told her, she told in the pared-down way of something that has had years to shorten its grief: that the @[Heart of this country](the-heart-of-winters-veil) had been carried out in secret, under guard, toward safety — for the keepers had felt hands on the world's other anchors, and would not wait to be robbed — and that a thief had lifted it out of the very convoy that was saving it. Out of the world entire. “I was paid,” Tirig said, because it was true and she owed the truth here. Frost crackled: something that might have been laughter with all the warmth removed. You were spent. Ask, someday, who paid you, in what coin, and what he truly bought. It was not the Heart.

What is a Heart, the party asked — Neth asked it, actually, warden-blunt, and the lake seemed to approve of the bluntness. It was not a jewel, the frost wrote. It was a kept feeling — old as my court, distilled through more winters than your line has names for — and it held this borderland closed the way a heart holds a body warm. And then, to @[Tirig](tirig), the patterns slowing, deliberate as a lesson: You carried it out against a sorrow the size of a life. Kept feelings are not inert, thief. They seek their vessels. Tirig stood very still at the lake's rim, in the doubled light, with the amulet cold in her fist and something enormous rearranging itself behind her face. Give back what you carry, the frost wrote at last. All of it. And we will both learn what that now means.

---

The socket stood at the forest's heart, and they heard it before they saw it. A bole of living silverwood, greater than the trees around it, rose in a clearing where both skies met almost directly overhead — and set into it at the height of a standing woman was an empty setting: a socket worn smooth by ages of holding, its edges polished by the slow pressure of something beloved. It sang. Faint, high, patient — the party heard it as a shimmer at the hearing's edge. @[Tirig](tirig) heard it in her teeth. And with every step she took toward it, the truth she had been outrunning since a mirror underground kept pace at her shoulder: the singing did not reach toward the amulet in her fist at all. It reached past it.

@audio: door-threshold fade=3
@[The Lost](the-sorrowsworn) came for her at the clearing's edge. The snow between the trees stood up wrong — gathered, elongated, wore too many arms — and what faced her had no face but a mouth, and the mouth was a question, and the question was the only one it knew: where. Its wail was not loud. It was thorough — the sound of every wrong turning ever taken, played at once — and under it the clearing's geometry broke: ten steps became a hundred, the party scattered across sudden distances, each of them alone in reach of everyone, shouting across gaps the forest invented as fast as they closed them. And the thing's arms found @[Tirig](tirig), and grasped, and began — gently, terribly, with the patience of drift — to draw her away from the socket, out toward the trackless white, in the direction that never arrives.

And the white began doing to you what the creature was. This is your chapter; the record turns to you now, at the clearing where it always meant to. Every silver tree became a door — you saw it happen — a door home, each one, to the house you left, the winters you left, the faces you left, all of them standing open at once and none of them reachable, and the old sweet ache rose over its banks and became the whole of the weather: the conviction, older in you than your name, that home is a thing that exists only over your shoulder. That leaving was the one true thing you ever did, and arriving is for other people. And you knew — in the drowned way Sov had known in a crypt, and Amyl in a vault — that this flood was being poured. Something the gray had left in you was being found and played, string by string, and the thing at the end of the arms was not dragging you. It was agreeing with you. It only ever walks people the way they were already going.

You did the one thing a lost thing cannot survive. You stopped. You planted your feet in the snow of the country you had robbed, in the reach of its grief, in front of the socket and the amulet's whole history — and you let the faces go. All of them, every borrowed face, every face that could be anyone — the record does not know what the party saw in that moment and the party has never once agreed on it, except that for a breath there was only one face, and it was yours — and you said your name. “I am Tirig.” The arms stilled. “I am not lost. I left. There is a difference, and I am done pretending there is not one.” And then the sentence that unmade it, the arrival it could not digest: “And I am exactly where I was going.” The Lost set you down the way a current sets down what it cannot pull under. The mouth closed around its question. And it came apart — not violently — like snow deciding, at last, to fall.

She set the @[amulet](the-heart-of-winters-veil) in the socket with both hands, the way you set a child in a cradle, and the record will report precisely what happened, because precision is the only honesty this moment permits: the socket took the husk the way a lock takes a copied key. Something turned. Something caught. Nothing sang. The seam between the skies steadied — the watermark calming, the doubled shadows settling into an agreement — and through the forest the party felt a loosening, like a held jaw unclenching: the country would hold, for a while, on what had been returned; the shell remembers the yolk. But the singing had not stopped. It had come closer. It was in her teeth now, and behind her sternum, patient as a second pulse — and across the clearing the frost wrote its last patterns up the silverwood, unhurried, for her alone. The socket is not singing to that. A pause, the crystals growing. It never was. You will keep better than I did, thief — when you consent to know what you are. Then, as the patterns faded: You gave back more than you know tonight. It counts.

They came out of the mountain into a war. The Veilmark camp was burning at its edges — the Hollow Tusks had come up the valley in force in the night, forty at least under a captain with a writ-case at his belt, and the camp's forty souls were scattering up the high paths with children on backs while the truce-scarred held the low ground with hunting spears. It was not a raid. It had paperwork. And at the cave mouth, exactly where the mouth's breath met the morning, @[Gorvach](gorvach) stood doing the only thing the party had ever seen him do, which was keep the gate.

The battle at the mouth was short and it was ugly and the record keeps its shape rather than its blows: the party coming out of the breath into the Tusks' flank, @[Volcaris](volcaris) finally pointed at something it approved of, @[Bayne](bayne) and the warden's staff and @[Tirig](tirig)'s bow buying the fleeing camp its minutes — and the arithmetic never once favoring them, because the dominion had sent enough, and the dominion always sends enough. The spear that ended it was thrown at the threshold itself — at the gap, at the gate, at whoever held it — and @[Gorvach](gorvach) took it the way he took everything, without surprise, stepping into its line because the gatekeeper's arithmetic has only one column: the door is worth more than the keeper. He went down against the cliff with the shaft standing in him, and he did not stop giving orders, which among his people the party would later learn is called dying well, and which the party declined, unanimously, without one word of discussion, to permit.

The retreat went the only direction left. Into the mouth — @[Amyl](amyl) carrying the orc like a bundled sail, @[Iratius](iratius) burning the threshold behind them, the Tusks' horns loud and then muffled and then wrong — because the passage was wrong. The breath had reversed. The cave that had exhaled since before the camp had a name was inhaling now, drawing them deeper with a pressure like a tide's decision, and the way back was longer than it had ever been, and the turns came in the wrong order, and the white forest did not arrive. What arrived instead was green.

They came out of the mountain into a country that had never heard of winter. Warm air, thick with growing; a night sky — one sky — crowded with too many colors of star; hills of flowering turf rolling away under a moon that was frankly showing off. And behind them: no mountain. No mouth. A low green hillside, doorless, seamless, humming faintly with insects, as if the passage had closed its accounts and filed them. The Tusks did not follow. Nothing followed. They were, in every direction they could name and several they couldn't, somewhere else entirely — and @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s violin was not humming anymore. It was, very quietly, in its case, playing.

@audio: remembrance-silent fade=8
They made camp because there was nothing else to make. @[Saeron](saeron) and Neth worked on @[Gorvach](gorvach) through the first hours — the spear had missed what it wanted, Saeron judged, and the orc would keep till morning, if morning here meant anything — and the last thing Gorvach said before the healers' draught took him was a question, flat and literal and aimed at the impossible sky: “Whose country?” Nobody answered, because nobody knew, and he nodded once, as though that too were information, and slept. And in the smallest hour of that first strange night, the gray came for the seven of them — the seven exactly, as it has always been exact; Gorvach slept on beside them like a stone honestly earned, and no door opened for him, and the party was obscurely glad of it — and the door it opened was small, and warm, and smelled of woodshavings and lamp oil. This is what they watched.

## A Remembrance

*of Melancholy*

*— she is perhaps thirteen —*

*The gray parts on a small room. A hearth, one chair, one narrow bed, winter pressing at the shutter — and the memory keeps its accounts openly: the hearthstones are rendered down to their cracks, the little carving-knife on the mantel is true enough to cut, and the walls are only the idea of walls. In the chair by the fire sits a shape made of warmth. It has an outline, and hands, and a lap, and no face at all — a mother kept the way heat is kept, long after the particulars have gone.*

*@[The girl](the-girl-at-the-grave) sits on the floor at the shape's feet. The shape's mouth — the smudge where a mouth should be — is moving, telling something, and the girl mouths along with it word for word, in perfect time. The party can hear nothing. They do not need to. This is a story told so many nights it has worn a channel, and the girl is keeping to the channel the way water does.*

*The scene ends, and begins again. The second time through, the shape in the chair is dimmer, and the girl's lips run a half-beat ahead of its smudged mouth — she is leading now, and the memory of her mother is following. The third time, the chair holds only firelight. The girl tells the story alone, to no one, and stops in the middle of a word. The party watches her face do a small, terrible arithmetic: the voice that tells the stories in her head has become her own voice, and she cannot say when the exchange was made, and there is no one left to ask.*

*She takes the plank from beside the hearth, and the knife from the mantel, and begins to carve. Words — small, dense, urgent, line upon line, shavings curling to the floor. And as she works, her lips move to a different rhythm: @[three rising notes](the-tune), over and over, hummed the way a child hums to keep the dark honest. No sound crosses the gray. Every one of the watchers knows the three notes anyway. They have always known them.*

*They lean close to read what she is carving, and cannot. The letters are whole, and plain, and there — and they slide from the eye the way a name slid from a gravestone in a brighter country, long ago or not yet. Whatever she is writing down to keep, the world does not permit it kept. She does not know that yet. She carves like someone building a levee one spoonful at a time.*

*The memory closes on dawn-gray: the girl asleep against the cold hearth, the plank hugged to her chest, the knife still folded in her fist. In the chair, nothing — rendered so carefully, so faithfully nothing, that the nothing is its own kind of portrait. The gray takes the room gently, and the party is elsewhere.*
