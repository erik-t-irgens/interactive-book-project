# Foreword
*On what this book is, and why it exists*

@audio: epilogue-morning fade=10
If you are reading this, then the table has gone quiet — not for a night, but for good, or at least for now. That is not how I wanted it to end. We were partway up a mountain; I had drawn the far side of it and everything waiting there. What follows is my attempt to walk you the rest of the way, even if we cannot roll for it.

I want to be honest with you about something before you turn another page. You never saw the whole of what you were inside of. That was by design — a good mystery keeps its own counsel — but it means that a great deal of what you did carried weight you had no way to feel at the time. A throwaway line was sometimes a load-bearing wall. A dream you shrugged off was the truest thing said all evening. You were closer to the center than you ever knew.

So this book does two things at once. First, it fills in what you lived through — the campaign as you experienced it, up through the night the masks came off at Boar's Rest — but restored to its full shape, including the parts each of you saw alone and never shared aloud. You will find your private visions here, set beside one another for the first time. Read them together. They were always meant to be read together.

*And second — slowly, and in the right order — it shows you the rest. The far side of the mountain. What was true underneath the town, underneath the war, underneath your own missing years. I will not spend it all at once. Some things have to be arrived at, or they mean nothing. But by the end you will know what I knew, and I think you will understand why I could not simply tell you.*

*There is one more thing woven through all of it, and I will name it here only so that you know to watch for it. You will remember the errands — the small troubles of Triboar and the roads beyond it. A child lost beneath a ruined house. A prisoner from the North. A grief in a forest. A pact, a betrayal, an old accident that would not stay buried. They arrived looking like the ordinary work of adventurers: unrelated, incidental, the sort of thing a party takes on because someone has to. They were none of those things. Each of them had a name on it before you ever heard of it, and the name was one of yours. Not shared among you — assigned. I built a door for each of you and disguised every one of them as a task for everybody. You will see, as you read, the moment each door was meant to open. I will not point at them from up here; half the pleasure is the recognition, and you have earned the right to feel it land on your own. But know that it is there, and that it was there from the first night, and that nothing you did in this world was ever only errand.*

---

So let me begin the way the world itself begins, which is to say: in the middle, with the ink already dry.

This is Faerûn in the year 1492 by Dalereckoning, twelve years after the Second Sundering tore Toril from its twin and the overgod remade the Tablets of Fate. Magic has returned, unruly and bright. The gods walk close to the world again and choose their champions from among the living. It is, by every reckoning of scholars and kings, an age of renewal — a morning after a long dark.

*But there is an older law beneath the new one, and it is this: nothing is ever truly renewed. It is only carried. The dead are carried into memory. Memory is carried into grief. Grief, left too long in the cold, hardens into something that will not let go. There is a place where all of it collects — a gray country on the far side of a thin veil, where the forgotten go to be kept by a keeper who has herself been forgotten.*

You began your story a very long way from all of that — or so it seemed. You woke in Triboar, a frontier town at the edge of the map, with the harvest coming and refugees at the gates and something wrong in the water that no one wanted to name. You woke, each of you, with a hole where a piece of your life should have been. And you woke knowing one another the way twins know one another across a room: without introduction, without explanation, with a certainty that had no memory attached to it.

*You told yourselves it was coincidence. Strangers thrown together, as strangers are, by a town in trouble. You were wrong, and the wrongness of it is the whole of this book. But I will let you keep the comfort of that error a little longer, because you kept it so well for so long, and because the slow unlearning of it is the truest thing you ever did at my table. Memory. The thinning of the veil. A love that outlived the one who carried it. Hold those three things loosely as you read. They are the ground you have been standing on the entire time — you simply could not see the floor.*

---

A few words on how to read what follows, and then I will get out of the way.

Everything up to and including the eighth night — the masquerade, the ledger, the fiend who swore himself your enemy — is true and settled. It happened; you were there; I have only restored the parts you could not see. After that, the road turns into country we never walked together, and I have built it here from my own notes and drafts. Take that latter half as an offering rather than a decree. It is the campaign as I intended to run it, not as we proved it in play, and intentions are softer things than history. Where you would have surprised me — and you always did — imagine that you have.

You will not find my notes inside the chapters. I tried it that way first, and I pointed too much. They live at the back of the book now, collected chapter by chapter behind the appendices' door — and they assume you have finished, and they speak plainly, and they will spoil everything they touch. Read the story clean. Then, if you want to know how the trick was done, come find me behind the screen.

*Thank you for the seasons, and the dice, and the hours you gave to a place that only ever existed because we agreed together to believe in it. It was the best use I have ever made of a weekend. Turn the page whenever you are ready. She has been waiting a long time to be remembered — and so, in their own way, have you.*

*— Your Dungeon Master*
