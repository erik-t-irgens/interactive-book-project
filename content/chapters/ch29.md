# XXIX. The Cord Door — The Feast of the Moon
*The Cord Door*

## Saeron

@image: images/saeron.jpg Saeron Redcord

@audio: nesme-lantern fade=6
The Feast of the Moon came to @[Nesmé](nesme) at dusk with every window lit — hundreds of small flames bound to hundreds of names by small correct knots — and the moon came up over the moors fat and white and, exactly as a small even hand had ordered, early. The party stood their bought posts through the first bells with the town's gladness moving beneath them: processions to the graveyard with candles cupped against the wind, names spoken over thresholds, the year's dead being fed and remembered in the old way, a town doing the thing the Feast is for — and doing it, unknowing, over a cellar where a pane of night-glass hung curing toward the exact hour when the veil between the living and the dead wears thinnest of all the year. The strike was set for the second bell, one hour shy of moonrise. @[Saeron](saeron)'s door, the record notes here, was one of two in this party the mirror never showed — he had not yet come to the table when the mirror did its showing — and it did not matter, and it never had: his vision had been on paper all along, folded, carried, and tonight, at last, arrived before the curtain.

They went down into the wool-house at the second bell, in the order the plan had drilled, and the record gives the order slowly, because it was built with care and deserves to be seen whole. @[Darz](darz) went first, for his ear — “Louder. Down. Left at the wool-bales. It knows we're coming; it doesn't care” — a lantern in one hand and nothing in the other, having refused a weapon on the grounds that he would not know which end to hold. Behind him went the breakers: @[Iratius](iratius) and @[Gorvach](gorvach), mauls slung across their backs, whose entire assignment was the glass — the glass and nothing else, whatever else the room might offer. Behind the breakers came the wall: everyone else, blades and staves and Amyl's staff, whose assignment was everything that was not the glass. @[Osric](bettany-and-osric) moved at his brother's shoulder in his monk's economy, asking nothing, matching him step for step. And Bettany took the stair-head above with the signal-lamp — the post she had chosen herself, and held: she had crossed nine years and a moor to be present, and no argument on the continent was moving her one step further from it, or one step nearer.

@audio: mirror-glass fade=4
The cold room opened below like a held breath. The cistern had been stripped to its bare bones and swept clean — no stores, no tools, no clutter; a cleanliness that was somehow the worst sign yet — and in the bare heart of it hung the mirror. Nothing held it. No frame, no cradle, no rope, no scaffold: a pane of grown black glass the size of a barn door, standing on the air as though the air had agreed to it, frost-lit from within, humming at the edge of hearing. It needed no earthly pity, and was not asking for any. And where its light lay along the old stone, the stone moved. Slowly. The walls and floor of the cold room ran with a ripple the party had seen exactly once before in their lives — in a cave under Triboar, around a stone that should not have been — rock behaving like the memory of water, the whole room breathing at the pace of something asleep.

Around it the quiet crew turned from their work; and the two who carried nothing lowered their hoods; and one of them said, in the reasonable voice of the ash-marked everywhere, “You are early.” Then everything happened at once, because the other one had already begun to sing.

@audio: glass-rise fade=2
The working woke ahead of its hour. That was the adepts' contingency and the night's first cost: at the first blade drawn, the singer tore the schedule and spent the working's stored season all at once — the pane flaring from black to a deep and somehow wet gray, the hum climbing to a note the teeth hated — and the veil-thin night, generous to all workings alike, lent it strength. The glass did not open. But it reached. And what its reaching pulled up into the cold room came in waves out of the frost and the seams and the party's own shadows: @[the Wretched](the-sorrowsworn) — the gray's orphaned misery given mass, things like crowds compressed into bodies, too many hands, faces that were only need, moving in masses that broke and reformed like water — wave upon wave, drawn up the thin night's rigging toward the brightest grief in the room. The party formed the ring they had drilled. The mirror-breakers went for the glass. And then, in the middle of the fight, with no warning and every warning, @[Saeron](saeron)'s door opened.

@audio: remembrance-silent fade=4
The gray did not wait for sleep. Perhaps it could not — the veil that thin, the shard that ready, the man standing at last in the corrected version of his oldest night — and between one wave and the next the air in the cold room parted, and @[the Remembrance began to play](the-remembrances). There. Mid-battle. A lantern-show nobody could stop: white void, the blizzard, the rope-line of human knots leaning into wind, her moving down the line with bare hands — the whole silent film unspooling in the sleeting air above the frost, casting its pale light on the fight below. And the Wretched turned. All of them, every wave, mid-crest — turned from the living toward the playing memory like starving things shown a laid table, because here was empathy itself, kept and shining, the one feeling their whole nature had been orphaned from — and the party understood in a single collective heartbeat what the rules of the next ten minutes were. The memory could be reached. The memory could be marred — where a gray hand clawed through its light, the film dimmed and fogged, fidelity bleeding — and it was playing, and it could not be paused, and it had to finish. “Defend it,” @[Saeron](saeron) said — the first battlefield order of his recorded life — and the party re-formed around a memory the way walls re-form around a heart.

@unlock: saeron.4
What followed, the record can only give as arithmetic under fire, because that is what it was. The waves came keyed, uncannily, to the film's own beats — cresting each time she reached a new knot on the rope, as though her tenderness were the tide-pull — and @[Saeron](saeron) stood at the ring's center doing the thing his whole nature was built to refuse: choosing. Cover the memory's east light, and the stair-head thins. Reinforce the breakers at the glass, and the film's edge takes claws. Send @[Amyl](amyl) to the stair and lose the ring's anchor; hold the ring and hear Bettany's lamp swing wild above. Every round of that fight was a triage, and every triage crossed his oldest law — refuse no one, carry everyone, take the nearest station and hold it — and the flood came up in him accordingly, not lit but rising, a tide of everyone at once, every need in the room arriving in him as his own, the shard the gray had left in him thrown wide open by its own kin. And he knew what it wanted. It wanted him to try to carry all of it. It wanted the fire-line again, the nearest station, the whole train held unrelieved until he died of being needed — and the man who had taught an orc that words stay, and a warden to set it down, stood in the crush of the wanting and did the opposite of everything. He delegated. He chose. Out loud, name by name, round by round — Amyl the east light; @[Iratius](iratius) the glass; @[Tirig](tirig) where she judges; @[Gorvach](gorvach) the stair — spending his friends like a man who had finally, terribly, accepted that the rope holds because it is a rope, and not because one knot carries the line. It was never yours to carry alone. He had said it to a dying woman at a wall of thorns. The night made him mean it.

---

The last wave split. The record will keep this part plain, because it promised itself no frill anywhere near it. One prong went for the memory's unguarded west edge. One went up the stair — at the lamp, at Bettany, at Darz and the fleeing watch. @[Saeron](saeron), mid-order, mid-word, chose the stair, because the stair held the living. And the west light of the memory stood open. One heartbeat. A crest of gray hands rising into it. @[Sov](sov) was already there. Nobody saw him decide, because there was nothing to see; nobody saw him cross the room, though he must have. He was simply in the gap, as though the gap had opened around a man already standing in it — feet set shoulder-wide on the wet stone, both hands on @[Bayne](bayne) for the first time in anyone's memory of him, the blade angled low and patient, the film's pale snow falling through him and dressing his shoulders white. He had been standing in gaps, undecided, his whole quiet life. This one he filled on purpose.

He held it. The crest broke on him and did not pass. Bayne moved the way it had moved once in a crypt — a temple blade, believed in — and the gray want parted around one decided man, and behind his back the memory played on untouched, her bare hands moving down the rope. He held it while the breakers did their one assignment: @[Iratius](iratius) and @[Gorvach](gorvach), shoulder to shoulder at the pane's edge, swinging on the count Saeron had drilled into them. One — together, full from the shoulder. Two — and the black surface starred under the maul-heads like ice on a well, and then did the thing the survivors describe first, always, before any other part of this night: it filled with Sov. Every facet of the starring glass, wall to wall of the dying mirror — not the room, not the breakers mid-swing, not the memory playing at its back — Sov, only Sov, Sov from every angle at once, a hundred of him standing in the gap with the blade angled low, as though the mirror had gathered every last coin of its attention and spent it all looking at one man. Gorvach says he shouted a warning. Nobody heard a warning. Three — — and the mirror died. A sound like a frozen year breaking up; and it did not burst. It threw. The whole failing pane went in one direction only, its death aimed where its last attention had been aimed, and a shard the length of a boar-spear took @[Sov Thimron](sov) through the chest, and stood him half a step back, and did not put him down. He could have stepped aside. There had been a whole breath in which the glass showed everyone exactly where its dying was going. He had finished, forever, with stepping aside. The room went white, then lamplit. And Sov Thimron kept his feet. He looked down at himself, once, unhurried — at the black length standing out of his chest, at the dark already traveling its edge, at the blade still level in his two hands beneath it — the look of a craftsman finding the one flaw that condemns the work, and accepting the finding without appeal. Then he looked up, across the room, through the settling frost, and found them. Each of them. One at a time, without hurry, the way he tied knots. Then he lowered Bayne; and the shard went from him as the glass let him go, the way a thing lets go that has finished what it was for; and he walked the two steps to the cistern wall — walked them; the record wants that verb challenged and will not allow it, because those present will not have it softened — and sat down against the stone, sitting the way a man sits at the end of a long day, and laid the blade across his knees, and when they reached him, running, his face was doing the thing none of them had ever seen it do. It had decided. All of it. One direction. No remainder. “It was easy,” he told them, sounding surprised, and pleased, and younger than any of them had ever heard him. And then he was not looking at them anymore, but past them, upward, at something arriving that the shadows leaned toward like grass, and the cold room filled, from no direction at all, with the smell of snow.

@[The feathers came down](the-raven-queen) like the film's own weather escaping its frame. Black, unhurried, impossibly many, falling through the lamplight and the memory's pale glow alike — and no figure that any account agrees on, only a darkness at the room's heart with a shape to it, patient, vast, folded — and the party stood inside the falling and could not move, not held, simply unable, the way sleep cannot be argued with; and when the feathers finished falling and the room was only a room again, the stones where he had sat held one thing: @[Bayne](bayne), laid straight, blade and worn sheath together, the way he set things down for other people to find. No Sov. No coat, no boots, no body borne away nor left — nothing else beneath the settling black but the floor, and Bayne, laid straight on the stones. Laid straight, and exactly, the way feathers had been laid for him on sills and snow-crusts across the whole length of this book. The collection, complete. The courtship, answered — or claimed. And the Remembrance above them, in the settled hush, played out its final length: her forehead to the last man on the rope, her lips moving on the sentence, and @[Saeron](saeron) — kneeling at the bare floor with his brother's hand on his shoulder and his whole ring of the living safe around him — read her lips aloud for the record, because someone had to, and it was his door, and his voice did not survive the middle of it: “I will never let you be lost.” The film closed like a hand closing. The gray took its light home. And the Feast of the Moon, outside, over their heads, rang its third bell over a town that was going to wake up.

The shard lay in the wreck where the letting-go had dropped it: a boar-spear of black glass, its point and a hand's-breadth of its length gone dark, drying. Nobody touched it. Everybody measured it. There is a kind of arithmetic the eye does against the will's whole strength, and the party did that arithmetic all night, separately, in silence, and every one of them reached the same total, and the total is the reason this record uses plain words for the rest of this chapter.

@audio: nesme-lantern fade=8
@[Nesmé](nesme) stood. The record sets the fact down in two words because the party could not, for a long while, set it down at all: the mirror in shards, the working dead ahead of its hour, the quiet crew broken and the ash-marked singers fallen or fled — and above it all, dawn on the Feast's morrow arriving over a whole town: bells for the morning rites, smoke from breakfast fires, children in the lanes comparing candle-stubs, twenty-five hundred people waking unharvested into a day they would never know had been scheduled otherwise. It was the first one. The record counts on its fingers, deliberately, in front of you: @[Triboar](triboar), lost. The Tree, saved by another's fire. Nesmé — Nesmé the party saved, outright, first of the as-yet uncounted wins of this war, and the room where they won it held eight of the living and an absence the exact shape of a quiet man, and nobody said the word victory, and nobody has said it since.

It came through the glass. A hand's-width shard of the mirror had survived whole against the cistern wall, black and dead as any window at night — and while the party stood in the wreckage, it fogged. Once. From the inside. The way a pane fogs when someone breathes on it from a room that is not there. When the fog faded, a note lay beneath the shard, square and dry, as though it had been slid under the world's smallest door. The hand was the folded page's hand, the satchel's hand — square, devout, deliberate, unhurried — and it was addressed to no one, which is to say addressed: There you are. The next table is already laid. —@[Verity](the-harvest). That was all. No second hand beneath it. The party stood in a slow half-circle and read it more times than any of them afterward admitted, and the year rearranged itself around them: the order behind it all had never once, in two seasons, spoken to them — around them, over them, through rumors and writs — other men's sealed orders — and other people's mouths — and now, on the morning of its first defeat, it had turned, and looked, and made them a promise. @[Aelendir](aelendir) said the useful thing, eventually, folding the note away for @[Aevren](aevren)'s files with two fingers, like evidence, like a dead wasp: “Good.” And did not elaborate, and did not need to.

The accountings of that morning, briefly, because grief does its bookkeeping in small entries. @[Iratius](iratius) stood in the dawn a man in open breach — his deadline had died at midnight with the working, unmarked, unremarked — alive and uncollected, which he understood to be a cliff rather than a pardon, and stood on it without flinching, because the night had reordered what frightened him. @[Bettany](bettany-and-osric) came down the stair when it was over and found @[Saeron](saeron) at the bare floor, and stood over him a while, and delivered the verdict nine years had owed: “I decided,” she said. “What I saw you do. Both times.” She did not say the verdict aloud. She sat down next to him instead, which was the verdict. @[Darz](darz) walked the broken cold room with his head tilted, listening, and reported the silence like a man reporting a sunrise: “It's stopped. First town I've stood in since autumn where nothing is being set.” @[Gorvach](gorvach) took his knife to the stair-post of the wool-house, and carved — slowly, correctly, in the letters a monk had taught him on a shield — his second word. The party found it there in the full morning light and left it for Nesmé to puzzle over for a generation: KEEP. And @[Amyl](amyl) wrapped @[Bayne](bayne) in Sov's spare oilcloth, wound twice, the way its owner had always wrapped it — the way you carry a thing you mean to return — and stowed it across his own back, and nobody said for whom, because everybody said for whom, silently, all morning, in the manner of a decision assembling itself without a single word being spent on it.

Because here is where Part Two of this record ends, and the record will not pretend to a suspense it does not feel. They stood at a bare patch of floor where their friend had been filed — taken whole, body and blade-less, into a country they had refused at its own door a season ago, by a collector who had been laying feathers on his sills since long before any of them understood what was being priced — and the arithmetic of what came next did not require the map table, or the council, or one spoken proposal. It required exactly one sentence, and @[Tirig](tirig) provided it — Tirig, the thief, the Keeper, the woman who had learned this year what it means to give something back — standing at the empty floor with one hand at a throat that has been bare since the Spine — the old habit still checking for what holds a door open half a world away, saying it flatly, to all of them, in the voice she kept for exact things: “We know where things are kept.” And the party looked at one another over the bare stones, eight of the living and one appointment, and the Feast bells rang the morning in over Nesmé, and somewhere north past every map in the room, a gray country adjusted its shelving.

@audio: remembrance-silent fade=8
One more thing belongs to this chapter, and then the record will do what the night could not. The memory that played over the battle — marred at its edges, defended at its heart, finished under falling feathers — deserved better than to be watched in pieces between waves, and the record has the power to give it better, and intends to use it. Here it is again, whole, uninterrupted, beginning to end, exactly as it wanted to play. She earned that. So, @[Saeron Redcord](saeron), did you.

## A Remembrance

*of Empathy*

*— she is perhaps five-and-forty —*

*White. A blizzard rendered as a void with weather in it — and through the void, a single line of people roped together at the waist, a dark cord strung with human knots, leaning into the wind. The landscape is nothing; the memory spent nothing on it. Every face on the rope is perfect. All of them kept, every one, the way joy kept the wall-raisers — but these faces are hollow, frost-burned, spent, and the keeping feels less like love than like inventory. Like someone counting what she has left.*

*@[She moves](the-girl-at-the-grave) down the line against the wind, unroped herself, checking each knot with bare hands. And the party sees what her gift has grown into. When a young woman on the line weeps, tears cross her face too — a half-beat later, the same track down the same side. When the old man at the line's end shudders, she shudders after him, the identical rhythm, a body taking dictation. She is not watching their suffering. She is having it, serially, one soul at a time, and she does not once look away, and the party begins to understand that this is not a power. It is a door she cannot close.*

*They shelter under a rock overhang while the white erases the world. The old man is failing — and she holds him, and his fear plays across her face in real time, his terror worn openly on her features while his own face keeps the stillness of the very tired. She is being two people at once, the dying and the witness, because someone must feel this and he no longer can. It is the most generous thing the party has ever watched anyone do. It is also, they can see plainly, eating her alive.*

*Then the vow. She presses her forehead to the old man's and mouths a sentence against the wind. Then moves to the next on the rope, and mouths it again. And the next. By the fourth, the party can read it whole — I will never let you be lost — and by the seventh they are mouthing it with her, all of them, involuntarily, and each will privately carry the question of whether that was witness or complicity for a long time afterward.*

@unlock: the-tune.3
*She steadies the line the only way left: hand over hand down the cord, humming into it — the @[three rising notes](the-tune), the old channel — and though no sound survives the wind, the party watches the song travel the rope like a current, roped shoulders dropping one by one as it passes through them, fear conducted away down a string.*

*The storm thins, and the memory closes on the counting. She stands aside and touches each survivor on the shoulder as they pass, her lips shaping every name — and the line is short. The party is never told what it started as; the fidelity of each kept face tells them. Last of all, she ties the rope's free end around her own waist, takes the rear, and stands a moment looking back the way they came, into the void where pursuit would be — a woman lashing herself to everything she has not yet lost. Then the white takes it, and the gray takes the white.*
