# XXVII. The Ember Door — The Barrow
*The Ember Door*

---

## Iratius

@image: images/iratius.jpg Iratius

@audio: door-threshold fade=6
Six days to the Feast, and the barrows took them in the way old battlefields take everyone: quietly, and on their own terms. Two armies had disagreed here once, to the last man, long enough ago that the moor had eaten the names and kept only the shapes — mounds in ranks under smooth turf, ditch-lines softened to suggestions, here and there a standing stone worn blank as an old coin — and the ground had that stillness that is not peace, the stillness of a room where the argument has merely paused. The party walked it in single file without anyone ordering single file. And on @[Iratius](iratius)'s tongue, all that gray afternoon, a word kept stirring — the word, the one a mirror had put there seasons ago with no place and no face attached, the one he had quietly nailed to his own sword like a man labeling a wound. @[Volcaris](volcaris). It sat up in his mouth at every mound they passed, tasting of smoke, and he walked with his jaw set against it and told no one, which the party could tell, because his silences had corners.

@audio: none fade=5
The smoke found them at dusk — one thread of it, peat-gray, rising from a fold between mounds — and they came over the last rank of the dead and looked down at a small fire, a tinker's wagon patched past its hundredth year, one hobbled mule, and @[a man sitting on a barrow-stone](the-mark) with his hands open on his knees, watching them arrive as though he had ordered them delivered. Gray-faced. Older than his years, and his years were not few; something about him suggested a house with the hearth swept out. The sword in @[Iratius](iratius)'s scabbard was silent now the way held breath is silent. The man looked up the slope, found Iratius without hunting for him, and said, in a voice like the moor: “You're the sword. I wondered which of them it would send.”

What happened next the record will give as it went, because @[Iratius](iratius) crossed that fold with fifteen years drawn and leveled, and the man on the stone did not move, and what stopped the blade a hand's width from his throat was not mercy. It was the man's absolute stillness — the stillness of someone with nothing left that steel can reach — and the question he asked into the space above the edge: “Before you collect, soldier. Do you know what it is you're collecting? Or have you carried the invoice fifteen years without once reading it?”

So @[the mark](the-mark) read it to him, there, at blade-length, in the peat-smoke, and the party stood in a ring and heard the shape of @[Iratius](iratius)'s life rearranged. Fifteen years ago: a prisoner transport across the low country — the prisoner, himself; the escort, a band of pact-bound men, warlocks every one, in the service of a devil they had come to want out of. “They did not transport me,” the man said. “They transported my soul out of me. In a lead box, on the second wagon. Payment. A soul of particular vintage buys a great deal of freedom, and they were buying theirs.” And the ambush — the ambush that had made a boy an orphan and an avenger in one hour of fire — had been the escort's own hand: a desertion staged as a massacre, deaths faked in smoke, the box gone north while the county buried its dead and blamed the man whose soul was in it. “Your people were not the target, boy,” he said, and said it flat, which was somehow the kindest available way. “They were the tinder. Men needed a fire to disappear in, and your family was standing where the fire needed to be.”

Iratius did not lower the sword. His voice, when it came, was a stranger's: “Name the devil.” And the mark looked at the blade's fuller, at the banked fire in it, with the expression of a man reading a signature he knows, and named him: “@[Veyran](veyran).” The word went around the ring of the party like cold water finding low ground. A masquerade rearranged itself in eight memories at once — a sommelier's smile, a parting gift, more than one target remains — and the mark, watching their faces, added the codicil that cost @[Tirig](tirig) the most, though she never said so: “You have met his web, then. Brokers. Intermediaries. Pleasant men at parties. He staffs it with people who owe him, and he pays” — the gray face almost smiled — “in exactly the coin you dream in.” Tirig said nothing. Tirig then continued saying nothing in a way the party noticed, and filed, and did not touch.

@audio: mirror-glass fade=3
It was at the devil's name that @[the sword cracked](volcaris). Not broke — cracked: a hairline opening down the fuller with a sound like a frozen lake deciding, light bleeding up through it the color of banked coals — and out of the crack, one syllable, one only, in a voice @[Iratius](iratius) had not heard in fifteen years and would have known in fifteen hundred: his name. Not the sword's name. His. The blade shook in his grip — the record declines to say whether the shaking began in the steel or the hand — and the mark watched the light play up the fuller and delivered, gently, the worst sentence of the evening: “Ask what your fire runs on, soldier. Ask what everything of his runs on. He was never a generous devil. He is an efficient one.”

@[The Angry](the-sorrowsworn) came up out of the old blood while the word was still ringing — barrow-ground, standoff, fifteen years of banked heat: it could no more have stayed away than water can stay uphill. It rose between two mounds like a wound standing up: red weals and backwards joints, too many elbows, a face that was all clench — and it grew. That was the lesson the first exchanges taught, at the usual tuition: @[Iratius](iratius)'s flame washed it and it broadened; a furious blow swelled it like bellows-work; rage was not its weakness, rage was its diet, and the harder the party burned the bigger the argument got. It was @[Gorvach](gorvach) who found the grammar, because of course it was — Gorvach, who fought the way he spoke, moved in flat and economical and bored, and where his spear struck without heat — no anger in the jab, a tradesman's stroke, timber to be shifted — the thing flinched and did not grow. “Like moving furniture,” he reported, mid-fight, literally. And the party cooled by degrees, blow by leveled blow — all but one of them.

Because it wanted you, standard-bearer, and this is your chapter, and the record turns to you in the smoke. The heat climbing you was ladled, not lit — something the gray had banked in you long before your fires were yours being stoked now, coal by deliberate coal, by a hand that was not yours — and the thing between the mounds was not herding you away from the mark. It was herding you toward him. Every backwards joint bent that way; every clench of its face opened that way; the whole of the night funneled toward the gray man at the fire with his hands open on his knees — and your arithmetic, your real arithmetic, sat where it had sat all evening: the kill would feed the thing; the kill would fire the ritual and release the payment; the kill would meet the deadline, clean, tonight, with five days' grace; and the kill would put one more soul of particular vintage into an efficient devil's stores — and it was offered, all of it, at the exact moment it became unbearable to refuse, which is how you knew, coal-bright and certain, who had set the table.

You fought cold. That is the whole of it, and the record will not dress it: you shut the furnace door on fifteen years, and you leveled your blade like a man closing a ledger, and you walked through the press of the thing's want — it clawed at you and found no heat to hold — to stand over the mark, close enough to finish it, close enough that no one could have stopped you. And then you did the one thing his whole web is built never to survive. You looked at him. Not at the invoice; at the man — the swept-out hearth of him, the fifteen soulless winters, the stillness that was not courage but the far side of loss — you looked, and you kept looking, the full minute, the way almost no one in this story has managed to keep looking at anything, and what you said when you finally said it was quiet enough that the Angry had to strain: “I am owed a debt.” A breath. “Not by you.” And then, lifting your face to the dark beyond the fire, to the direction webs come from: “Collect it yourself.”

The Angry starved standing up. It reached for the strike that was not coming, and reached, and its reaching found nothing banked anywhere in you to stoke — and it went out of the world the way a bellows goes when the seam is cut: a long exhale, a folding, the red weals graying, down and down into the old blood that had lent it, which took it back without comment, being long practiced. The barrows resumed their stillness. It was, the party agreed afterward, the only fight any of them had ever won by declining to have it.

@audio: none fade=6
Nothing marked the breach. That wants saying plainly, because everyone at that fire braced for thunder: the deadline stood five days out, the delivery was now never coming, and somewhere — in a study, in a cellar, in whatever room efficiency keeps — a clause quietly changed state, and the moor did not flicker, and the peat-smoke rose straight. That is how it is with contracts. The thunder is filed for later. The mark packed his wagon in the morning with a tradesman's economy and paid his fare in the only coins he had. To the party, roads: what was moving on the moor, and toward where — “Buy your assurances, travelers; the moor towns are buying theirs. @[Nesmé](nesme) most of all. Wall-coin. Blade-coin. And lately quieter coin, for quieter work, and the quiet ones pay best three days from now.” And to @[Iratius](iratius), at the last, one flat forecast, delivered the way you hand a man his hat: “He will send for you now, soldier. He is patient, and he is owed, and he knows to the copper what you are worth.” Then the wagon and the mule and the gray man went, unhurried, north into the sleet — a man with no soul has nowhere particular to be, and goes there slowly — and the party stood in the barrows and watched him out of sight, and nobody put words to the fact that the two-season hunt was over and the hunter had come home empty-handed and heavier.

@[Iratius](iratius) did not sheath the sword that night. He sat apart at the fire's edge with @[Volcaris](volcaris) bare across his knees, the hairline in the fuller breathing its coal-light, and the party heard him — those who were honest later admitted straining to — talking to it. Low. Not commands. The register a man uses through a door to someone locked on the other side. Only one sentence survived to the record, and the record has decided it is enough: “If you can hear me in there — I am going to get you out.” Across the fire, @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s hand moved to his breast pocket, to a folded contract, and the two of them found each other's eyes over the flames, and the appointment they had made in a glade counted its parties again and came up larger. A very particular fire. More names on the kindling now.

@audio: remembrance-silent fade=8
Five days to the Feast, said the morning. And in the smallest hour before it, the gray had come for the seven — exact as always in its count — and the door it opened was hot, and smelled of rain striking char, and for the first time in any of their shared witnessing, there was no song anywhere in it: they watched the whole of it through, waiting for her lips to shape the tune the way they always shaped it, and her lips never did, and the absence sat in the vision like a held scream. This is what they watched.

## A Remembrance

*of Wrath*

*— she is perhaps forty —*

*Sound would be a mercy here, and there is none. A great hall is burning in total silence — light without roar, collapse without thunder, a fire like a held scream. The party knows the wall. All of them know the wall — they watched it rise, course by singing course, a mother's story warm in its first stone — and they watch it come down now with the light dying in the mortar, and the knowing is worse. The knowing is so much worse. Ash falls thick and slow, and the gray renders every flake.*

*The memory keeps its accounts backward here, and the reversal is the confession. Her own people — the bodies among the embers — are soft, half-drawn, looked-away-from: grief spared itself the details. But the burners are perfect. Every face beneath every hood is kept in merciless fidelity — the mole on a jaw, the sweat-line of a brow, the young one who would not look at what he was doing — while the holy symbols at their throats are smudged past reading. She kept the men. She declined to keep their excuse.*

*@[She arrives](the-girl-at-the-grave) too late, and stands in the gate-light with the heat bending the air around her stillness. Then she raises her arms — slowly, precisely, a conductor before the downbeat — and the dead of her order get up. Burning, they get up. She sends them, and the silent fire walks, and the perfect faces of the burners come apart in the perfect silence, and the party stands in the middle of the only Remembrance that feels like being made to watch.*

*One of the risen — half flame by now — turns its head to her in the middle of the march. There is something in the set of the burned face: not accusation, quite. Recognition. A question asked with the last architecture of a jaw. She looks away. It is a small motion, one muscle in a neck, and it is the entire moral history of the night: she does not stop, she does not answer, she looks away and lets her dead keep walking.*

*Her lips do not move in this memory. No @[three notes](the-tune), no channel-worn story. The party understands, in the way one understands things in dreams, that they are standing in the place where the song was not — and that its absence is the loudest thing the silence has ever done.*

*Dawn closes it: the hall a black ribcage, ash coming down like the snow of an earlier winter. She kneels in the ruin and lets it fall on her — shoulders, lap, hair, graying her by inches — and makes no move at all to brush it away. Around her, one by one, the risen lie back down like tools being set down, and the gray takes the rest.*
