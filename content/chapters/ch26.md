# XXVI. The Seam of Winter
*In which the way out asks what day it is*

@audio: bright-country fade=4
They stayed at the glade eleven notches, because leaving a thing you have just begun to heal is a skill nobody in the company had, and the green-gold patch grew by a hand-width a day, and the fairy sang to it, gray and glad. Then the Court's rider came back. One rider, one elk, one scroll-tube of ice that did not melt: the Court, having priced awe and found its books unbalanced, extended a courtesy — with, naturally, an invoice attached in the smallest script on the page. Passage by the Court's Road, the winter-kept way, exiting where the bearers should name. They named the moors. The sword named them, really — @[Volcaris](volcaris) had run warm and certain for days, wanting east and out and down — and @[Iratius](iratius) stood at the fire with his ledger's date behind his eyes and said, “The moors,” in the voice of a man booking passage to a duel.

The brother walked them to the road's edge, and stopped there, and everyone understood before anyone said it. The Court's Road began at a stile of rime standing alone in the green, and on the near side of it the brother's boots were planted, and @[Aelendir](aelendir) laid the truth out gently, because it was his country's truth to carry: the Feywild had kept him — name, years, and all — and its keeping was the only thing holding those years off him. Beyond the stile they were waiting, decades of them, patient as creditors at a door. He could not cross and stay the man who had followed a fiddle. “Then I'll wait,” the brother said, with the terrible mildness of the fey-kept, “you walked too fast the last time anyway.” The fairy took his arm — the two keepers of @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s history, standing in the first green-gold either had seen in years — and Corvyn did two things before he crossed the stile. He taught his brother @[the tune](the-tune), all eight bars, note by patient note, because the country could keep a man's name but it could not, now, repossess his music. And he folded the contract's copy into his breast pocket and said, to the brother, to the fairy, to the invoice-writing air itself: “I will find the clause.” Nobody in the party doubted him. He had become, in one afternoon at a frost-slate, the most dangerous kind of client.

The Court's Road ran through the green country like a seam of winter — a corridor of rime ten paces wide, frost-ferns on the bracken, elk-sign in the crust, the warm Feywild standing on either side close enough to touch and declining to enter — and the party's blaze ran quieter inside it, shaded under the Court's writ, which after weeks of being the loudest thing in the country felt like a hand lifted off the back of the neck. They walked it four notches. What the record keeps of those four days is mostly one thing, and it keeps it in full, because the record has grown aware — belatedly, and with something like shame — that the man in question has been all folded paper and four quiet sentences for the length of this book, and that it owes him better than a margin.

It began the second evening, when @[Gorvach](gorvach) sat down across the fire from @[Saeron](saeron), laid his war-shield flat between them like a table, and made the longest speech of his recorded life. He had been quieter than usual since the owned North — since the day he had watched Saeron hold a dead woman's pendant beside a dead clan's Veilmark, two marks that had outlived their makers and gone on speaking — and whatever he had been concluding since, he had concluded it at his own pace, like everything he kept. The conclusion was this: “Words stay when breath does not. Teach me to make them stay.” And Saeron — who had said perhaps forty sentences since Silverymoon — looked at the orc for a moment, and took out his knife, and scratched the first letter into the frost on the shield-face, and said, “This one is called ash. It sounds like the start of ask.” No preamble, no ceremony, and no praise — that was the teaching, all four nights of it: exact, patient past all reason, every stroke corrected by being silently redrawn beside rather than crossed out, total attention paid as though attention were the only coin he had ever trusted. Gorvach drilled letters the way he stood watches, methodical, unhurried, treating the alphabet as a gate with twenty-six wards. He learned fast, in the way of disciplined people. And the first whole word he asked for — having mastered his letters, offered anything in the language — was keep. He scratched it deep enough to survive the frost's nightly healing, and looked at it a long time, and nodded once, satisfied: the word now stayed.

One exchange from those fires, because the record has been rationing this man and regrets it. @[Gorvach](gorvach), studying his teacher over the shield on the last night: “You teach like a man who was taught kindly.” And @[Saeron](saeron), after a silence exactly long enough to be an answer in itself: “I was taught patiently. It is not always the same thing.” He redrew the orc's crooked rune beside the crooked one, and that was all, and Gorvach — who knew a closed gate when he stood before one — asked nothing further, and copied the corrected stroke, and the two flattest men in the company sat in a silence that had considerably more in it than most of the book's conversations.

The road ended at a second stile, and the second stile ended the Feywild. They stepped through rime into sleet — iron sky, gray horizon, the Evermoors rolling away south and east in wet folds, the wind smelling of peat and old water and, faintly, of the world owing nobody anything — home, in short — and when they turned, the stile stood alone on an empty moor with nothing behind it but more moor, its frost already losing the argument with the rain. @[Tirig](tirig) stood very still in the sleet for a moment. The singing behind her sternum had gone quieter with every step down the Court's Road, and out here it was almost — almost — what it had been before the white forest, and her face did something complicated that the party pretended not to catalog: relief, and loss, in whatever proportion those two travel in.

The shepherd found them before they found anyone — a moor-man behind a wet flock, staff and dog and total unsurprise, the moors being the sort of country where eight armed strangers and an orc materializing by a hoarfrosted stile ranks well below wolves on the season's list of concerns — and it was @[Saeron](saeron) who asked him, in a voice arranged to sound idle and fooling no one in the company: what day it was. The shepherd told him. The date, the tenday, and — unasked, in the way of countrymen for whom the calendar has one load-bearing beam — the distance: “Nine days shy of the Feast o' the Moon, that is. Candles are dear already, if you're buying.” The party stood in the sleet and did the arithmetic in eight separate silences. Fifteen notches stood on @[Amyl](amyl)'s staff. The world had run ahead of them while the Court's country held them — pocketed two days on the way through, casually, the way a tollman keeps change — and Saeron took out the folded page, and did not unfold it, and put it away. “Nine days,” he said, to nobody, in the tone of a man rebalancing a ledger whose currency had just been devalued.

They took stock that night at a moor-edge waystation — the Wet Ram, peat smoke and wool damp and a taproom of drovers — and the stock, laid on the table, was this. The sword pulled east-southeast now, hot and near and — for the first time in its life — stationary: whatever @[Volcaris](volcaris) wanted had stopped moving, somewhere out in the barrow country, and was waiting. And the ledger's date came out of @[Iratius](iratius) at last, at that table, after two seasons of finger-arithmetic no one had been allowed to audit. “My debt comes due on the Feast,” he said. Flat. “The same night as everything else in this year, apparently. I am meant to deliver — something — by that night, or be in breach.” He looked around the table once, daring comment. “I have not yet decided which.” Nobody commented. But @[Saeron](saeron)'s hand moved, very slightly, toward the pocket with the page in it, and the two clocks of the year clicked into one, and everyone at that table heard it happen.

The taproom gave them the moor's news for the price of listening. Wolves, bold this winter. A survey party the Evermoors had eaten whole, chains and all. Towns buying walls: @[Nesmé](nesme), chief among them, paying blade-coin to anything that could hold a spear, the way moor towns do when the year turns. And one item that made two heads at the party's table come up slowly: the ingot caravans. All winter, the drovers said, metal moving north through the moor roads in guarded strings — north, mind, when every honest trade was running south ahead of the snow — good pay, no questions, “and nobody who takes the coin will say who signs for it.” The drover who said it laughed. The party did not, and @[Corvyn](corvyn) and @[Aelendir](aelendir) traded the particular look of two men filing a masquerade under current affairs, and the record permits itself one raised eyebrow in the margin: the Thimble's threads, it appears, still spin.

They walked out the next morning into a sleet that was trying to be snow, east-southeast, the sword warming through its wrappings like an argument being won. Eight days to the Feast. By the second evening the barrow country stood up out of the moor ahead of them — low mounds in ranks, old past naming, the turf grown over them smooth as knuckles under a glove — and @[Volcaris](volcaris), for the first time since a mirror underground, went absolutely still in its scabbard: not cold. Certain. Arrived.
