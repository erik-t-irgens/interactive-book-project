# XX. The Long Dusk
*In which the North holds a border, and the border does not hold*

@audio: gray-dream fade=8
The border settled into its terrible routine within a tenday, because that is what borders do. The circles grew a thorn-wall overnight along the dusk's edge — living rampart, waist-high and knitting itself — and studded it with fire: torch-rings, watch-platforms in the low branches, rowan stakes at intervals the oldest wardens swore by and nobody dared audit. The watches mixed wardens and oath-friends deliberately, so that every shift held one person who had fought the dark before and one who was learning; and the camps, which had faced away from the blackened root all season, turned themselves around at last, and faced the thing, and held.

What came out of the dusk came in kinds, and the defenders learned the kinds the expensive way. The unpickers, first — the long licking shapes of the first night, patient as leaks, that starved torchlight and had to be met with @[Iratius](iratius)'s summoned fire, which they could not drink, and hated. Then the low striders that hunted in silences, whose approach you knew by the birds stopping. And worst, though it never once crossed the wall: the tall thing that walked the border on the far side, every night, unhurried, pausing at each torch as though taking a census — counting, the watches said, and stopped saying it aloud after the third night, and never stopped thinking it. Steel meant little to any of them. Fire meant much. And @[Bayne](bayne), drawn now without ceremony, meant most of all — the temple blade parting the dark stuff the way it had parted it in a crypt, in its second season of being believed in.

The forest itself began coming over to the enemy, and this the wardens grieved hardest. Wolves at the treeline with black-threaded gums, too patient, watching the fires in shifts of their own. Night-birds that flew into the dusk on some errand no bird has ever had, and came back flying wrong. The @[ichor-work](the-unmoored) the party knew from a bear and a stag, now general — and against it @[Amyl](amyl) and Neth ran what the camps came to call the green line: dawn sweeps of the border country, finding the touched things early, when the threads were shallow — and some of those they saved, the corruption cut and burned out by circle art before it reached the quick, a fox and a yearling doe and one entire marten family carried back to the healers' fires in a basket. It was small. Everyone knew it was small. It was also the only column in the whole ledger of that tenday that ran the right direction, and the camps fed on it accordingly.

And behind the wall's arithmetic ran @[Saeron](saeron)'s, which nobody audited because nobody could find the hours in it. He ran the hurt-line at the western fires — triage, dressings, the carrying of the ones past carrying themselves — and the watches swore he was never once seen to sleep, which the record doubts and sets down anyway, because it is what the watches swore. A senior warden went, one bad midnight, past every green art at the fires, and it was Saeron who knelt with her at the end; and those nearest heard him say — low, level, in the tone of instruction rather than comfort — “You carried it well. Set it down now. It was never yours to carry alone.” And at dawn, even there, at the wall's edge with the torches guttering, the crimson cords came off and went back on, thirteen knots, by firelight.

The great working failed on the twelfth night, and its failure set the arc of everything after. All sixty seniors, linked at the thorn-wall from moonrise, raised a sealing across the wound — the whole green art of the North bent to one closure, and it took, and held: a day and a night of ordinary noon in the root-country, the dusk gone, the border quiet, the camps daring to cheer. And on the second evening the sealing opened along its length like stitches in a wet wound, all at once, and the dusk stood back up where it had stood, one spear-length wider. The eldest took the report without expression and posted the arithmetic where the camps could read it, because she ran her people on truth: the door gained ground daily; closures bought days and spent the seniors like coin; and @[the Tree](the-grandfather-tree) — the canopy graying now in a widening ring overhead — was losing. “We cannot close it,” she said at the fires, flat as Gorvach. “We can only lose it slowly. If anyone alive can do better, they are not here.”

The deliberations happened at those same fires, and the record keeps two of them. The first was the door itself: it was @[Iratius](iratius) who finally said it — that doors have two sides; that somewhere through that fartherness the wound presumably had an edge that could be worked; that he had walked into worse rooms on thinner arguments. The counter-arguments were practical and were losing, until @[Sov](sov) ended the debate without meaning to. Asked directly — by @[Tirig](tirig), who else — what he felt when he stood a watch at the wall, he answered with one word, chosen the way he chose everything, and the word was: “Homesick.” In the silence after, nobody proposed entering again. They did not dare, and the record wants this said plainly, because the party would spend a long time afterward wondering: not daring was the wisdom, that season. That door gets a chapter of its own, much later, and it is reached by a different road.

The second deliberation was north, and it sharpened all through the dusk-weeks because of the sword. @[Volcaris](volcaris) had spent its whole recorded life swinging — a weathervane in an argument, warmth that would not hold a bearing — and somewhere in that tenday the argument ended. The warmth set. North — steady, patient, insistent as the @[crystal](the-heart-of-winters-veil) at @[Tirig](tirig)'s throat, the two pulls lying on the map like parallel rulings — and @[Iratius](iratius) hated it visibly and would not say why. A ledger that points at a moving man had gone still: either the mark had stopped moving, or the sword had changed subjects, and he did his fire-side arithmetic with a new grimness and shared none of it. But leaving was not yet a question anyone could ask out loud. You do not walk away from a border you are holding. The map lay by the fires, both pulls aligned, and the border held them, and everyone understood that something would have to change before the road could.

@[Sov](sov) managed his own gravity like a man rationing a medicine. He assigned himself day shifts at the wall, only days, and said so plainly, which cost him; and the party kept his nights without being asked. Once — once that the record knows of — the arrangement failed: he woke standing at the thorn-wall in the smallest hour, fist full of feathers, face turned to the fartherness, with no memory of the tent flap or the frost or the hundred paces of sleeping camp behind him. @[Tirig](tirig) was beside him, cloakless, breath smoking. “How long have you been here?” he managed. “Since you started walking.” “Why didn't you wake me?” And Tirig looked at the dark past the wall, and gave him the answer he kept: “You weren't asleep.” They stood together until the torches changed, and in the morning he told the others everything, unprompted, which was the bravest thing the record has him doing with no enemy in sight.

Then the dusk went quiet, and the quiet was the worst thing it had done. Two full nights: nothing at the wall, nothing in the treeline, the tall census-taker absent from its rounds — the border's best nights on paper, and the oldest wardens walked through them with their shoulders up around their ears. It was Berra who put figures to the dread. She had made herself mistress of the enemy's own instruments — Certitude's gauge-ring, read now for the defense, the @[listening pins](the-glass) conscripted — and on the second quiet evening she brought her columns to the eldest's fire with her jaw set. “The tide is drawing back,” she said. “All readings. Everywhere on the ring at once.” She let the sheet fall. “Tides do that. Before.”

They doubled the torches that night, and ringed the master's warded grove — the dusk had crept to within a long bowshot of the hazel hurdles, and Berra checked the wards at the gate twice and then a third time — and the camps lay awake under a canopy going gray, listening to the forest not make a sound. The dusk did not creep, when it came. It stood up.
