## A Remembrance

@audio: ascension-night fade=8
*of Remembering*

*— she is as old as she will ever be —*

They know the place before they know anything else. The sun-bleached walkways over bright shallow water; the footbridges converging; the great tree, and the worked stone at its root. It is the country of the very first dream — the brightest place the gray ever showed them — and it stands now in deep winter, the silver trees white to the last twig, and above the canopy, plainly, impossibly, two skies: one bright, one black, meeting along a seam like a healed wound in the world. The white forest. The threshold. Her mother's grave beneath the tree. It was always all the same place. It was always going to end where it began.

And there is sound. Not the water-far murmur of the wall-raising — sound entire: wind in silver branches, boots creaking snow, breath, the knock of a knuckle testing a metal bowl. Because this is not her memory, worn thin by a goddess's distance. This is his. He stands at the edge of the circle with the @[Resonance Bowl](the-resonance-bowl) in his arms — younger by nothing at all, eladrin do not spend the way others do, the same face the party knows from firelight and masquerade — and more than one of the watchers turns to look at @[the Aelendir beside them](aelendir), and finds him staring at himself across a gulf of centuries, beginning, at last, to be permitted to remember.

The fellowship stands in a ring around the grave-tree, joined hand to hand — and the party knows them. The flour-sleeved widow of the wall, old now. Faces from the rope-line, older. The young woman whose tears crossed a face not hers. Perhaps forty of them, every one rendered past perfect, and at the ring's heart, at the stone where a girl once knelt and called down shadows, @[the woman](the-girl-at-the-grave) — gray-haired entire now, as the mortar-dust always promised — turns to face her people. And she speaks, and for the first and only time in all the country of these memories, they hear her voice.

“No one after tonight will be lost,” she says. “Not you. Not anyone. Not ever again. What it costs, it costs me first and most. If any of you would go home, go now, and I will love you exactly the same.” The wind moves in the white branches. Nobody goes. An old soldier laughs, warm and unafraid, and squeezes the hands he holds, and that laugh — the sound of a person consenting with joy to a thing no one present understands — is the exact center of the tragedy, and the party stands inside it and can do nothing, which is what witnesses are for.

@[Aelendir begins to play](aelendir). @[The song, complete](the-tune) — the three notes and everything they were always climbing toward, the whole architecture the party has carried in fragments since a violin in a taproom — and the working takes. Light lifts from the ring of faces like breath in cold air: brightness leaving, joy leaving, going up and inward toward @[the woman](the-girl-at-the-grave) at the stone, and the singers' voices go flat mid-note and keep singing anyway, pale now, gray-eyed now, steadfast now and forever, the @[first Shadar-kai](the-gray-country) being born mid-song and holding the harmony regardless — the most loyal thing the party has ever seen, and the most stolen-from.

@unlock: the-girl-at-the-grave.5
From her chest, one after another, small crystal shapes rise into the cold — and each one, as it lifts, flashes a scene the watchers know. A hearth and a carved plank. A honey-cake on nameless graves. A wall full of best days. A conductor before silent fire. A rope through a blizzard. A pair of empty hands in a doorway. Six heartbeats, a whole life leaving a body in its keystones — and then a seventh shape, and an eighth, scenes the party does not know, one of them proud and terrible with a pulpit in it — and the shapes scatter upward past the two skies and are gone to wherever hidden things go, and @[the woman](the-girl-at-the-grave) at the stone grows very still, the way a house grows still when everyone has left it.

Then the world balances its books. On the grave beneath the tree, the carved name smooths itself blank — not worn, not broken: unwritten, the letters closing like water — and the party understands without being told that the same smoothing is happening everywhere at once, in every ledger and lintel and mother's memory that ever held her, the price of godhood collected in a single universal keystroke. The goddess of remembrance pays with her name. The gray floods in from the horizons — their gray, the flat colorless country of every dream they have ever shared, being born in front of them out of the brightest place in the world — and she opens her eyes, which are new, and dark, and vast.

And she reaches up to her own face for the tears that belong to what she has just done, and her hand comes away dry. She looks at the dry hand for a long time. That is the first act of @[the Raven Queen](the-raven-queen), witnessed entire: she tried to weep, and there was nothing left in her to weep with, because it was scattering across the planes in eight small crystal shapes — and six of those shapes are standing in this memory, watching her discover it.

She gathers her pale people with a gesture that is already a queen's, and they go into the gray together, unhurried, a fellowship still. @[Aelendir alone remains](aelendir), in the last unbleached circle of green at the foot of the tree, the bowl silent in his arms — and as the party watches, the fog takes him from the inside. His hands soften first; he looks down at them as they go; his face lifts, unfinished, toward the place where the skies had been — the only time in all the Remembrances they see the smear take someone while he is still in it. Mercy, or payment, or the new goddess's first gift: he will not remember. The last thing in the memory is the Bowl's final tone, hanging in the empty air long after everything else has gone gray and silent — longer than sound should last, the way his tones always do — and then even that, and then the party is elsewhere, and the elsewhere is quiet.
