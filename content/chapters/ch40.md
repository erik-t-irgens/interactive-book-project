# XL. The Deliverance
*In which the bells ring, the doors open, and a boy comes through awake*

@audio: deliverance-bells fade=6
The bells began before the light did. Not alarm bells; that was the first wrongness, and the party was up and armed at the shutters before their ears finished explaining it: the bells were glad. Peal answering peal across the doubled dark, tower taking it from tower, harbor bells and school bells and the shrine bells at every five-way corner, ringing the way cities ring for a wedding or a war's end; and below in the street, doors opening, lamps kindling, neighbors calling to neighbors two words the party could not hear and did not need to — @[a Deliverance](the-deliverance); a Deliverance — and flowers, in this city that blooms them out of season, beginning to move toward the shrines in the dark like pale traffic. @[Fundament](fundament) was waking up joyful. Somewhere, last night, while the party slept, a town had died; and the city that kept them was ringing every bell it had, in gratitude, for the saved.

@[Darathra](darathra)'s runner came while the bells were still arguing, with a slate instead of a note: intake stands mustered; her wards were receiving; and — in her own chalk, quick and square at the bottom — four words that were an order, an apology, and an act of war all in one line: You will work it. They understood her completely, and this book will make sure of the reader too: not spectate. Work. There would be hands wanted below, and the hands might as well be seven pairs that knew what they were carrying; and there is no testimony in any court of any world like the testimony of the people who did the carrying. They dressed in silence. @[Aelendir](aelendir) put the old sheet inside his coat. Nobody suggested otherwise.

@audio: fundament-doubled fade=4
The way down to the receiving hall is a processional, and it is meant to be: a broad switchback stair sunk through the city's roots, lamp-lined, garlanded now by ward-runners moving ahead of the descent, and the party went down it in Darathra's muster among cooks and porters and physicians and folding-cot carriers, down past the levels they knew, past the levels anyone knows, to where the elder stone stopped being walls and became the world. And there the party's schooling betrayed them, all at once, the way schooling does: because they had stood in the basin at @[Ascore](ascore) with their hands on the original, and their ears were trained now, and the great courses of pale masonry rising around the stairwell's last turns were humming. Almost rightly. Almost — the hum of held feeling, the Mooring's kind fullness, copied by a hand that had every proportion and no warmth, the way the black stones copy, the way everything in this city is copied: a lullaby sung by something that has read about lullabies. @[Tirig](tirig)'s hand found the rail. @[Amyl](amyl)'s found Tirig. Nobody said Ascore aloud. The stair let them out at the bottom of the world, and the bottom of the world is a cathedral.

@audio: none fade=8
This book has spent its year being careful with the word vast, and spends it now. The @[receiving hall](the-receiving-hall) of Fundament stands where the two countries actually meet — not the fade above, not the strait's mannered gradient: the seam itself, the wound's floor — a nave of elder work so high the bells of the city above arrived in it as a rumor, its long walls pierced by galleries, its pavement half in each world: the near acres honest dark stone under honest lamplight, and away toward the far end, by degrees, the floor going pale, going wrong, going luminous, until at the hall's far heart, framed in an arch that made the Moongleam gate look like a cottage door, stood @[the Crossing](the-crossing) — a standing plane of @[not-quite-glass](the-glass), taller than masts, wide as a street, deep with the moving dark of somewhere else — and before it, in disciplined ranks down the whole shining approach: biers. Hundreds of biers, blanketed, lamplit, numbered, waiting; and the catchers at their stations; and the carriers in their files; and the whole apparatus of the tenderest crime in the world, oiled and mustered and ready, under bells.

They saw the @[antechambers](the-antechamber-mirrors) on the way to their stations, and the party would think about the antechambers for the rest of their lives. Along the nave's flanks, in recessed bays behind pillars, stood mirrors — tall frames of the elder pale stone holding surfaces that were not the Crossing's moving dark but something quieter, more finished, more owned: instruments, the party understood at once, of a different make — between the Sharran mirrors they had broken and the honest arch at Moongleam, some third and finer thing — and each one, glanced at in passing, held in its reflection not the hall but somewhere else. A rain-dark street under mountain eaves. A harbor at night. A rise of moor-grass above a town's lamplit grid that @[Saeron](saeron) stopped dead in front of, one heartbeat, before Darathra's muster carried him on. And one — the fourth bay, the one the party walked past slowest — held bright morning: sun-bleached walkways over shallow glittering water, footbridges converging, and a single great tree with worked stone at its root; and every one of the seven knew it, the way you know a room from a dream, and could not place it, and had no time; and the mirror held its morning patiently behind them as they went, like a page holding its place.

@unlock: the-archivist.2
The @[archivist](the-archivist) ran the floor. The party had seen her file kindness in ones and twos for a season; they had never seen her spend it at scale, and the sight reorganized everything they thought they had understood about the small gray-eyed woman with the folder. She was everywhere, and she was calm the way keels are calm; she placed her people with two fingers and a word; she checked the blanket-weight against the crossing-chill by holding one to her own cheek; she moved down the bier-ranks adjusting a pillow, a number, a lamp-wick, with an economy that was not coldness and was the opposite of coldness — it was rehearsed love, love drilled like a fire brigade — and the party, working their stations, watched her work and felt the day's first true vertigo: because whatever else was truth in that hall, her tenderness was not a performance. She wanted every head cushioned. She wanted every name right. She had built a machine to catch what her machine knocked down, and she was checking its every strap herself, with her own hands, in the deep of the world, under bells; and @[Sov](sov), tightening a cot-lace at the rank's end, said quietly, to no one, in the voice he keeps for ledgers: “She means it. That is going to be the problem.”

@unlock: merris.4
And moving through the staff, with lists, smiling, came a woman the party knew. Gray still, though the cut was intake-issue now and the inquisitor's sash was gone; unhurried; pleasant; her stylus ticking down her columns with the light sure touch of a person entirely at home in her work — and she looked up as the seven of them made her, all at once, the way a line of soldiers makes a shape on a ridge, and @[Merris Thulane](merris) did not look surprised to see them at all. She never had. She finished her column. Then she came down the rank to them, unhurried, and stood in front of seven people who had watched her open a sluice under a doomed town and step backward into the glass smiling, and she smiled that same smile — easy, unarmored, the smile of the only person in the room with no cognitive load at all — and said: “You made good time. I hoped you would.” And then, with a small nod at the biers, at the hall, at the whole tender terrible apparatus, in the voice of a believer welcoming friends to her church: “Welcome to the work.” And she walked on down her list; and @[Saeron](saeron) stood with his hands open at his sides for a long ten-count, and closed them, and went back to his blankets; and the party understood that they had just been extended professional courtesy by the first willing traveler, in the place where betrayal retires to, and that she had meant every word exactly as much as the archivist meant hers.

The name arrived before the people did, the way names do in musters: called down the ranks for the manifest-boards, chalked at the head of every file, entered at the clerk-stations in the square devout hand. @[Jalanthar](jalanthar). A Rauvin town; timber and ferry-trade; the party had ridden within a day of it once, in another life, on the road east of Silverymoon. @[Darathra](darathra) was three ranks away when the chalk went up, checking cots, and the party watched her read the word, and stop, and take out her own folded copy of the undated list, and hold it a moment without opening it — because she did not need to open it; she had read it every night for three nights; smith and miller and ferry-keep — and put it away, and square her shoulders, and go back to the cots, because the bells were ringing and the biers were waiting and there is no order a Lord Protector can give to yesterday. But the party saw her write the date, later, in the margin of the list, in the intake hall, standing: the hour the chalk went up. Filling in the one blank the enemy's paperwork had left her.

Then the Crossing woke, and the Delivered came through, and this book will set it down plainly and let it be what it is. The great dark surface silvered, the way water silvers when something rises; the wardens at the arch stood to their marks; and out of somewhere else, barefoot on the pale luminous floor, in nightclothes, in shop aprons, in one case in a baker's dusting of flour from a batch that would burn untended in another world — the people of Jalanthar walked. Asleep. Upright, eyes closed, faces slack and easy, walking the walk of deep dreamers, alone and in pairs and in stumbling family clusters with their hands still linked from whatever last instant the working had found them in; and the catchers received them — two to a sleeper, an arm and an arm, the practiced pivot — and laid them on the biers, and the carriers lifted the biers, and the files began to move; and it was gentle, all of it, gentle past enduring: the blanket to the chin, the head turned to keep an airway, a physician's two fingers at every throat, the numbers called soft as a litany — forty-one, breathing well; forty-two, breathing well; a child, alone, caught and carried by a warden who tucked the trailing foot in with a hand that had done it ten thousand times — rank after rank after rank of a town being carried up sleeping toward the quays, where the morning and the doctrine and the flowers were waiting; and the party worked. That is what the chapter has instead of a battle: seven people laying their enemies' victims down softly, hour upon hour, blanket upon blanket, doing the one thing their year had taught them past all argument — you tend what is in front of you — while the bells rang gratitude through a mile of stone overhead, and the hum of the almost-right bricks held its one wrong note under everything, patient as interest.

@unlock: aelendir.3
The party kept themselves whole in their separate ways, and the book audits them briefly, because the cost was real. @[Amyl](amyl) worked the throat-checks with the physicians and said the cobbler's name to himself, once, at the first bier, like a man saluting. @[Tirig](tirig) ran a carrier-file with flawless economy and did not look at the Crossing again after the first family came through holding hands. @[Corvyn](corvyn), stationed on blankets, hummed — the honest three wrong notes, under his breath, over and over, a lullaby against a lullaby — and the sleepers nearest him, the physicians noted without explanation, breathed easier. @[Iratius](iratius) carried. Only that: carried, bier after bier, with the strength that was all his own now and had to be paid for in shaking forearms by the third hour, and would not rotate off. @[Saeron](saeron) worked the child-ranks, because somebody had to and he has an old arrangement with the worst duty in any room. @[Aelendir](aelendir) stood at the clerk-station confirming names — names, his oldest business — saying each one aloud correctly, making the record true where the record was all that would be true. And @[Sov](sov) walked the laid ranks with the archivist's own gait, straightening what wanted straightening, and stopped being able to tell, somewhere in the fourth hour, whether he was mocking her or joining her, and did not stop.

She found them at the shift's turn — of course she did; she finds everyone — coming down the rank with water and that terrible efficient kindness, and she thanked them. Simply, by each name, with the folder under her arm: the city was grateful; the Session would hear how its guests had served; the Deliverance — and here she looked down the shining approach at the biers still coming, and the party watched something cross the small gray face that was not administration: a hunger of purpose, a determination so old and so total it had worn itself smooth, the look of someone thirty years into a labor whose end she can finally see — “the Deliverance,” she finished, “is nearly done with the gathering. After the winter, there will be no more mornings like this one. No more towns. No more lists. It will be finished, and everyone will be home, and no one —” the smallest pause, a cataloguer's pause, the party's second true vertigo of the day — “no one will ever be lost again.” And she moved on down the rank with her water; and @[Tirig](tirig) stood looking after the small gray figure among the sleepers with her eyes gone hunter-still, because she had heard a sentence shaped exactly like that once before, in a dream of silent films, on the lips of a girl the whole gray country had been trying to show them all year; and the suspicion that entered her in that hour never left her, and she was right about everything except the one thing that mattered.

@[Jalanthar](jalanthar) came through until the lamps of the first watch were lit. Nine hundred and forty biers; the party heard the final count called and confirmed at the clerk-stations, and heard, too, what was not called: no losses in transit — and one file empty. One rank of biers at the approach's end stood blanketed and waiting and was never filled, and the wardens struck it without comment, the way professionals strike a contingency; and @[Darathra](darathra), passing, counted the empty cots with one long look and filed the number wherever she files the things she will want later. The Crossing's silver stilled. The great hall began its long exhale — carriers returning, lamps trimmed, the litany of breathing-well giving way to the homelier noise of an honest shift ending — and the party stood down off their stations into that specific exhaustion that has love and horror in it in equal weight and no place to put either.

And then the Crossing silvered once more — out of sequence, unannounced, the wardens coming around to their marks with the first crack in their discipline the party had seen all day — and one more figure came through the standing dark. Not walking the dreamers' walk. Walking. Eyes open. A boy — taller by a hand than the party had left him, road nowhere on him now, the desert gone from his boots, coming through the doors that mercy had prepared awake, alone, on his own two feet, with the pale floor lighting under his steps; and every warden and catcher and clerk in the hall's near acres stopped, because it does not happen, because it has never once happened, because the whole tender apparatus of the Deliverance exists so that it can never happen — and @[Gregory](gregory) crossed the shining approach through the ranks of his own sleeping countrymen, past the struck contingency biers, past the frozen catchers, straight to the seven people standing at the shift-line, as though the hall were empty and had always been empty; and the party saw his eyes find them, and saw what the link had made of him in a year, the age in him that had nothing to do with height; and he stopped in front of them, and swayed; and @[Amyl](amyl)'s hands were already moving; and Gregory of Triboar said, clearly, into the vast listening silence of the bottom of the world — “It's coming here. Now.” — and his eyes rolled white, and he went down into Amyl's arms like a tide going out, asleep before anyone caught him, the first and last of the Delivered to arrive awake; and far away above them, unaware, joyful, the bells of Fundament were still ringing.
