# XV. The Two Watchers
*In which a sentence is spent, a pocket is reached, and a door closes quietly*

@audio: mirror-glass fade=5
The sixth evening put a voice to half the mystery. @[Sov](sov) had gone out alone at dusk — a small rebellion, ten minutes' walk to the chapel lane and back, the others in sight of the windows the whole way, or so the theory ran — and in the lane, in the hour when the lamplighters make the shadows briefly worse, a figure was waiting that had not been there between one lamppost and the next. Deep hood. A shape that held still the way soldiers hold still. Sov's hand found the haft of his mace and stopped there, arrested by something he could not have explained — and the figure spoke one sentence, low, unhurried, in a voice that seemed to have crossed a distance much longer than the lane:

“Your mother kept her side. I have tried to keep mine.”

And the lane was empty. Between one heartbeat and the next, without footsteps, without theater — empty, as if the shadows had simply reconsidered holding a man's shape. Sov stood a long time. Then he went back to the house and did something none of them had quite heard him do in a season of catastrophes: he asked them all to sit down.

@audio: gray-dream fade=8
He told them about Lenore. His mother — a courtesan of Ormath, far south, beautiful by profession and stubborn by nature — who had died. Not nearly died: died, laid out and mourned, and then returned, walking back into her own wake with river-mud on her hem and no explanation she would ever give past a single sentence, spoken only when pressed and never twice: that she had made an arrangement, and kept it, and it was no one's business but the parties'. Two months later she was showing. She named the child Thimron — raven, in the old elvish — and would not say why, and touched the name like a debt every time she said it, and she lives still, so far as he knows — a seamstress in Ormath, far to the south, gray now at the temples, to whom he said one hurried goodbye on the night he fled the city with a stolen blade, and to whom he has never written since. Not for lack of love. Because every letter he ever started began with a question she had spent his whole childhood declining to answer. “I grew up knowing my existence was somebody's terms,” he said, to the quiet room. “I never knew whose. Tonight a stranger in a lane knew my mother's bargain — knew there were sides, and that she kept hers.” He turned one of the black feathers over in his fingers. “I am tired of being the only person who does not know what I am for.”

He did not tell the party why he left Ormath that night, or why a temple’s sword left with him — because he could not have. There had been a why. He was certain of the shape of it, the way you are certain of a missing tooth: something urgent, something joyful, something worth stealing for. It was gone. The sword had stayed — wrapped, senseless, heavy, a debt whose origin he had somehow misplaced — and that, though he had no way of knowing it, was the truest thing said in the room that night: a man surrounded by friends, carrying a blade he could not account for, missing a reason the size of a person.

The watching, meanwhile, resolved into grammar. It was @[Corvyn](corvyn) — a performer, self-taught in the reading of rooms he was never born to — who put words to what the rest had only felt: they were being watched by two. Different heights. Different habits. Different hungers. “One of them watches @[Sov](sov) like a question,” he said. “The other watches him like an answer it is checking.” The question kept to lanes and doorways and was never there when you turned. The answer kept to high places, and had feathers, and was there when you turned — that was the worst of it — sat plainly on its finial, insolent as an auditor, until you blinked.

On the seventh morning the warning came, and it came in a way that emptied the blood from four faces at once: written, folded small, and found by @[Sov](sov) among his own collected feathers — inside his breast pocket, where no hand could have gone unfelt. The hand was spare and disciplined. @[The legation](fundament) is not here for the council's answer. It is here for the bridge's. When the bridge fails — and it is being failed, deliberately — the offer will change its name from partnership to rescue. Do not be in the room on the day this city says yes. And below, after a space, as if the writer had weighed the cost of the second thought and paid it: You have counted the feathers. Now ask what the feathers are fattening. They stood around the note a long time. “The question,” @[Corvyn](corvyn) said at last, quietly, “warns him about the answer. And about the birds.” It did not need saying that a man who could reach the inside of Sov's pocket had chosen, every day of that week, to leave him unharmed — and it did not comfort anyone that the choosing was the comfort.

---

They tried once, on the eighth day, to turn the watching around: a lure walked slow through the lane at dusk, @[Tirig](tirig) wearing a borrowed face on the rooftops, @[Saeron](saeron) folded into a doorway with his breathing stopped. It taught them exactly one thing. The high places were empty in the way that means recently, all evening, everywhere they looked — and when they regrouped, unenlightened, the crow was on Mariel's own gatepost, watching them return, with the patience of a creditor who has seen every version of this before.

The note came under the door that same night, and it wore the lane-voice's manner almost perfectly. Come tonight, alone, or with only those who already know. The old necropolis lane; the third door below. There are things about your mother that should not wait for morning. @[Sov](sov) read it four times at the table while the others read his face. It said everything the lane-voice would say. It hooked the exact hook. The party's answer was immediate and total and had the quality of a door closing: he was not going alone, and that was the end of the discussion, and the discussion ended with supper assignments for the morning and the candles put out.

@audio: none fade=8
And some fraction of @[Sov](sov) — he could not have said which faith it belonged to — lay awake with the note and did its own arithmetic. The voice in the lane had spent his mother like the only coin it had, once, unasked, and vanished rather than profit from the spending. Would that voice put her on paper and slide her under a door? He did not wake anyone to ask. That was the mistake, and he made it the way he made every mistake of his careful life: quietly, alone, and for what seemed at the time like consideration. He left before the kitchen lamps were cold, with the note, and the feathers, and both faiths, and he closed the door behind him the way careful men close doors — silently, and on everyone who would have come.
