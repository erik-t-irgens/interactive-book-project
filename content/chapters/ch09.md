# IX. The Geas
*In which a fire tells on its setter, a prisoner speaks in heartbeats, and a door opens from the wrong side*

*— Here the book crosses out of what we played and into what I planned. —*

*From this chapter on, you are reading the road I built, not the road we proved.*

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=6
There were no orcs in the Tower. There was only the fire — and the fire, it would turn out, was worse.

The rumors in the street had promised a battle, and the party descended into smoke instead. The Tower's lower levels were choked with it, rolling up the stairwells in slow gray coils, and the bucket-train @[Corvyn](corvyn) had built out of the town's panic proved the night's first mercy: a chain of hands passing water down landing by landing, into the basement where the smoke was thickest — down the long hall of the cell block, to a burning office at its far end, directly opposite the cells. The party fought the fire at the seat of it while the chain fed them, and the prisoners watched from behind their bars with the fixed attention of those who cannot run from whatever comes.

It was @[Saeron](saeron) who took the head of the bucket-chain — the station nearest the flames, where the heat curls paint off a door at three paces and every second bucket turns to steam before it lands — and held it, unrelieved, the whole length of the train. Twice someone moved up to take his place; twice he had somehow already stepped forward to a nearer station that had not existed until he stood in it. When it was over he presented his forearms for dressing last, after everyone, at his own quiet insistence, which nobody won an argument against; and to @[Iratius](iratius) — who understood fire, and had watched the whole performance with a professional's disbelief — he offered the only sentence anyone got out of him that night: “Pain that buys something is the cheapest coin there is.”

When it was out, and the party stood soot-blackened and coughing in the wreck of the room, the fire told them what it had been for. This was @[the Twelve](the-twelve)'s records office. The cabinets had been pulled open before the burning started — not rifled, but selected — and what had burned hottest, at the center where the oil had been poured, were the duty ledgers: the files and patrol schedules of the Twelve, years of rotations reduced to curling ash. A fire does not choose its fuel. Someone had chosen for it. And the choice was paperwork — the dry administrative record of who guarded what, and when, and with whom — which is the sort of thing a person burns only when the record itself has become dangerous to them.

@[Darathra](darathra) came down through the smoke with two of her Twelve beside her — @[Nik](nik), grim and soot-streaked, and a sober, exact woman named @[Edda Vell](edda-vell), the Mistwarden, keeper of the Tower's keys and rotations, who took one look at the burned cabinets and went very still. A third of the Twelve had been below that night as well, they said — @[Merris Thulane](merris), the Inquisitor, who had gone to audit the lower levels when the alarm went up and had not yet come back. No one thought anything of it. There was a great deal not to think anything of, that night, and it was all of it about to matter.

It was while the party was being tended — burns dressed, lungs clearing, the bucket-chain dispersing above — that the cold reached them. It came from the direction of the cells, threading under the smoke like a second weather: frost, feathering the stones of the corridor, thickening toward the far cell where a shape sat huge and motionless in the dark. And with the cold came something else, at the very edge of hearing. Whispers. Not from the prisoner — his lips were still — but around him, riding the frost the way scent rides wind: a murmuring in some old tongue of the fey, soft and grieving and endlessly repeated, in which only one impression survived translation. Someone begging forgiveness. Someone speaking of a lady beneath the ice.

*And @[Tirig](tirig) felt it before she saw any of it. A pull — low and wordless, somewhere beneath the sternum, the way a held compass trembles — drawing her down the corridor toward the frost and the shape inside it. She had felt the far North her whole remembered life the way one feels a wall at one's back. This was the first time it had ever felt like a hand on her shoulder.*

---

The orc's name was @[Gorvach](gorvach) — though no one had it from @[Darathra](darathra), who knew little more than the capture-record: he had given nothing to the Twelve in all his months below, not to questioning and not to kindness, nothing but the mark burned into his palm. The name surfaced the way everything else would surface that night — out of the cadence itself, one of his flat literal answers to a flat literal question — and the story the party assembled from those answers was smaller and sadder than the mob's version. He had arrived before any of it — before the refugees, before Sera, before the party — walking out of the north unarmed and half-frozen and strange, taken without a struggle by a patrol he did not resist. He had not raided. He had not so much as raised a hand. But there was frost on him that no season explained, and his veins ran pale with a shimmer like starlight under the skin, and the Twelve who took him understood only that an orc this far south, this wrong, was a spark on dry tinder. The truce with the northern clans was a held breath. Darathra had locked him away not for what he had done but for what the town would do about him — and had denied him to a mob because the truth would have fed the fire faster than any lie.

He was a prisoner twice over, and the second cage was the crueler one. He sat idle in his cell not from resignation but because there was nowhere the geas would let him go; the will that had sent him south held him still, patient as winter, and the man inside the compulsion had simply worn down against it like a stone under dripping water. Her Harper friends — the quiet, careful people Darathra answered to and did not name — had taken one look at him and known what the Twelve had not: this was no generic orc, and no ordinary binding. Something old had made this. Something old wanted something, and had spent a living person to want it with.

---

Talking to him was the puzzle, and it was a puzzle with no lock to pick — only a man to reach.

He would not answer questions, at first. He sat fixed on rhythms: the drip of water somewhere in the stone, the pulse in his own wrists, the heartbeats of the people before his bars — his eyes flicking in patterns, keeping time with cadences no one else could hear. Direct questions got literal answers or none. Any question that began with why slid off him like water off ice; the geas owned the whys. It would have been patience that found the way in — someone's; my money, for whatever a dungeon master's money is worth, was on @[Saeron](saeron), who knew better than anyone at that table what it costs to keep a fire banked. They notice the pattern in his eyes. They match it — speak in rhythm, question by slow question, meeting the cadence instead of fighting it. And when even that stalls, it is the truce that turns the key: someone invokes his oath, the peace his people bled for, the thing that is his and not the winter's — and for the space of perhaps a minute, @[Gorvach](gorvach) of the Veilmark Clan surfaces from underneath his geas and looks at them with his own eyes.

*What he gave them in that minute, he gave in fragments, each one costing him — frost crawling from his lips as he forced the words past the binding's edge. He had been sent to find something. The thing he was sent for was not in the North anymore; it was in mortal hands. And the place it belonged to — the place it was taken from — lay beyond a cave mouth in the far, far North: a white forest with two skies. Then the white took his eyes again, and the frost sealed his lips, and the minute was over. But before he sank back under, his eyes went — once, deliberately — to the small @[crystal at Tirig](the-heart-of-winters-veil)'s throat, and he inclined his head toward it, the way one bows to a superior. They would discover it that same night: held in a closed fist, the crystal leaned. A patient, insistent tug, faint as a sleeping pulse, and always, always toward north. He had not needed to say why. He had looked at her the way a compass looks at north, and that was the why, and neither of them could have put words to it.*

Two skies. Only one of them had ever heard those words before, and she had never said so aloud. The corridor waited — and @[Tirig](tirig), who volunteered nothing on principle, spent one long breath deciding, and then spoke. “The mirror,” she said. “In the cave. It showed me a white forest. Two skies over it — one bright, one dark.” She said no more than that, and no one pushed, because her voice had the sound of a door opened exactly one hand's width. But her hand had gone, without her deciding it, to the small crystal she carried — the amulet that had come south with her out of the white — and the frost on the bars of @[Gorvach](gorvach)'s cell breathed a little brighter, and nobody in that corridor needed the connection drawn for them.

---

The other cells gave up their own weight while the party worked. Lethis Dorne, the thin young scryer, still swore his innocence and still dreamed, he said, of a mirror that screamed back — and the party, who had stood before such a mirror, let the claim sit unanswered. And Tovric Hale, the silenced guard, jailed for treason — silenced in the oldest way there is: his tongue had been cut out, the wound long healed, the work of somebody who had wanted him alive, and mute, and instructive, had been carving into the soft wood of his bunk all this time. Asked, letter by scratched letter, what he had seen and who had hurt him, he wrote the same two letters over and over, pressing them deep: M.T. And when the party pressed further — slowly, patiently, a man spelling out his testimony with a splinter — the shape of it came clear. He had seen interrogations no one had sanctioned. The orc. And Sera, in the days before she died. He had tried to report what he saw, and the last thing his voice ever did was try.

The grinding began somewhere below them and to the east — iron on stone, old and heavy, a sound with rust in it — and @[Edda Vell](edda-vell)'s face changed before anyone else understood. There is nothing down there, she said, in the voice of a woman checking her own keys. There is nothing down there but the old sluice, and the sluice has been sealed since before — and then she stopped, because the sound was the sluice, and the sluice was opening, and the only people alive who knew its grate-sequence were the Mistwarden who kept the Tower's keys and the Inquisitor who audited them. And the Mistwarden was standing in the cell block with her keys on her belt.

They came up out of the deep with almost no sound at all. Orcs — war-painted, disciplined, moving through the Tower's forgotten underworks like men who had been given a map — pouring from a passage that every record said was dead: an old storm-sluice from the years of earlier Lord Protectors, opening onto tunnels beneath the town that everyone had understood to be abandoned. The raiders said nothing of kinship. They said almost nothing at all. They made for the cells with an economy that was its own kind of confession — no looting, no burning, no wasted violence, just a spearhead of picked warriors driving toward one particular door — and the battle met them in the corridor: the party, and @[Darathra](darathra), and @[Nik](nik) with his greataxe, and Edda Vell fighting with a key-ring on her hip and murder in her eye, all of them between the raiders and the prisoner none of the raiders would name.

It came in surges, and the surges kept time with the prisoner. Each time @[Gorvach](gorvach)'s breathing broke its rhythm, the cold walked the room: a Tusk axeman drove at @[Iratius](iratius) and the blade frosted mid-swing, dragging slow, so that the parry that should have been desperate arrived early; @[Saeron](saeron)'s cords stiffened on his forearms like wet rope freezing; breath failed halfway down every throat, and weapons turned treacherous in numbed hands. @[Corvyn](corvyn) learned to call the surges — he could hear them coming in the whispers, a swell in the choir a half-beat before the frost moved — and his warnings carved the fight into survivable pieces. And through all of it, one small wrongness that @[Tirig](tirig) did not mention and the others did not miss: the cold declined, every time, to touch her.

It was close, and hard, and the frost made it stranger — Gorvach's distress rolling off him in waves that rimed the walls and numbed the hands, the whispers rising under the clash of it like a grieving choir. But the raiders had been built for a quiet extraction, not a standing fight, and the corridor was narrow, and the defenders would not break. When the last of them fell, the silence came down hard, and @[Saeron](saeron) knelt among the dead and found what the dead had carried. Their tusks, first: each warrior's tusks ritually bored, hollowed clean through — old, deliberate work, the mark of a clan that was not Gorvach's own, whatever their orders said. And on the raid-captain, folded small and carried dry against the chest, a note in a hand none of them had seen before — not Verity's firm patience but something blunter, squarer, a soldier's devotion pressed into ink:

*“The faithful do not falter at walls. Deliver the frost-bound to his purpose, and let @[the season's rite](the-harvest) conclude unhindered. —Conviction.”*

Another order. Another voice in that same vernacular of the devout — purposes, rites, faith — and another name that was less a name than a virtue wearing one. First Verity. Now Conviction. Somewhere behind the world there was a whole vocabulary of such names, and things kept arriving in Triboar addressed from it.

@audio: none fade=4
And @[Merris Thulane](merris) never came back from her audit.

It assembled itself in the corridor's aftermath, fact by fact, the way the worst truths do. The sluice had not been forced; Edda's inspection found the grate-sequence properly worked, by someone who knew it — and only two people knew it. The fire had been set in the one room whose ashes could hide a tampering: the rotations, the schedules, the record of whose watch had been altered in the days before @[Sera](sera) walked into the wood alone. Tovric's splinter-carved M.T., pressed deep enough to read by touch. The unsanctioned interrogations — the orc, and Sera, in the days before her death. @[Darathra](darathra) stood in the burned office with the whole of it landing on her, a woman discovering that the rot she had been fighting outside her walls had been seated at her own table, wearing her own colors, auditing her own keys. @[The Inquisitor of the Twelve](merris) had burned the Twelve's memory, opened the Twelve's forgotten door, and gone down through it ahead of the raiders she had let in — fled into the abandoned dark beneath the town, toward whatever she served, leaving behind a silenced witness, a murdered colleague's tampered schedule, and a betrayed old woman holding a key-ring in a smoking room.

Which left the door.

The sluice stood open at the bottom of the Tower, breathing cold, wet air up out of the dark — an abandoned throat of tunnels running away under Triboar, where the maps ended and the rumble lived. Because it was louder here. The deep sound that had rolled beneath the whole night was not beneath them anymore so much as before them, down that passage, patient and vast and no longer quite so far away. The party stood at the grate and looked into it the way one looks off a cliff. @[Merris](merris) was down there somewhere. Whatever she served was down there. Whatever @[the Harvest](the-harvest) truly was, the party could feel with animal certainty, it was being readied down there, tonight, beneath the festival lanterns of the town.

*And pressed into the wet mud just past the grate, among the broad boot-prints of the raiders who had come up, there were other prints going down. Small ones. A child's bare feet, unhurried, walking — not dragged, not running, walking — down into the abandoned dark, hours old, alone. @[Gregory](gregory) had found his door before the party ever knew it existed. And somewhere below, the thing that had called him all this time was waiting for the morning, and the morning was the Harvest.*
