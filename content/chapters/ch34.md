# XXXIV. Compliments of the Gentleman
*In which dinner is served for one, and the terms of an old paper are read aloud*

@audio: gray-country fade=6
The invitation was waiting for them two sleeps out from the Vault, on a table. The table requires its own sentence: a table, in the flat gray heart of the Shadowfell, dressed — white cloth, candles burning a civilized gold in a country that renders all flame colorless, six chairs and a seventh drawn up as an afterthought so pointed it could have cut bread — and on the cloth, against the salt cellar, one card. The hand on the card was old-fashioned and vain of its flourishes, and every person present had seen that hand described once, with unusual care, in a Harper's iced and furious letter. The card said: Dinner. Come as you are. The door will keep. And behind the table, freestanding in the gray, without post or lintel or wall to explain it, a door — good oak, brass furniture, respectably polished — stood shut, and waited, and was in no hurry at all, exactly as advertised.

The deliberation was short. Walking through that door with an amnesiac, a nameless grief, and one working plan was either the maddest thing this company has done or the sanest, and the margin is thin. In the door's favor: two papers — a contract in the Hells with a squad's ghost-debt on it and a contract in a glutton's drawer with a gnome's whole life in it — an appointment made in a glade, two papers, one fire, and the party keeps its appointments. A coin — somewhere in that creature's keeping, eleven men @[Iratius](iratius) had been mourning uninterrupted for fifteen years. And a name — folded in @[Saeron](saeron)'s breast pocket beside an iron key, a true name with unknown weight, which is the only kind of weapon that grows heavier the longer you decline to use it. Against the door: everything else. Iratius's vote carried, because it was his deadline that had broken and his hunt that had ended and his family in the vault; and @[Sov](sov) — who had taken his own name back on the road and wore it loosely, like a borrowed coat — ended the discussion about whether he, of all of them, should wait outside: “I have had enough,” he said, mildly, “of waiting rooms.” They knocked. Of course they knocked. @[Gorvach](gorvach) was not there, and somebody had to represent his position that doors have manners.

@audio: gentlemans-table fade=6
The far side of the door was warm. That was the first wrongness, and the kindest. They came through into an evening — a terrace evening, sky the color of banked embers from horizon to horizon, because the sky was Avernus and the embers were not a sunset — onto the grounds of @[an estate](the-hells-estate) that sat above the burning plains the way a good house sits above its own vineyards: gravel walks raked to the grain, black topiary in geometric patience, a long ornamental water in which the ember-light lay perfectly still, and at the end of the walks a house of taste — and precisely that; not a fortress, not a lair: a manor — proportioned, restrained, old — the kind of architecture that money cannot buy and time cannot rush, standing in the exact center of damnation like a signature at the bottom of it. Somewhere beyond the grounds, at a distance kept deliberately audible, the plains did what the plains do. On the grounds themselves, nothing was out of place. Nothing has been out of place there, the party understood, for a very long time, and everything underfoot agreed to it.

He received them on the steps, and they saw him plainly at last — after a year of his hand in other people's margins, and one warm decanter at a masquerade: a gentleman. Tall in the way of men who have never once hurried; dressed forty years out of fashion and entirely correct, in a deep wine coat with cuffs that took candlelight like signatures take ink; silver at the temples of a face that was handsome, and kind-eyed, and had been practicing both for centuries; and hands — the party all marked the hands — kept, manicured, expressive, the hands of a man who has never lifted anything heavier than paper, and never needed to. “You came,” he said, and his delight was, horrifically, genuine. “People so rarely come when invited. They come when compelled, which is duller for everyone. Please.” And he stood aside from his own door like a man sharing a private box at the opera, and the sword on @[Iratius](iratius)'s back, wrapped and peace-bonded and silent for two planes, went warm as a held hand.

Dinner was served, and nobody ate it. Not refused — nobody offered the courtesies of refusal, and nobody was pressed — the courses simply arrived, and shone, and cooled, seven untouched plates going down the table like unstruck bells, while at its head their host dined. Alone. Beautifully. He ate the way some men duel, with an economy that has spectators built into it, and he did not once glance at their plates, because the plates were not the point; the point was the demonstration, conducted in silverware, that comfort in that house was his alone to feel, and that he felt it. Midway through the second course he rose, took a bottle from his own sideboard, did a thing with a cloth and a knife and the cork that turned pouring into calligraphy, and set one glass — one — at the table's center where nobody could be said to own it. “A red, with my compliments. You enjoyed one very like it once, at a masquerade. I sent it to your table. You never asked whose compliments they were.” He poured for a doomed town through a sommelier's manners, and he has waited a hundred and seventy-six pages to tell them, and I have waited a hundred and seventy-six pages to let him.

The paper came out with the third course, the way another host might bring out the good stories. A single instrument, aged to the color of tea, unfolded beside his plate and read aloud in the voice of a man reading a beloved label: the transport commission; the delivery deadline; and the clause — he looked up here, pleasantly, to be sure of the room — the clause providing that upon breach, the obligation passes to the surviving obligor, in service, in person, in perpetuity or at the creditor's pleasure, whichever endures. “The deadline,” he said, folding the paper with two exact creases, “broke at the fifth bell of the Feast of the Moon. You have been mine, @[Sir Iratius](iratius), for thirteen days now. I simply preferred to collect you rested.” And into the silence that followed he selected a grape, and considered it, swallowing it whole.

@[Iratius](iratius) came out of his chair — and their host said, “Sit.” Not loudly. The way you say it to a good dog whose training you trust. And the worst sentence of that whole dinner is two words long. Iratius sat. His body folded him back into the chair between one heartbeat and the next, smooth as a servant's bow, while his face — and this is the part the party could not look at and could not look away from — arrived a half-second late, understanding what had already been done with him. The pact obeys its holder. The party's hands were on steel, all of them, chairs scraping — and the master of the house waved them down with two fingers, mild as a grandfather. “Manners,” he said. “We are at dinner. Nothing at this table will be done to anyone that the paper does not permit.” Which was, they would all reflect later, the single most honest and most terrible sentence he spoke all night.

The talk resumed, because he resumed it. He rose from his chair the way a cat rises from a windowsill — unhurried, certain of the room — slinking another grape into his mouth, and gestured with poise at nothing and everything around them. “What do you think of my collection?” he said, leaving no room in it for any true answer. He stepped over to @[Iratius](iratius), and appraised. “The clause matured at an excellent hour,” he remarked to the table at large, regarding him over the rim of his glass. “I confess that when it did, I was pleased on more counts than the ledger's. Look at him. Fifteen years of grief under discipline. Do you know what that does to the bearing? Most assets slouch.” And, later, gentler, worse: “A handsome thrall is not necessary. It is merely preferred.” Nobody at the table moved. Moving was what he was pouring for. And from his waistcoat, as if the thought had reminded him, he produced a @[coin the size of a doorknocker](the-doorknocker-coin) and the color of a cold hearth, and set it by his plate the way other men set a snuffbox. “Your family, incidentally, banks with me.” Iratius's hand started across the cloth before his mind could stop it. “Ah,” said their host — just that — and the hand stopped, and the coin went back into the waistcoat, against the beat of a heart that does not; and Iratius of the Ember Door counted eleven worn faces in his memory of one glance — the men he has only ever called his brothers; nobody at that table, in a whole year of roads, had once heard him say soldiers — and said, in a voice filed entirely flat: “We were twelve.” “You were,” their host agreed, warmly. “The twelfth is otherwise instrumentalized. Waste,” he added, to the whole table, confiding, “is the only sin I recognize.”

With the cheese he sent for a portfolio, “since we are doing accounts,” and did his accounts — thumbed the season's commissions with the licked-finger contentment of a squire at his rents, reading names aloud not for the party's benefit but for his own pleasure, which was worse: “The Bellhammer annuity, performing. The widow of Yartar's second face — renewed, vain thing. The choir at Mornbryn's Shield, in perpetuity, poor lambs. The @[Heart of Winter](the-heart-of-winters-veil)'s Veil —” flat, unstressed, one name among grocery — “procurement; executed; a client's matter; closed.” And on down the page, while at the table's far side a huntress who steals for commission sat with her blood arriving at a conclusion her face refused to publish. He rose while the page turned; poured, unasked, a second glass of the masquerade red; and set it at @[Tirig](tirig)'s elbow with a small bow. “You enjoyed this once, at a dance, before a winter of consequences. It pairs well with retrospect.” She did not touch it. He did not remove it. It stood at her elbow the rest of the night like a hand on a shoulder. And @[Saeron](saeron) found himself watching the hand that turned those pages rather than the names on them: the signatures wheeling by, the same proud flourishes over and over; and something filed itself away wrong in him, like a word misheard through a wall, and would not say what it was.

@[Iratius](iratius) spoke once, low, into his untouched plate — the first words he had spent since the clause was read. “My brothers kept a better table than this in a tent in the rain. Collect whatever you own of me. You will find it is less than you paid for.” Their host let the sentence finish. Something moved under the kind face — a seam of heat, there and mastered, like a coal turning over in a banked grate — and when he spoke again, the pleasantness had acquired edges. “Kneel.” And the body of the best knight any of them had ever followed went down beside his own chair onto the beautiful floor, plates-level, eyes-level with the candle flames, and was kept there — not spoken to; kept, like a hat on a peg — while the conversation continued over him for the length of an entire course, and the heat under the kind face banked itself back down to warmth, appeased, sweetened by the watching. Pleased, @[Veyran](veyran) slid a manicured finger over Iratius's cheek, up his temple, and down to the tip of one pointed horn, before walking back to his seat and reclining into it with newfound arrogance. The party made themselves watch, all six, all course, because looking away felt like leaving him alone down there, and this company does not leave people alone down there. We were meant, at our table, to hate him at leisure. Take your time. He did.

@[Corvyn](corvyn) broke first, and no one who was there begrudged him it, then or ever. He came up out of his chair with the fiddle-hand pointing, and his voice cracked on the first word and steadied on all the rest. “Leave him be. This is not collection — it is appetite. I have watched appetite eat a whole glade gray, and it is never full. Let him up.” And their host turned upon him a face of purest delight, the face of a man whose evening has finally been asked for by name. “The artist objects,” he said. “Good. Objection is a vintage too.” He signaled — at nothing; no servant came, because nothing in that house is employed; it is owned — and from the dark of the wine-wall a small bottle lifted itself, and drifted the length of the room, and set itself down, precisely, before Corvyn's place. And it was not gray. It was the opposite of gray: a @[small bottle shining from within](the-shining-bottle) — honey-and-morning, festival-colored, glowing the way the glade does not — because this, exactly this, was what had been taken; the gray was never in the bottle; the gray is what a place is, after the bottle. “A first performance,” he read, in the voice of a man in love with his own cellar. “Boy soprano and fiddle, house of mourning, north country. Sweet, with bitter notes —” and here the voice did the thing the party would be years scrubbing from their ears: it went small, and soft, and sorry — a nursery voice, a voice for skinned knees, wet with borrowed sorrow — “the joy of a little child who believed — oh, and he did believe it so — that he had cost his family a son, playing anyway, dedicating it, in the silence under the applause, to the brother he thought he had spent. His poor, poor parents wept straight through it.” The voice folded itself away, satisfied. “I confess I have never opened it. Some things improve unopened.” And Corvyn Stonemason's-son stood there with his whole childhood shining on a table in the Hells, priced, cellared, described to strangers — and it was the party's hands, @[Amyl](amyl)'s and @[Sov](sov)'s, one on each arm, that drew him gently, gently back down into his chair; and the bottle stayed standing before him the rest of the night, twin to the glass at Tirig's elbow, two small monuments to the evening's single lesson, which is that everything they had ever felt had a price, and he had paid it, and kept the receipts.

Late — the candles low, the plains outside at their deliberate audible distance — he looked down the table at the kneeling man as though remembering a pleasure deferred. “Ah! Poor thing. How could I have left you down there so long — in such a vulnerable position?” A smile slid across half his face, fanged. “Get more comfortable.” Another snap. “Here,” he said, and pointed to his own lap. @[Iratius](iratius) rose slowly, an ache in his knees, and came up the length of the table, step by compelled step, and stopped at the arm of the host's chair. “Now, now. Closer,” Veyran said, in an unvoiced whisper. Iratius leaned in, closer, for what felt like a pregnant minute — and the host raised one manicured finger, and tilted the paladin's chin with it, turning the face slightly to the candlelight like a man verifying a hallmark; and leaned in himself, unhurried, lips perfectly pursed, to the very border of the next thing — and the room stopped, all of it, the party and the staff and the flames — and he held it there. Held it. His eye slid across the room to the table of guests, and the pursed lips allowed themselves a fanged grin. And then he released the chin with the smallest push of the finger and smiled, not at Iratius — at the table, at all of them, taking the temperature of their hatred like a cellarman — and said: “Iratius! Not in front of guests. Anticipation is the better part of every vintage, and I have waited far too long to spoil it now. We are going to have so much time, and so much fun, together.” And the record, which has described monsters for two hundred and forty pages, files him above all of them, and expects no appeal.

Another snap, and @[Iratius](iratius) winced as the compelling let him go. “We will finish the paperwork at breakfast,” their host said, rising, the perfect host once more, the coat of the man sitting perfectly. “The house will show you your rooms — yes, all of you; take him with you.” A little wave, generous. “Return him rested; he will need the rest. I am a patient investor, and the term, after all” — the kind eyes went once more to Iratius, warmly, terribly — “is in perpetuity.”

The night belonged to the party, and it kept two things. @[Iratius](iratius)'s confession, given standing, because he would not sit while he said it: that he had known since the barrow there would be a price for the broken deadline, and had let himself believe it would come with horns and armies, something a man can fight; and that he had walked them all into this house knowing there was a clause and not its shape; and that he was sorry — said once, to each of them, by name, going around the room, while nobody breathed. And the last hour, in which nobody planned anything at all, and @[Amyl](amyl) made tea with his own leaves over a candle, and they drank it in silence in a guest room in the Hells — seven cups, because @[Sov](sov) poured the seventh for himself and filled it mostly full; and if a man wearing his own death takes his tea a little short of the brim, nobody said anything about that either.

The two lights of that world do not rise; the embers simply insist. Toward what passed for dawn, the party stopped pretending to sleep, and dressed, and did the thing this company does at thresholds: they looked at one another, one at a time, all the way around, the way he tied knots, the way she counts her people. Then they went down to breakfast.
