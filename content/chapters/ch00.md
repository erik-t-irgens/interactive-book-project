# Prologue — The Grey Between

@audio: gray-dream fade=8
*— as someone, somewhere, dreams it —*

*The dream again.*

*I know it before I have finished falling into it: the flat gray of it, the cold that is not cold, the hush like a held breath that never ends. No horizon. No floor worth the name. Only the fog, and the pale distance, and me.*

*There is never anyone in it. I have walked it — whole nights of it, though there is nowhere in it to go — and I have never found a wall, a door, a voice. If it wants something from me, it has never once troubled itself to say so. It is an empty room the size of the world, and some nights I could scream at the waste of it: all that space, and nothing done with it.*

*I do not grant it meaning. Dreams are the mind sweeping its floor, and those of us who live close to the dark sweep up more shadow than most. That is all this is. I have work — more work than any one life should be asked to hold, and worth more than sleep, and it will outlast every soul who ever doubted me. I begrudge this place the hours it steals. I have learned to leave it: to fix my mind on the morning's first task and pull myself up it, hand over hand, into waking.*

*And yet. Some nights, lately — I will not call it a feeling, because it is not one — there is a direction to the emptiness. A quarter where the gray hangs heavier. As if the room were about to have someone else in it.*

*It never does. I wake, and I do not think of it again.*
