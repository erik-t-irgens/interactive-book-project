# What the Archive Kept

@audio: threshold fade=5

Winter closed the river, and Harrow's Ferry closed with it. @[Silda](silda) let the town believe she spent her days drying out tax rolls. Some days she even did. The courthouse clerk brought her tea and gossip in roughly equal measure, and from the gossip she assembled her second ledger: who in town would not walk the bell-hill path after dark, and which families had held that reluctance the longest.

Three names came up again and again. All three, she noted, had once held the office of @[Warden](warden) — a title the town remembered as ceremonial, a sash worn at festivals, though no one could say what it had been a sash *for*.

## The Sixty-Second Step

@audio: undercroft fade=6

She went back down in the dark of the year, when the town slept early and asked few questions. This time she counted past the first shelf. Sixty-one steps to the bell. At the sixty-second, the stairway changed under her boots from courthouse brick to a stone she had no name for — grey-black, faintly warm, and worked so finely that her lantern found no seams at all.

@unlock: undercroft.2

The shelves below were not empty. They were *waiting*. Row upon row of them, cut from the same seamless stone, receded past the reach of her light, and each held a shallow depression the size of a cupped palm — thousands of small absences, arranged with the patience of a library that had lent out its every volume and expected, someday, each one returned.

In the third row, one depression was filled. A disc of the black metal, twin to the bell's, lay in its socket. When her lantern touched it, the disc did not reflect the flame. It reflected — she checked twice, wrote the observation, underlined nothing — a different room.

The knock came on the fourth night after, at the door above the chandler's, at the hour when even the river ice was quiet. The man on her landing was old in the way of trees rather than men, and he wore, over his winter coat, a sash so faded its color had become a rumor.

"You've been down," said the @[Warden](warden). It was not a question, and he did not sound angry. He sounded like a man who had been waiting a very long time for someone to talk to.

@unlock: warden.2

"I have," said Silda. She stood aside from her door. "I kept notes."

For the first time in the acquaintance of anyone living, the Warden of Harrow's Ferry smiled. "Then you're the one the letter was for," he said, and stepped inside, and began — slowly, the way the deep rooms breathed — to tell her what the hill was a lid *on*.
