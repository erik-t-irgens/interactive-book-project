# XXXVII. The Session
*In which the party attends upon the government of the enemy, and finds it governing*

@audio: fundament-doubled fade=6
The summons came with breakfast, on paper the color of cream and weight of consequence, in the square devout hand the party now knew from a customs ledger: The @[Session of the Consensus](the-session) convenes at the third bell. The presence of the seven late of the North Ride is requested in the gallery. No signature. Titles, the @[archivist](the-archivist) explained, collecting their cups, do not sign invitations; invitations are the city's, and the city needs no signature. The deliberation lasted the length of the porridge. They had crossed two planes to reach the table of their enemy, and the table had, with breakfast, politely requested them; there are doors you knock on and doors that knock first, and this one had knocked. “Wear what you have,” the archivist advised, at the threshold. “The Session does not dress. It is one of the things they agree on.”

She walked them up through the civic quarter, and gave them, on the way, the government — methodically, in the tone of a good guide who has given this lesson to every intake and refined it to the bone. The Consensus is six. Each of the six is an office, not a person; the person fills it, the way a hand fills a glove, and the office has a function, and the function has a domain. The Diplomat speaks to what is outside; the Seeker learns what is unknown; the Priest keeps what is holy; the Warrior holds what is far; the Enforcer orders what is near; the Oracle sees what is coming. “They do not rule,” she said, and the party noted the precision with which she chose it. “Ruling is a claim. They handle. Each is given a domain to handle, and answers to the table for the handling. A matter comes before the Session; the six speak, each from their function; and when they agree — only when they agree — the Consensus has spoken, and the thing is done. Until they agree, there is no Consensus. There are only six people at a table, which the founders,” and here the smallest pause, a cataloguer's pause, “considered the point.” @[Amyl](amyl) asked who the founders were. “Patient people,” said the archivist, and walked on.

@audio: session-chamber fade=8
The Session sits in a righted hall of the elder city — of course it does; the party was beginning to understand that Fundament keeps its power where it keeps its questions, in buildings that predate the asking — a long vaulted chamber of the pale seamless stone, its upper reaches lost in a soft standing dusk that both lights entered and neither claimed, its floor relaid in new warm timber that met the ancient walls the way a ship's deck meets the sea: humbly, and built to be replaced. There was no throne. There was a table — long, dark, honest wood, old enough to have opinions — with six chairs down its sides, three and three; and at its head, where the light from the high apertures fell whitest, a @[seventh chair](the-seventh-chair), plainer than the rest, empty. Nobody sat in it. Nobody, in the whole of the session, so much as brushed it in passing; the traffic of clerks and papers bent around it like water around a stone; and more than once, at the difficult turns of the morning's business, the party watched one member or another glance toward it — briefly, involuntarily, the way the devout glance at an altar from the far end of a church — and then return, recalibrated, to the matter at hand. The gallery ran along the western wall, raised three steps, railed in new wood. The party was shown to it with courtesy and no ceremony at all, and the city's government came in.

They came in like weather fronts, and the party read them the way they have learned this year to read a room that matters. The @[Enforcer of Truth](verity) came first, because order arrives early: a human man of iron middle age in a plain field coat, who walked the room's perimeter once, checking sightlines out of a habit older than his office, before taking his seat — and @[Saeron](saeron), in the gallery, went very still, watching his hands square his papers to the table's edge, because he had studied that hand's work by candlelight in a wool-house, and the squared corners of a folded note, and the party understood without a word passed among them that the man below had written There you are, and that the next table — this table — had indeed already been laid. The @[Diplomat of Truth](clarity) arrived talking, a tall tiefling in dove gray whose horns were capped in silver filigree and whose warmth reached the gallery before her footsteps did, greeting clerks by name, laying a hand on a shoulder here and there, spending charm the way a careful banker spends interest and never principal. And the party knew her. She had come to Silverymoon in the winter — a caller in the days before the Heartsilver performance, and a guest of honor at it — and she had given her name then, with a small deprecating smile, as a virtue: Clarity; and they had filed it as missionary fashion, and liked her, because she had been warm, and attentive, and in every hour they spent near her, entirely sincere. Watching her take the Diplomat of Truth's chair, they understood the smile at last: she had not been wearing an affectation. She had been wearing a title. And the sincerity they had liked her for was not softened by the office one degree — which is, the party was coming to understand, the most dangerous thing about this entire city: it means every word of it. The @[Priest of Truth](certitude) came robed, an aasimar of grave and dreadful gentleness with eyes like a winter dawn, who paused at his chair to touch the table — not lean on it; touch it, two fingers, the way one touches a reliquary — before sitting. The @[Seeker of Truth](revelation) arrived reading, a half-elf in scholar's black gone gray at the cuffs, thin as a bookmark, trailed by two clerks carrying instrument cases, who looked up from his papers exactly once on the way to his seat — at the gallery, at the seven of them, one long assessing look over the spectacles, and back down. The @[Warrior of Truth](conviction) came in travel-stained, a dwarf built like a gate, with the North still on his boots and a rime-burn across one cheekbone that had not finished healing, who sat down the way tired soldiers sit and put his helmet on the table in front of him like a deadline. And last, without sound, in robes the gray of the country the party had lately crossed, came the @[Oracle of Truth](the-oracle); and the seven late of the North Ride stopped breathing, all together, in the gallery, with one identical discipline; because they knew him. Gaunt, gray-eyed, deliberate; the face that had done the expensive kind of nothing at a milestone in the dark; the voice that had taught them the manners of a country and left them at its door. Their guide. He crossed to the last chair without one glance at the gallery, and sat, and folded his hands; and in the whole of the session that followed he looked at the party exactly twice, and both times it was the way a man looks at a clock he already trusts.

@[The Diplomat](clarity) opened by welcoming them, which was its own small masterpiece of the morning. She rose, and turned to the gallery, and gave the chamber the seven of them in the doctrine's own grammar: survivors of the Sharrans' harvest in the North; walkers of the gray road; guests of the Deliverance's city, seated in its Session by its invitation — “and proof,” she said, warm as a hearth and precise as a scalpel, “that what the Lady of Loss unmade, mercy can carry home.” The gallery's other occupants — clerks, petitioners, two ward-captains waiting on the housing item — turned to look at them with the open kindly interest this city gives its rescued. And the party inclined their heads and said nothing, and swallowed it, every syllable, publicly, because the terrain came first and the argument against a story that is almost right cannot be won from a gallery; and @[Iratius](iratius)'s jaw, @[Tirig](tirig) reported later, could have cracked walnuts.

The business of the morning was housing, and it was thorough, and it was — the party kept arriving at the word and kept distrusting it — good. The next intake was discussed the way harvests are discussed in provident towns: how many, how soon, which wards had capacity, which wards claimed capacity out of pride and would be checked. Ward-captains were called to report. And the second of them came to the table with a slate under her arm and two runners waiting at the door, compact and iron-gray and comprehensively unimpressed, and gave her numbers in the clipped complete sentences of a woman who has run musters her whole life; and it was @[Darathra Shendrel](darathra). The party's row of the gallery became, for several seconds, a study in the art of not standing up. She did not look at them while she reported — beds, cisterns, the roof timbers wanted for the eastern dormitory, a shortage of nails she pursued up the table with the tenacity of a border dispute — and when the Enforcer had granted the nails and dismissed her with visible respect, she gathered her slate, turned, and walked the gallery's length on her way out; and passing the seven of them her eyes crossed theirs for exactly one stride — one — with everything in them, the whole graveyard and the ink and the season of mourning and the arithmetic of sudden hope, pressed flat behind the Lord Protector's parade face; and what she did was tap the rail once, twice, with two fingers, without breaking step — the old Triboar watch-sign, the party would swear to it, for hold your post; I will come to you — and was gone through the door with her runners falling in behind her.

They argued liturgy next, and the party learned more about the city in twenty minutes of it than in two days of streets. The Priest had brought a wording. The autumn rite of the Deliverance — the party gathered it is the city's high feast — names the saved, and the Priest wished the formula amended: not the carried, his proposed text ran, but the chosen. He made the case the way winter makes ice, slowly and totally: mercy that merely carries is a ferry; mercy that chooses is a love; the city's gratitude should know it was wanted. And the Diplomat opposed him with both hands flat on the table, and her warmth banked down to coals: “Chosen builds a second city inside the first, @[Certitude](certitude). A city of everyone whose mother was not chosen. I have sat with those families. Write chosen into the rite and I will spend the next ten years explaining to the unchosen's children why the Deliverance loved their neighbors better — and so will you, at every deathbed in your district.” @[The Seeker](revelation), without looking up: “The carried implies capacity constraints. Accurate.” @[The Warrior](conviction): “Say saved and be done. Nobody weeps over saved.” It went four rounds. @[The Oracle](the-oracle) said one sentence — “The word will matter more in a year than it does today” — which settled nothing and stopped the argument dead; and the item was tabled, and the party sat in the gallery having watched the machinery of the lie maintained with total sincerity by six people who believed they were quarreling about kindness; and that, more than the strait, more than the panes, is the thing this chapter cannot make comfortable, because there is no argument in the world harder to watch than a good one held inside a false premise.

The Avernus item was brief and instructive. A liaison clerk — mortal, sweating, escorted — presented sealed invoices: transit sureties, the schedule of instrument maintenance, a renewal rider on services the docket named only as procurement, standing. Nobody at the table touched the papers barehanded; @[the Priest](certitude) handled them with a cloth, distaste performed as ritual; the Warrior said, to no one and everyone, “I have @[buried better creditors](conviction),” and was not reproved. The Enforcer processed the item in nine flat sentences and had it off the table like something scraped from a boot. And one small thing rode under it, and the party caught it because they were listening for it: the clerk, asked after the counterparty's schedule, allowed that the principal had lately suffered “a reversal, of standing and of face, in his own demesne — the terms of service are unaffected” — and across the chamber, for the breadth of half a second, @[the Enforcer](verity)'s eyes came up from his papers to the gallery, to the seven survivors of the North seated in it, and went back down; and nothing in his face moved at all; and the party understood that at least one person at that table did not believe a single syllable of the story the Diplomat had welcomed them with.

@unlock: the-harvest.4
Then @[the Seeker](revelation) stood, and the morning arrived at its point, and the party understood at last why the paper with their invitation on it had come with breakfast. “The @[Ascore](ascore) anchor,” he said, and the chamber quieted by a degree the other items had not commanded. Instruments came out of the cases; a chart went up on a stand: a coastline of the nothing, and a point inland of the far shore, in the desert the party had not yet seen, marked with a sigil older than the hand that had copied it. He gave the table the readings in the flat voice of a man carrying bad numbers a long way: the far anchor — the original, he said, and said it the way masons say bedrock — was failing. Drift in the harmonics. Shear along the fade. “The seam is held,” he said, “by a very old agreement between very few points. If Ascore fails, the strait does not widen politely. It bucks. The harbor quarter stands on the fade. I am not prepared to model the morning after, and I am the person in this room who models mornings after.” Silence took the table. The Warrior said what warriors say — his people were holding the North with both hands and no fingers to spare. The Priest said the rite could be sung for the anchor, and the Seeker said, with the courtesy of long practice, that the anchor did not attend services. And into the gap that opened — into it, the party would think later, with the smoothness of a thing rehearsed — @[the Diplomat](clarity) turned her face up to the gallery, and smiled with genuine and terrible warmth, and said: “It happens that the city is host, this week, to seven persons of demonstrated capability in exactly this wilderness of the world — the seven who found the anchor the Sharrans had corrupted beneath @[Triboar](triboar) and broke its working, and who stood, at cost, against the greater instrument that, to this city's enduring grief, met its mark. The Session is aware of its guests, and grateful to them. As guests. As friends of the Deliverance. The Session could ask.” And the party sat very still under the strange vertigo of hearing their own true deeds read back to them accurately, warmly, and inside a story that made every one of them a chapter of the lie.

The party conferred in the gallery with their heads together and their voices under the rail, and the record of that conference is short because the conference was. The stakes below were real: they had stood in the harbor quarter; the harbor quarter had a fish market and a school and a laugh in it that they knew. And the intelligence was priceless, because for the first time the word anchor had arrived with its family. They had met such stones only ruined: the black thing in the cave under Triboar that rewrote living flesh until @[Sera](sera)'s blood changed it; the greater working that took a town entire; the shattered heart of a mirror in a wool-house cellar. Corrupted, an anchor pulls — hauls the Shadowfell up close against the hull of the world, the way a fouled mooring hauls a ship onto its own rocks. Which meant — @[Aelendir](aelendir) said it under the rail, in six words, and @[Amyl](amyl) finished it — that the thing uncorrupted must be the opposite of everything they had fought: a @[Mooring](mooring-stones), in the oldest sense; a stone that keeps the planes riding apart at their proper distances, each at its own safe water. An original. The elder work itself, opened to them by the enemy's own invitation — the thing Ascore could teach them about stones that hold against stones that take might be worth more than every sword in the city. And @[Tirig](tirig) sat through the whole of the deliberation with her hand at her bare throat, where a crystal had once leaned north in a closed fist, wondering a thing she did not say aloud. And the trap, if it was one, was at least a trap with honest walls. Aelendir said it for all of them, very low: “We said we owed the next thing we did the truth of this place. The truth of this place is anchored at Ascore.” @[Iratius](iratius), whose counsel has gotten quieter and has not gotten worse, added the soldier's clause: “And if we are doing the enemy's maintenance, then the enemy needed maintenance, and that is worth knowing too.” They stood. Amyl spoke for the seven, briefly, with the plain courtesy he keeps for tribunals: the party would go.

What followed was the only moment of the session the party could not read, and it is set down here exactly as it happened. The @[Consensus](the-session) voted. Each office spoke its assent in a formula evidently older than the room's new floor — the Diplomat: “Spoken for the outside”; the Seeker: “Spoken for the unknown”; the Priest: “Spoken for the kept”; the Warrior: “Spoken for the far”; the Enforcer, last but one, looking at the party and not at the table: “Spoken for the near” — and then the @[Oracle of Truth](the-oracle), their guide of the gray country, the man whose office ends at a door none of the six had named all morning, rose for the first time in three hours, and looked at the gallery, at the seven of them, directly, deliberately, in front of the entire chamber; and inclined his head. Once. Like a colleague. “Spoken,” he said, “for what is coming.” And sat down; and the Consensus had spoken; and not one person in that room save seven understood that anything unusual had happened at all.

The session closed with the reading of the day's rulings into the city's register, and the chamber emptied the way working rooms empty, all papers and shoulder-claps and the scrape of benches, and the @[seventh chair](the-seventh-chair) stood in the whitening light at the table's head, untouched, unexplained, and the party filed out past it along the gallery rail; and @[Certitude the Priest of Truth](certitude) was the last of the six to leave the floor, and before he left it he turned to the empty chair and bowed to it — fully, formally, from the waist, the bow of a man alone with his altar who does not know he is watched — and the party, watching, added it to the ledger of this city's questions, which was growing, and which every one of them was privately beginning to suspect had a single answer.

@audio: fundament-doubled fade=6
@[Darathra](darathra) was waiting for them in the colonnade, at parade rest beside a pillar, exactly where the watch-sign had promised. Up close the season showed: new gray over her ears, the vambraces still on, a thinness at the jaw — and none of it had touched the spine. She looked at the seven of them for a long moment, one at a time, counting, the way she counted her town out of a burning year; and then she said, in the voice she keeps for musters, pitched for six feet and no further: “Supper. Tomorrow. My ward, the captain's house; the runners know it. Bring your appetites and nothing you would not show a customs officer.” And then, because she is Darathra, the parade face opened for the width of one sentence — “I mourned you. All seven. I am extremely angry that it was wasted, and I expect to hear everything” — and closed again, and she was away down the colonnade with her runners at heel, a Lord Protector in a city that had misplaced her title and not one ounce of her office.

They walked back to their lodging through the failing gold of the afternoon, past the shrine at the five-way corner where the flowers had been changed again, and took the stock of the day on their own stairs, quietly, in the manner that has become their custom in this city where the walls are old and the questions older. They had sat in the enemy's government and found it diligent. They had watched the lie argued over like a beloved child's schooling, by people who did not know it was a lie, before an empty chair that outranked them all. They had been claimed by the doctrine in public and had let it stand. They had taken the enemy's commission, to save the enemy's city, which was full of their own people, who did not know they were anyone's. And a man who had walked them through the country of the dead had told a room full of power, in a language only seven of them spoke, that he saw them, and that what was coming was theirs. “We came to fight a fortress,” @[Saeron](saeron) said at last, to the stairwell in general, working the wrapping on his ribs. “I would have preferred the fortress.” Nobody laughed, because it was not a joke; and below them, out the stair window, the two lights lay down together along the harbor, and the ferries rode the fade with their paired and patient lamps, and somewhere in the eastern quarter a crew was still at work by lantern on the roof timbers of a dormitory for people who had not yet lost everything, and did not know they were about to be saved.
