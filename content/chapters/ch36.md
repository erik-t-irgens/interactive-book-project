# XXXVI. Fundament
*In which the party arrives at the capital of the enemy, and finds it is a home*

Morning in the gray country is a rumor the sleepers agree on, and the party woke into it slowly, in the camp they had made at the first milestone: the small fire @[Amyl](amyl) kept alive out of stubbornness more than heat; the two fistfuls of ash in their oilskin, which had not settled in the night, and stirred when the fire stirred, like a thing keeping time; the violin in its case at @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s side, only wood now, and heavier for it; the small @[shining bottle](the-shining-bottle) wrapped beside it, which lit the inside of the pack faintly when opened, like a coal that had opinions. And @[Iratius](iratius) sat a little apart with his hands held out toward the flames. Nobody remarked on it. He had never once done it, in a year of winter roads, and now he did it the way everyone else had always done it, because his mornings were cold now, and would be, and the fire was only a fire, and he was learning what everything costs when nothing is subsidized. @[Sov](sov) had the last watch. When the others rose he was standing at the camp's edge with his tea going cold in his hand, looking out across the flat gray at the one thing in all that country with light in its hands.

They walked toward it for the better part of three days, and the country changed under them by inches — the flat gray taking on grain, then give, then gravel; the milestones arriving at honest intervals; their path finding the caravan road and becoming easy, and the ease its own instruction. Wagons passed them, heavy-laden, colorless lanterns swinging, and the second driver raised two fingers off the reins in the universal salute of the road, and after a moment the party returned it, and walked on for a while in silence, digesting the fact that the underworld had teamsters, and the teamsters had manners.

@audio: none fade=8
On the third day the light stopped being a direction and became a place, and the road rose onto a causeway of fitted stone, and the causeway ran out toward the brightness — and ended. Not at a gate. At a shore. The party stood at the rail of the last stretch and looked out at what the maps of no plane have a color for: @[a strait](the-strait). A band of — the word the party settled on, after trying several, was nothing, and they used it precisely — running left and right to the limits of sight, between the gray country behind them and the doubled brightness ahead. It was not water. It was not air, or fog, or dark. It was the place where two worlds met at their exact edges and disagreed — where the world's low warm hum and the shadow's endless hush ran into each other at full strength and canceled, the way two waves precisely opposed leave a flat calm that is not stillness but war — and what remained was a band of un-place with a tide in it, a slow visible breathing of the boundary itself, that the eye could not rest on and the ear heard as a pressure, and into which the causeway's last stones simply went, and stopped mattering. And out across the band, toward the city's brightness, the nothing changed. It took on sheen; then texture; then — the eye insisted, and after a long minute the party believed it — water. Honest water, lying against the city's distant feet in a broad bright band, as though the world resumed by degrees the nearer you came to the thing built in its wound. The strait was not a river with two banks. It was a fade, and the city stood at the far, finished end of it. @[Aelendir](aelendir) stood at that shore a long time. “It is a seam,” he said at last, quietly, in the voice he saves for true things. “We have been calling the city that for half a book, and I thought we were being poetic. Look at it. The worlds are sewn shut here, and the stitching failed, or was picked — and something has built its house in the gap.”

The harbor sat where the causeway met the shore: stone quays, mooring posts, lamp-standards burning steady, and a queue — wagons, walkers, drovers with their animals blindfolded and calm — waiting under the lamps for the ferries. The ferries deserve their sentence. Broad, low, timbered dark with age and scrupulously kept, each rigged with two great lanterns, one at the bow and one at the stern — and the bow lamp burned pale, the color of the gray country's day, and the stern lamp burned deep gold, the color of the light across the water, so that each boat carried, fore and aft, a small honest copy of both worlds it served. The ferrymen were quiet men and women in oiled wool who moved with the economy of people who do a dangerous thing so routinely it has become a kind of politeness. They counted the passengers aboard. Then they counted them again. Nobody made a joke about it. And as the lines were slipped, the near ferryman said the only thing anyone official said on the whole crossing, in the tone of a man reciting something older than his employment: “Hold something. Not the rail — the rail is ours. Something of yours.”

The crossing took the length of a held breath and lasted, by @[Amyl](amyl)'s count of his own heartbeats, the better part of an hour, and both of those are true, and nobody aboard has ever reconciled them. In the strait there was no gray country and no golden city; there were the two lamps, and the boat, and the passengers, each gone silent, each — the party looked, and then looked away, because it was like watching people pray — holding something of their own: a locket, a hat brim, a child's hand, the worn place on a walking stick. @[Tirig](tirig)'s fingers went to her bare throat, and found the old absence there, and held that. @[Iratius](iratius) held his empty hands, one in the other. @[Sov](sov) stood at the rail with the cold coming faintly off the seam in his chest, like a window in winter, and held nothing, and watched the nothing go by with the expression of a man being shown a country he has already visited. And then, halfway over, sound returned beneath them — a whisper first, then a lap, then the honest slap of water on timber, the world arriving back under the hull by degrees — and every shoulder on that ferry came down at once, in one unconscious motion, like a held chord resolving. The gold lamp's light stopped being a lamp's and became a sky's, and the hush let go of the boat the way a wave sets a swimmer down, and the far harbor took them in on real water, under real gulls, to the knock of a real wooden quay.

They came up out of the ferry into the city, and the city, at last, at close range, resolved — and the party's first understanding of @[Fundament](fundament), before the markets and the bells and the doubled light, was this: it is old. Not old the way Silverymoon is old, storied and tended; old the way the Vault had been old — the causeways they walked on were poured or grown or persuaded by no craft any of them could name, in a pale seamless stone that took both lights strangely; the towers went up at a scale the eye kept trying to file as geography; the great load-bearing bones of the place had the patience of things that have stood since before standing was witnessed. And into those bones, everywhere, visibly, humanly: repair. New masonry braided into old in warmer, honester stone; timber galleries bracketed onto ancient walls; scaffolds up on a dozen faces with lamplit crews at work even now; streets of ordinary shops and homes built into the feet of structures whose original purpose nobody advertised, the way swallows build in a cathedral. A city found, not founded — a derelict of some elder age, raised and righted and reinhabited — and not one plaque, not one carving, not one word anywhere the party could find, then or later, saying whose bones they all were living in. The city did not explain itself. The party filed that beside everything else the gray had declined to explain, and kept walking, and the question walked with them.

The customs-house stood at the harbor's head — honest recent stonework grafted onto an ancient footing, clerks' windows, a queue — and the party stood in the queue, because @[Gorvach](gorvach)'s position on doors extends to counters. The officer was a young man with an Amphail accent and ink on his fingers, courteous as a Tuesday. He took their names; he turned the great ledger around; and there they were. Already entered. Seven names in a square, devout, unhurried hand, a lodging assignment beside them, and a small notation the officer read without a flicker of surprise: expected. “Welcome to Fundament,” he said warmly, stamping the page. “Long road, by the look of you. The Archive sends someone down for new arrivals — ah.” He glanced past them, and his courtesy went up a degree, the way courtesy does around certain colleagues. “There she is now.”

She was small — every account the party ever gave of her agrees on small, first, always — a gray-eyed woman of no plane's fashion in particular, ink at her cuffs, carrying a leather folder the way soldiers carry orders, with a face that did kindness efficiently, as though kindness were a form she had filed many times and could complete from memory. “Welcome,” she said. “You will want food, beds, and to know where things are, in that order. Everyone claims the order is different. It never is.” Somebody asked her name. “@[Archivist](the-archivist),” she said. “The name comes with the office; the rest of mine would only clutter your first day.” And she turned and led them up into the city at the pace of a woman who has never once been late for anything, and all seven of them liked her immediately, and she earned it, and kept earning it, and there is no sentence in this chapter more carefully weighed than that one.

@[Fundament, from inside](fundament), is a working city, and the work is the vertigo. They had crossed two planes braced for a fortress — walls, chains, the machinery of a stolen population — and they walked instead through a morning market where a woman argued a fishmonger down with both hands; past a school letting its noise out into a courtyard; along an aqueduct's cool shadow where children raced boats made of bark that had never grown on any tree the party could name; under the two lights that everyone but them had stopped looking up at, past walls ten thousand years older than the laundry strung between them. The streets were clean the way loved streets are clean, not the way ordered streets are. The wardens on the corners nodded, and did not follow. Nothing was afraid. They had come armed for a stronghold, and found a place where, in some other life, any one of them would have been glad to live; and nobody said that aloud, all day, which is how the party knew that every one of them was thinking it.

@audio: deliverance-bells fade=4
The Deliverance found them before their lodging did. A street shrine at a five-way corner: a stone basin, a bell, fresh flowers — fresh; the party had seen nothing bloom since the Feywild — and a carved relief, old enough to be respected and new enough to be legible, that told the city's story to anyone who stood before it. Wagons, ascending a road of light. Below and behind them, a town breaking apart under a sky rendered as cracked black glass — and at the ruin's edges, hooded figures with mirrors for faces, reaching after the wagons and falling short. The archivist gave them the history in the tone of a good guide, briefly, gravely: the cult of the Lady of Loss; the working they had grown in secret under a doomed town; the night it ripened, and the town paid; and @[the Deliverance](the-deliverance) — the crossing prepared in mercy and executed in one evening — that carried the saved out from under the Sharrans' harvest, through the doors that mercy had prepared, to the city that would keep them. She said it beautifully, and moved on. And the party walked after her in a silence with teeth in it, because they had stood in that graveyard, and they knew exactly how much of the story was true, and the exact size of the one thing missing from it, and there is no argument in the world harder to win than an argument against a story that is almost right.

At the corner's far side, an old man was telling anyone who paused that it had not been like that — that there had been singing, and a glass, and no fire at all until the end — and the entirely ordinary people of the district were kind to him. That is the sentence the party carried away and turned over for days: they were kind to him. A woman touched his arm and agreed with him — “That's right, uncle, the glass; that was their working; the Deliverance came in time, thank the doors” — and the old man stopped, and frowned, and looked down at his hands, because she had agreed with every one of his details and he was somehow further from the truth than before he had spoken; and the crowd moved on gently around him like water around a stone. @[Saeron](saeron) watched the whole exchange from the shrine steps without moving. “They have built a city where the truth sounds like a symptom,” he said at last, “and treats like one.” And @[Amyl](amyl), beside him, watching the old man be steered lovingly toward the bakery: “Worse. They have built one where it agrees with you first.”

It was in the harbor market, in the failing gold of the afternoon, that the day put its knife in. The party had stopped among the fish stalls while the archivist arranged something ahead, and @[Tirig](tirig) went still — hunter-still, whole-body still — looking across the crowd at a broad back at the far stall: sleeves rolled on heavy forearms, a bark of a laugh over the hagglers' noise that reached them a half-second after the head went back to loose it. A laugh they knew. A laugh from a common room on the North Ride, from a winter of stew and rumor and a floor a fool once scrubbed. The crowd shifted; a fish-cart came through; and the far stall, when it cleared, held strangers. Nobody said the name. All the way to their lodging nobody said it, each of them carrying it privately, the way you carry a coal in a pot — because to say it was to ask the question, and to ask the question was to open every door in this city at once, and they had promised one another on a boat with two lamps that they would learn the terrain first. It was the longest evening of the seven, and the ache of it was this: for the first time in the whole long year, hope and dread had exactly the same face.

The archivist's last arrangement of the day was a physician — “you have road in your lungs and worse in your bandages; the city will not have it said it let its guests limp” — and the physician was good: quick clean hands, honest tools, @[Saeron](saeron)'s ribs wrapped and @[Amyl](amyl)'s forearms dressed and @[Tirig](tirig)'s knee stitched with a neatness that earned even her respect. Then he came to @[Sov](sov), and unlaced the shirt at the party's nod, and stopped. The room went the particular kind of quiet that a professional's stillness makes. He looked at the seam of pale glass for a long moment — did not touch it — and then closed his case, and closed the file on his knee, and said, in a voice from which all the bedside had been carefully removed: “I was trained in this city. We do not treat that. That is not ours to treat.” He would take no payment for the visit. He let himself out. And the party sat with the sentence for a long time — not the refusal; refusals they knew; but the grammar of it, ours, the plural of a man who knows exactly whose the seam is, in a city where nobody had yet said her name.

They crossed the high bridge at dusk, because their lodging lay beyond it, and everyone stops on the high bridge; it is that kind of bridge. The two lights lay banked and golden-gray along the harbor water far below, and from that height the party could read the truth of the strait entire: honest water at the city's feet, silvering out by degrees into sheenless nothing toward the far gray shore, the ferries riding the fade with their paired lamps like slow deliberate fireflies, the elder towers standing up into both skies at once with the lamplit scaffolds of the living pinned to them like brooches. The party stood at the rail with the day settling in them. And their guide stopped too, a pace apart, and looked up — not at the view; past it; up between the two lights to where they failed to meet — all the way up, unhurried, complete, and then walked on. She did it, the party would learn, at every crossing of that bridge, every time, like someone keeping an appointment.

They took stock that night in good beds, in a warm house, in the capital of the enemy, and the stock would not tally. @[Aelendir](aelendir) said it for the table, quietly, while across the room @[Corvyn](corvyn) worked through a scale, badly, patiently, on a violin that no longer needed him to be anything but honest: they had come to breach a wall, and there was no wall; they had come to break a chain, and the chain was made of gratitude, and you cannot cut gratitude with anything they had packed. They had come to carry the taken home, and the taken were home — fed, employed, kind, grieving backwards for the living — and somewhere out in the lamplit dark below, a laugh they knew was pouring drinks or scrubbing pots or telling some new room's fools to mind her floor, believing them all a season dead. “We will have to learn the city first,” Aelendir said. “All of it. We owe the next thing we do the truth of this place.” And nobody argued, because outside the shutters Fundament was singing somewhere, several streets away, badly, the way happy towns sing at night; and it sounded like @[Triboar](triboar); and that was the fact, and it had teeth.

@audio: none fade=8
The dream, that night, did a thing it had never done in the whole life of this book. Six sleepers lay down at the seam of the worlds, inside the very scar they had been dreaming toward all year, and dreamed of nothing at all. No flat country. No heavier quarter. No figure, no fog, no direction. Sleep, ordinary and total, the kind the body banks. And @[Sov Thimron](sov), asked at breakfast, was quiet a moment over his tea, and then said that he had dreamed, actually: of a field of wheat. Nothing in it. Wheat, in a wind, in the sun, going on for miles — and somewhere out in the middle of it, he said, unworried, in no hurry at all, somebody sleeping. — I gave this city four chapters. The defense, entire: you cannot grieve what you never loved. Watch them love it.
