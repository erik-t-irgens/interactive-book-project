# VI. Between Moorings & Masks
*In which more time has passed than was spent, the circle grows by two, and a masquerade is not what it pretends to be*

@audio: triboar-hearth fade=8
They came up out of the earth into a wood that had aged without them.

It was the leaves that told them first. The party had gone down into the cave with the forest in one state and climbed back out to find it further along than any single night could account for — the autumn deeper, the canopy turned and thinning, the season advanced past what the hours they had counted could explain. Time had jumped. They could feel the wrongness of it before they could name it, and they would not fully understand it for a long while yet: that the dead ground around the cave-mouth had not been merely dead, but half-removed from the world, and that a place pulled out of the world does not keep the world's time. They had slept one night. The forest had spent far longer waiting for them to wake.

@[Amyl](amyl) and @[Corvyn](corvyn) felt the buried @[Presence](the-quiet-thing) again as they left the desecrated ground — the anxious thing beneath the world that had gone utterly silent in the deep — but it was diminished now, fainter, as though something between it and them had been dampened by what they had done to the stone. And when they came back to Triboar, the town confirmed what the trees had warned. Harvestide was upon it. The festival they had left comfortably distant was now only days away, the whole town caught up in preparation, the streets thick with it. Weeks had passed. They had been gone weeks, and had lived only a night of it, and no one could give them back the difference.

---

@[Nik](nik) brought them to @[Darathra](darathra), whose office was its usual controlled storm of paper and problem. She had a use for them, and it was a delicate one. @[Hyuth Kolstaag](hyuth) — the wealthy, difficult man whose name kept surfacing at the edges of every trouble in town — was throwing a Harvest masquerade at his manor, @[Boar's Rest](boars-rest), and using it to sell something he called assurances to the rich and the frightened. Protection, of a kind. Guarantees, of a kind. Darathra did not know exactly what was being sold, or to whom, or what the buyers were being guarded against, and she wanted eyes inside.

She laid out two ways to be useful, and let the party divide themselves as they saw fit. Some could attend the masquerade and learn what was being sold, who was buying, and who Hyuth had brought in to guard it. Others could hold the Tower and watch the cells below it — where, she confirmed at last, there was indeed an orc prisoner, the one the mob had howled for and she had denied. He was geased, she told them: mute not by choice but by compulsion, bound to silence by a magic laid on him, and not resisting his captivity so much as unable to speak of it. The peace with the orc nations was fragile, and this prisoner sat somewhere near the center of that fragility.

*She gave them one thing more: a letter, slate-blue, sealed with a crescent. A Moonletter, she called it, and it would open the doors of @[Moongleam Tower](moongleam) away in Everlund, where the @[Harpers](the-harpers) kept their northern watch. If the party turned up any evidence of Sharran doings, the Harpers wanted to know. The party pocketed it without yet understanding that it was a key to a door they would not reach for a long time, and by a harder road than any of them expected.*

And a formal invitation to Hyuth's fete arrived to seal the matter, gilt and grandiose, promising a private tasting for select benefactors and bearing a motto in the old tongue: Firmitas et abunditas. Firmness and abundance. Stability and plenty, sold as virtues, to people who could afford them.

---

It was @[Tirig](tirig) — who could be anyone — who resolved to be Darathra herself for the evening, because Darathra suspected aloud what the gilt of the invitation half-admitted: that the masquerade wanted the Lord Protector out of her Tower, on that night of all nights. If someone wished to lure Darathra to a party, then a Darathra they would have — while the real one stayed home with her keys, and saw what came calling.

There remained the problem of costuming, because a masquerade requires finery and nobody in the company owned anything fit to be arrested in, let alone feted. Triboar's answer was itself a small local legend: Irska and Fenn, twin elven caravanners whose traveling wagon unfolded, at the pull of certain brass levers, into a shop like the inside of a jewel box — silks that changed their minds about their color as you turned, clasps of silver worked finer than the coin that bought them, and prices that assumed you would rather not discuss prices. The twins were identical and immaculate: poise without a seam in it, a cadence of pure metropolitan flamboyance, each sentence begun by one and finished by the other as if they billed by the half-thought. “We could—” “—transform them.” “Entirely.” “For a—” “—consideration.” They were not foxes; it should be said plainly that they were elves, and gracious ones. It should also be said that everything about them — the tilt of the smiles, the appraisal folded inside the welcome, the faint sense of being weighed by the pelt — suggested the species so exactly that the party never privately called them anything else.

The fitting was a performance of its own — tape and pins and running double commentary — until it reached @[Amyl](amyl), and paused. The twins circled the firbolg the way surveyors circle a hill. “At this scale, in these silks,” one began, delicately, “perhaps the gentleman would be more comfortable—” “—in something ready-made,” finished the other, more delicately still, and the temperature of the wagon changed, because everyone present understood what the sentence actually contained. It was @[Darathra](darathra) who ended it, stepping forward with the flat civic finality of a portcullis coming down: the entire company's wardrobe would go on her tab. All of it. The gentleman first. The twins' objection died mid-syllable and was reborn, without a seam, as delight — “Of course—” “—Lord Protector” — and behind the borrowed face, @[Tirig](tirig) noted the speed of that pivot, and filed away what it said about which way these two would face in any weather.

The garbs, it must be admitted, were masterworks. A single palette ran through the whole company — midnight and silver and one thread of ember — cut differently for each but rhyming, so that seven strangers would read across a crowded hall as a set. The sole exception was @[Iratius](iratius), who declined the fitting with the courtesy of a drawn curtain and would attend as he was, in travel-worn crimson and steel; the twins mourned him from a distance all week, quietly, like a canvas that had refused the painter.

---

@audio: masquerade-gilt fade=6
Boar's Rest received them behind a screen of private guards notably better armed than anything Triboar itself could field — a detail the party marked and did not like. A thin, fluttering master-of-ceremonies checked their invitations with an air of delighted condescension and waved them through into a vast hall: masked guests in their finery, drink without end, a low warm murmur of politics and money moving beneath the music. The plan was simple. Split up, work the crowd, and meet back at the bar with whatever they had gathered.

Not all the security was private. Outside the premises, along the lantern-lit approach, stood members of @[the Twelve](the-twelve) in their town colors — lent to the evening, or purchased for it, keeping the peace at a polite distance from the peace they usually kept. One of them hailed Darathra as she passed with some easy half-finished business between colleagues — and got back a beat of blank, pleasant confusion before the Lord Protector recovered and answered in generalities. The officer thought nothing of it. Tirig, behind the borrowed face, filed away exactly how narrow the margin had been.

@[Iratius](iratius), out of habit, opened the paladin's quiet second sight — a slow breath, a prayer's worth of attention, and for six heartbeats the room lay before him the way a field lies before a hawk: every celestial a warmth, every fiend a wrongness, every walking dead thing a cold absence. He tasted nothing. The hall was clean of all three — which was itself a small strangeness, in a place that felt so wrong.

Three figures stood out from the masked crowd. The bartender, unmasked and unhurried. A hooded figure, also unmasked, keeping to a corner. And a very short guest, masked, of no particular importance. The party fanned out among them.

It is worth pausing here, because this is the night the company became what it would be. Two people entered the story at Boar's Rest who had not been part of it before — and though the party met them as strangers among the masks, they were not, in the deepest sense, strangers at all. In the kitchens that fed the fete worked a quiet chef, watching the hall through the staff doors with more attention than a cook has cause for. He wore no finery under his apron — only simple robes, and crimson cords wound tight about his forearms and hands covering what a sharp eye could guess at anyway: the old scars of a man who had absorbed a great deal of violence and given, one suspected, some portion of it back. He moved with a stillness that was not calm so much as discipline over something hotter. His name was @[Saeron](saeron). And among the arriving guests, masked and on the arm of a great lady of Silverymoon, came a bard named @[Aelendir](aelendir) — unlike any elf the North had ever shown them — features a half-step outside every elven family anyone could name, fair the way weather is fair, with something in the set of him that suggested the seasons owed him letters, with the unsettled beauty of a season about to turn: hair that seemed to brighten when the candlelight found it, eyes that sparkled with an otherworldly and perpetual curiosity, and the faint, impossible impression that the air near him smelled of blossom. He spoke like a man politely unstuck from the current century, and he carried slung at his back a @[shallow bowl of fey-wrought metal](the-resonance-bowl) — an instrument, though few in the hall could have named it. He was carrying something else in him too, though no one could have known it, least of all him: something older than anyone else in that hall. The circle was about to grow, and the gray country was about to show them why.

---

Amyl, who had no love for crowds, fled the crush of the floor for the quiet of a mezzanine lined with shelves, and there fell into conversation with a scholar named @[Theron Veil](theron-veil) — a man of odd stones and older questions, mirrored spectacles catching the light, the sort of person who collects strange facts the way others collect coin. He was pleasant, and curious, and asked questions that lingered oddly in the mind afterward. Amyl did not yet know that a scholar of oddstones was exactly the sort of person he would need, nor where Theron's questions were quietly pointing him.

*@[Sov](sov), working the bar, fell into talk with the unmasked bartender — @[Veyran](veyran), who poured with perfect poise and smiled with a fraction too much sharpness, the kind of man you remember for reasons you can't quite name. There was something behind the smile that did not match the apron. Sov could not have said what. He filed Veyran away as memorable, which was, as it happened, the most dangerous thing he could have done — though the danger would take a long time to arrive.*

The hooded figure in the corner was @[Darz](darz) — Hyuth's groundskeeper, the grieving man from the funeral, here in his employer's hall and utterly alone in it. He sat turning a place card over and over in his hands, and on the card was a single name: @[Sera](sera). @[Tirig](tirig), wearing Darathra's face, pressed him, and with the weight of the Lord Protector's seeming behind the questions he gave up the shape of his grief: they had loved each other, he and Sera, and he had lost her, and that was all. Of the cult that killed her, of Verity, of the working in the wood, he knew nothing. He was exactly what he appeared to be — a man in mourning, holding a card with his dead love's name on it at a party thrown by the man who employed him. Sov brought him an ale. Darz took it, and retreated back into his silence, and the party left him to it.

---

The guests of honor were announced in a processional, and it read like a map of powers the party did not yet know they were standing among. Lady @[Mariel Heartsilver](mariel-heartsilver) of Silverymoon swept in resplendent in swan plumage, and on her arm the masked bard, Aelendir. @[Aevren](aevren) of Everlund, styled a Courier in Good Standing, came veiled in gauze behind a crescent mask. A grainhouse steward; an auditor of the autumn stores in cornhusk finery; Theron Veil the scholar with his mirrored spectacles; Boren Barasque, caravan master of the Long Road; a Silverymoon attaché named Solenne standing in for the great Alustriel herself, a silver harp brooch at her collar; a discreet patron-merchant called @[Grey Thimble](grey-thimble); the Lord Protector Darathra, or the thing wearing her face; the sommelier Veyran; the twin outfitters Irska and Fenn; and last, the host — @[Hyuth Kolstaag](hyuth), behind a mask made from the skull of a boar.

*Hyuth's toast extolled the virtues he was selling: stability, order, investment, the moral case for buying an assurance in an uncertain age. And he closed it with a line that landed strangely on the ears of a party who had read those words before, etched by a firm hand on a scrap of parchment in a dead woman's severed grip. @[May the light not blind the veil](the-harvest) of our prosperity, said Hyuth Kolstaag, and lifted his glass, and did not seem to know he was quoting anyone.*

---

@audio: gray-dream fade=1
And then, for a heartbeat, the hall went out.

The masks, the music, the warm murmur of money — gone, drained to that flat and horizonless gray the party knew too well. But the circle standing in the gray was not the circle it had been. There were more of them now. @[Saeron](saeron) was there, the quiet monk from the kitchens, seeing the others for the first time and being seen. @[Aelendir](aelendir) was there, the bard, staring around him at faces he had met only that evening and recognizing them the way one recognizes a word in a dream. Where five had stood within the circle — six, counting the @[far stranger](the-far-figure) at its edge — there now stood seven, and the stranger made eight. The company that had woken as strangers in Triboar and slowly learned it was bound by something none of them could name had just discovered that the binding had been waiting, all along, to include these two as well.

And far off, at the very edge of the gray, past the newly widened circle, a figure stood. The eighth. Gaunt, and distant, and turned away, too far to make out — present as it had been present before, neither nearer nor speaking, one more shape in the fog. The party had grown, and the far figure had not come any closer, and the two facts sat side by side in the gray and did not explain each other. Then the hall rushed back — the light, the masks, Hyuth's raised glass — and eight people who now knew something new and unspeakable about one another stood scattered across a masquerade, wearing other people's faces, unable to say a word of it aloud.

---

The evening resolved back into its work, but the work had sharpened. The chef — @[Saeron](saeron), the Ilmater monk in the staff kitchens — found a quiet moment to tell the woman he believed to be Darathra that there was a @[hidden ledger](the-silent-ledger) of Hyuth's assurance deals somewhere on the grounds, and a large food store besides. Games would be played through the evening, he said, and they awarded coins, and each coin bought two seats at the private tasting where the real business would be done. The path deeper into Hyuth's operation ran through those coins.

*At the bar, @[Veyran](veyran) turned his attention to @[Iratius](iratius), and the attention had teeth. He needled the paladin about names — say it right and your hands won't tremble — and made it plain, with a smile, that he knew Iratius. Knew him from somewhere, knew something about him. And Iratius, groping after it, found nothing; the bartender's certainty met a blank where a memory should have been. Veyran seemed to find the blankness delightful. He slid a coin across the bar to the paladin who could not place him, a small gift with a great deal behind it, and went back to his pouring.*

The others worked their own angles. @[Amyl](amyl) sent an owl familiar circling over Darathra's Tower to check on the other half of the night's business — and the owl found the place strangely empty, no one visible on the main floors or above, which was odd if not yet alarming. And @[Corvyn](corvyn) and @[Aelendir](aelendir) together charmed Lady Heartsilver into parting with one of her coins, in exchange for a promised solo performance, an engagement in the north, and the commission of a music box — a fair night's work for a bard, and a second seat secured.

So the tally stood, as the masquerade wore on toward its private heart. Iratius held one coin. Corvyn and Aelendir between them held another. Four seats at the tasting were secured, and the rest of the company still needed a coin apiece to earn a place at whatever Hyuth meant to reveal to his select benefactors. The Silent Ledger was somewhere in the house. The food cache was somewhere on the grounds. And the true question — what, and who, Hyuth's assurances were actually underwriting — sat unanswered behind the masks, waiting for the party to buy their way close enough to see it.
