# V. Into the Mirror
*In which the party goes down into the dark, and each of them is shown a truth they were not ready to keep*

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=4
The Cultist had fled into the earth, and the earth, it turned out, went a long way down.

Where she vanished, the party found the mouth of a deeper cave still, and screams rising out of it from somewhere further in. They went down after her. @[Tirig](tirig) took the lead, reading the Cultist's footprints along the winding dark — and, being practical about the getting-out as well as the getting-in, dropping small arrangements of pebbles behind them to mark their own way back.

The deeper they went, the less the cave behaved like stone. The walls carried a moving, otherworldly texture — a soft energy that came in slow waves they could not feel, only see, the surface sliding and settling and playing tricks on the corners of their eyes, so that more than once someone turned to face a movement that had never happened. Mushrooms lit the dark in chosen places: giants of them, taller than Amyl, glowing a cold blue-white, and colonies of small ones spilling down the seams like frost — and all of them, when touched, cold, colder than the air, cold like things that had made an arrangement with some other winter.

The passage wound down and down, worked and deliberate, and then it split: a way to the right and a way to the left, both breathing that faint wave-light. They did not debate it long. The Cultist's footprints went right, and so did they — and the right-hand way opened at last into a hall. The footprints crossed the threshold ahead of them, went eleven strides into the open floor, and stopped. Mid-room. Mid-stride. As if the woman had been unmade between one step and the next. There was no other mark on the dust anywhere, and no one said anything useful, because there was nothing useful to say.

---

@audio: mirror-glass fade=5
It was an ancient place, and it had been made for one purpose. A massive @[black mirror](the-black-mirror), ornate and old, dominated the far wall. Before it stood three stone altars, and on each altar lay a body — dead, and yet breathing; rotting, Amyl confirmed, riddled with old unhealed injuries, and somehow still drawing breath. From each of the three ran black rippling tendrils that fed back into the dark glass, as though the mirror were drinking something out of them that death itself had not been permitted to interrupt.

@[Corvyn](corvyn) could tell them little from history, but he could feel what lay on the room: necromancy, strong and deep, threaded through with divination and abjuration. A religion read tied the whole chamber to Shar. They tested it carefully. Tirig's pebble struck the mirror and simply fell; @[Sov](sov)'s prayer to stir the dying failed against the bodies, which were held in some state his magic could not reach. The glass itself felt material and yet mobile, metallic, deathly cold — and when Corvyn laid a hand on it, it drained the life out of him in a cold rush until the paladin's healing pulled him back. This was no reflection. It was a mouth.

*It was @[Nik](nik) who found the way in. Each altar bore a keyhole shaped for an amulet — and the broken Sharran pendant the party had carried out of Sera's dead hand tugged toward them, drawn to its matching locks. The pendant that had marked her murder was the key to the room her murder had helped to build.*

---

The middle altar was Pain. When @[Sov](sov) seated the amulet in its slot, the sigil flared and a mist of memory played across the air while psychic screams tore through his mind; the body on the stone bore Shar's brand burned into its neck. It was only when the party severed the body's foot, the amulet still seated, that the binding broke — the tendril lashed out once, and shattered. A woman's voice spoke out of the air as the body's breath finally stopped and its sigil went dark: Pain is the truest prayer.

The left altar was Loss. A gaunt figure lay bound there by strands running from the mirror, its lips moving in silent prayer. The amulet, seated, called up another projection — and it was @[Nik](nik) who answered this one, not with a blade but with grief. He thought of @[Sera](sera). He offered a prayer of his own. And a single tear fell from him, and the tendril broke on it, and the sigil collapsed with a whisper: Loss is the only truth. The dwarf had freed a stranger from Shar's altar with nothing but the honesty of his own mourning, and said nothing about it.

The right altar was Oblivion, and it was the strangest. The figure slumped there had a face gone to fragments, memory smeared away to almost nothing. Sov invoked his god and set his hand to the corpse — and for a moment lost himself, his own sense of who he was guttering out like a candle before it caught again and steadied. He came back. The body did not; it vanished from the altar entirely, and the last sigil went dark, and a whisper followed it into the cold: To be forgotten is the only mercy.

Three altars. Three surrenders. Loss, Pain, Oblivion — the whole of what the Lady of Loss holds sacred, worked into the endless suffering of three living people to feed a mirror in the dark. The party had freed them, one mercy at a time. What the freeing had unlocked was worse than the lock.

---

*With all three sigils gone, the mirror did not go quiet. It brightened. Fresh tendrils unfurled from its surface and reached past the wall toward something vast and unseen beyond it, and the glass began to show the party reflections that were not their own — blurred faces, hollow eyes — and to pull, palpably, at their souls. They set themselves against it and held. And as each of them resisted the mirror's dragging weight, it showed them, one at a time and alone, a vision meant for them and no one else.*

They did not compare these in the moment. Some did not speak of what they saw for a long while after, and some never did at all. Set side by side now, for the first time, this is what the mirror showed.

## What Sov saw

@unlock: sov.2
*The mirror ripples. Your reflection melts away, replaced by a vast sky of shadow torn by violet lightning. From the storm, a throne of bone rises — and upon it… @[you sit](sov). Your skin is pale as marble, your eyes two hollows of endless night. Around you kneel an army without end: soldiers, their blades lowered in reverence. Their voices chant in silence, yet the weight of their devotion — as eternal as they are — crushes the air.*

*The vision flickers. For a moment, a familiar face replaces your own upon the throne — his mouth opening in a scream you cannot hear before shadow consumes him.*

*The mirror shifts again: you see yourself not on a throne, but bound to it, chains of darkness coiling around your arms and throat. The army still kneels, but not to you. They look through you, as if you are not yourself.*

*A final flash: you stand alone in a dead city beneath a hollow sky — the army gone, the throne shattered, only a single feather smoldering in your palm, wilting to ash yet never fully consumed.*

## What Iratius saw

*A word stirs unbidden on your tongue. You do not remember the place you first heard it, the face that taught it, or its cost if any — only the sting of smoke in your lungs, the taste of ash, and iron heat pressed against your palm. A whisper coils in your mind like smoke rising from coals, and before you can resist, the syllable slips free: ‘Volcaris!’ The blade drinks the sound, and erupts in flame.*

*And @[Iratius](iratius), alone among them, did not only see his vision — he spoke it. The word came up out of him and out into the cold air of the hall, @[Volcaris](volcaris), a name he had no memory of learning — and his blade answered. Fire ran up the steel and stood there burning, called out of nothing by a syllable he could not account for. He could not have told the others where it came from. That was rather the point.*

## What Tirig saw

*The mirror ripples into frost. Ice creeps across its surface, not clear but clouded, as though @[you are staring](tirig) into a frozen lake. Beneath the ice lies a shape — pale, shifting, never the same twice. A woman, a child, a stranger. Her face changes as quickly as snowdrift in the wind.*

*The ice cracks, and through it you glimpse a forest of silver trees, their roots stretching across two skies at once — one bright, one black. The roots knot themselves into a doorway, and in its center lies a heart of crystal, pulsing with white light. Each beat sends a chill into your bones.*

*For a moment, the reflection twists — you see yourself standing at the threshold, the heart in your hand, though your face is not your own. Behind you, a shadow stirs, vast wings unfolding. Ahead, the silver trees burn with black fire, and another figure — faceless, shrouded — reaches toward the heart.*

*The frost shatters. Only your reflection remains, blurred at the edges, eyes pale as winter.*

## What Amyl saw

*A musk of rot, of damp leaves and fungus, @[clings to your senses](amyl). You recall the pulse of roots in the soil, the crawling of beetles beneath your skin. A familiar face-like shape flickers in the mulch, opening its mouth to speak — but only spores pour out. A word attempts to lurch, and comes as a cough, a choke, a bloom: not life, not death, but something in between. You feel an unnatural sense of calm. Things are how they've always been, and always will be; balanced.*

## What Corvyn saw

*@[The mirror ripples into stone](corvyn). A wall rises, endless and seamless, blocks cut so clean they might never have been touched by mortal hands. In the gaps between them, faint carvings flicker — runes so small you cannot tell if they are letters, or simply cracks that pretend to be. Each glows faintly, pulsing in time with a heartbeat you do not feel in your chest.*

*Somewhere within the wall, a violin string hums. The note is thin, stretched taut — more vibration than sound. It shivers through the stone, and for a moment you see light caught in the mortar, like light beneath water. Then, just as quickly, the light blackens, as if iron were poured over it, leaving only a dark scar that does not heal.*

*The wall stands silent again. Only the faint echo of strings remains, a song you almost remember, but cannot place. In the vision's last breath, you glimpse a mason's chisel lying at the foot of the wall. Its edge is worn smooth, but the handle is slick as though freshly wet.*

And there was a seventh vision, though it belonged to none of the party. @[Nik](nik) had come this far with them — the grieving dwarf, Sera's husband, who had followed his wife's death into the dark — and the mirror had something for him too. And unlike theirs, his was not private. The glass threw it up for every eye, the way it had displayed the bound victims' memories above the altars, so that the whole party stood witness to what Nik alone should have owned. It did not show him a prophecy. It showed him a memory, which was crueler.

## What Gurpnik saw

*The mirror ripples, and you see her. @[Sera](sera). The warmth of firelight, the familiar weight of her hand in yours, the sound of her laughter soft against the walls of home. It's a memory you know — but it lingers too long, caught in place like a bead of amber.*

*As you watch, the firelight bends. Silver threads weave themselves through her hair, her smile, her skin. A glow forms — faint at first, then steady, as though the moon itself rests within her veins. Her presence pushes at the edges of shadow, and for the first time you notice how the dark of the memory recoils from her.*

@unlock: sera.3
*She doesn't seem to feel it. She only laughs again, squeezing your hand, her gaze full of quiet devotion. But the mirror forces you to see her truth: a light not wholly hers to give, waiting. And in that glow, you understand why the plants at the place she died grew lush where all else withered. Why her death was not just loss, but purpose.*

*The memory burns white at the edges, searing away into shadow, leaving only your reflection once more — empty-handed.*

Nik said nothing about it — there was nothing left to say; they had all seen. But something in him had rearranged by the time he turned from the glass, some final piece of his grief set into a new and heavier shape, and he carried it out of the hall without a word, through a party that now knew exactly what he had lost and could offer him nothing but their silence back.

---

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=3
*As they made to move on, a distant scream rolled up out of the deeper dark — a woman's voice, but beast-tinged, wrong — and an unnatural shimmer crawled across the stone of the walls. They gathered what the chamber offered: a violet-black crystal, a scroll steeped in Darkness, a satchel with a few coins. And they went on toward the scream, because the way out was the way through.*

The left-hand passages opened into a fungal grove — pale glow-caps and not-quite-mushrooms carpeting the stone in a faint cold light. The cold of the place was familiar: the same chill that hung on the ichor, though this growth bore no ichor's taint. Amyl clipped samples, and found that the cuttings stayed cold even stowed in his pack, as if they had brought a piece of that chill away with them. Beyond the grove lay an alcove that had served as a camp — bedrolls, travel rations, tool crates, all long abandoned, no cultist anywhere in sight. Judging it as safe as anything down here would be, the party took a long rest in the dark before pressing on.

@unlock: gwaerons-rest.2
*And here is a thing they did not know, sleeping in that cold pocket of the earth: they were no longer entirely in the world. The barren, lifeless ground around the cave-mouth, the place where nothing grew and the ichor gave way to bare stone, was not simply a dead patch of forest. It was a seam — a place worn so thin between the world and whatever lies against it that it belonged fully to neither, a between-place pulled half out of the world by the working the party had come down to find. That was why the buried Presence had gone silent here, and why Amyl and Corvyn had felt it vanish so completely: it was not merely muffled. It was gone, because here was somewhere it could not follow. And time, in such a place, does not keep faith with the world outside it. The party slept, and rose, and thought a night had passed. They were wrong, and they would not learn how wrong until they climbed back up into the trees.*

---

An old metal door barred the way forward, worked in the same austere style as the mirror. It answered to the same key: Sov set the Sharran amulet into a matching slot, and with a grinding slide the door opened onto a chamber that was almost wholly without light.

*A single shaft of illumination fell on the thing at its center: a @[stone, levitating](mooring-stones), perfectly still, its surface crawling with etched runes. From it a single shadow-tendril rose straight up and vanished into a blackness far overhead. Iratius tested that blackness — hurled a flaming brand up into it, and watched the flame snuff out instantly some three stories up as it crossed a threshold, the wood tumbling back down cold. A dome of unnatural darkness, sealing the chamber from above. Closer study showed the stone bending the light around it and muffling sound in its vicinity; Amyl caught glimpses of silhouettes at its edges that did not match anyone in the room. And the arcane truth of it, when they read it, was precise and chilling: necromancy built to siphon through the tendril — custom-tuned, the reading said, to a vessel.*

They probed the anchor and it answered every touch with wrongness. Tirig's arrow rang off its surface with a tone felt through the floor. Heavy blows reverberated and rebounded; Nik felt the backlash of each strike in his own arms. Iratius pressed fire to it and the stone reacted in something like anger, its runes flaring bright.

A side stair climbed away into the dark, toward what looked like an inner cave-mouth — an exit, maybe, and two of them went up to prove it, quickly, the way you check a door in a burning house. The stair ended at a black opening that breathed cold air, and there the two of them stopped, because the cave had gone wrong in a way that had nothing to do with anything they could point at. The wave-light on the walls had stilled. The mushrooms' glow seemed to lean. There was a silence with intention in it, the kind of silence a room makes when something in it is about to move — and then, out of the opening in front of them, from close, from far too close, came the scream. It was a beast's scream, and it was a woman's, and it was not entirely either — two voices forced through one ruined throat, grief and hunger braided into a single rising note that went through the party's teeth and stayed there. And under it, growing, the pounding of heavy steps.

---

@unlock: the-maimed-cultist.2
It lurched into the light a ruin of two things fused into one: a giant bear grown together with the one-handed Cultist who had fled them, skull and face half-exposed, black ichor sluicing from the seams where beast and woman met. It charged, and it hit like nothing they had yet faced. Its claws and ichor-slick bite tore into them; its stolen human voice wailed in a register that froze the blood; and it would not go down. The party landed blow after blow that should have felled it, and the @[mooring stone](mooring-stones) would not let it die. The anchor pulsed and the chamber answered its master: the levitating stone bent the light to blind them at the wrong moments, dragged at their strength, fed the horror's failing body some borrowed portion of its own wrongness so that wounds closed and the thing lurched up again and again. The fight went long and it went badly, the horror hammering them harder than anything in the wood had, the stone behind it refusing to surrender its guardian.

But the stone's grip was not perfect — whatever it was reaching for, it could not both hold the boundary of the world and fully keep its dead bear breathing, and at last the borrowed life ran thin. @[Nik](nik) ended it, when the ending came, with a single enormous stroke of his greataxe that clove through what was left of the thing, and it came apart in a wash of ichor and jagged bone. @[Corvyn](corvyn) worked an @[obsidian knife](the-obsidian-knife) loose from its jaw as it fell.

@audio: silver-fire fade=3
*And then @[Sov](sov) brought the amulet close to the anchor, and the last secret of the wood revealed itself. Sera's blood — still on the things they had carried from her — caught the dark and burned silver. It glowed, bright and argent, and pushed back the blackness that wrapped the stone, driving the shadow away from it before the blood spent itself and evaporated into the cold air. And as it burned, Sov was given something: a vision of a ritual. Not a prophecy, this time, but an instruction — a working shown to him in the moment he most needed it.*

@unlock: mooring-stones.2
He performed it. Whatever the silver light had shown him, he did, there in the lightless chamber over the levitating stone — and the anchor answered. The runes stilled. The wrong weightless stillness of it failed. And the stone sank, slowly, out of the air, and settled to the ground, and lay there dormant: a black thing gone quiet at last, its hold on the boundary of the world loosened by a ritual carried in a dead woman's silver blood.

@audio: undergrave-drone fade=6
They gathered themselves in the sudden ordinary dark of a chamber whose horror had gone quiet. In the bear-thing's alcove they found what was left of the people it had been made from — picked-clean corpses, torn clothing that matched the Cultist's garb — and Sov took a soggy scroll from the muck while Corvyn turned up a handful of coin. Back through the camp, among the frugal leavings of the cultists who had lived down here, Sov pried loose a fragment of altar stone, necrotic to the touch, etched in a script none of them could read. And then, burdened and quiet, they turned at last for the surface and the clean air and the world they thought they had left only a day behind.

They had gone down into the dark to answer one question, and they were climbing back up carrying a hundred worse ones. A paladin whose sword now knew a word he didn't. A dwarf who had freed a stranger with a single tear, and then seen his murdered wife haloed in silver light. A cleric who had glimpsed himself on a throne of bone, and then been handed a ritual out of nowhere by the blood of the woman whose death had begun all of this. And beneath every one of them, unspoken, the growing certainty that the murder they had come to solve was the smallest visible piece of something with no bottom to it. What they did not yet carry — what was waiting for them up in the light, patient as the turning of a season — was the knowledge of how long they had truly been gone.
