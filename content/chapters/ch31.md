# XXXI. Remember Everything, Feel Nothing
*In which six people pack without discussing it, and a door is cut in the floor of the world*

@audio: nesme-lantern fade=6
@[Nesmé](nesme) spent the days after the Feast doing what standing towns do, which is stand: burying the night's dead — the night had not been free, and the party stood at every grave — mending the wool-house doors, arguing about taxes, letting children back onto the walls to watch for a spring that was months out and worth watching for anyway. The town knew what it had been spared. It did not know the size of it, which is the mercy of all sparings, and nobody who did know felt any need to correct the scale.

The party did their grieving the way the record has learned this party grieves, which is sideways. Nobody said his name at meals. Everybody set his place. The children of the billet-quarter came twice a day to ask the orc, whom they had decided was the appropriate authority, when the quiet one was coming back to teach the next knot, and @[Gorvach](gorvach), who does not lie, told them the truth: “When he is done.” And @[Bayne](bayne) stayed. All of it — the blade he stole for and believed in and held a gap with, and the worn sheath, laid straight on the wet stones where he sat, the way he set things down for other people to find. He went, and the sword he filled his whole quiet life with did not go with him, and there is a sentence in that the record has read forty times and cannot yet write anything after. @[Tirig](tirig) oils it. Nobody has drawn it. It rides wrapped across her pack now, hilt forward — the way you carry a thing you intend to hand back.

@audio: gray-dream fade=6
On the first night after the Feast, the gray was different, and all six of them knew it before breakfast without one of them having said it. The dream again — the flat country, the cold that is not cold, the hush like a held breath — and in it, for the first time in the whole life of the dream, someone else. Far off. Too far for a face. A shape, sitting — sitting the way a man sits at the end of a long day — and around it the emptiness had a direction, a quarter where the gray hung heavier, and every sleeping one of them had begun, without deciding to, to walk toward it before waking took them back. Six people came down to breakfast and looked at each other and did not bother saying it. The dream had acquired a compass. The compass was him.

So the decision that fills this chapter was never actually made, in the sense of made — there was no council, no vote, no evening of weighed reasons. There was only the morning @[Amyl](amyl) went to buy travel bread and found @[Tirig](tirig) already at the baker's, and the afternoon @[Corvyn](corvyn) went to wax his boots and found them waxed, because @[Iratius](iratius) had done everyone's, and the evening @[Saeron](saeron) opened the door of the billet to start the conversation and found five packs standing against the wall next to his own, which he did not remember packing, and had packed. At our table this is the vote I never got to call. I have never once doubted the count: six ayes, in the order you always spoke in.

Here is what they knew, laid honestly, because the record owes this decision its actual arithmetic. They did not know what the glass does with what it takes; no one on this side knows that, and the party had buried that question with the town's dead once already, in another graveyard, over an empty plot. And this time they knew worse, and the record will not let them be braver on the page than they were at the fire: they had seen where the mirror aimed its dying; they had measured the shard and the dark along it; and no one walks away from that arithmetic. He had died. They made themselves say the word aloud, once, each of them, because @[Aelendir](aelendir) said that a grief you will not name walks behind you. And then, beside the word, they laid everything that refused to lie flat under it. He had left no body. The shadows in that cellar had leaned at something's arriving; the room had filled with the smell of snow; and afterward a door of dead glass had fogged from the inside and passed them a note with a promise in it. You do not get a note from nowhere. Nowhere keeps no tables. And if death had collected their friend, then death had collected him somewhere with tables, and notes, and manners — which meant that death, over there, was an administration; and an administration can be petitioned. Somewhere behind the world's glass there was a room with their names in its ledger and, by every sign worth the name, their friend somewhere in its keeping — and the seven-pointed arithmetic of this company has never once balanced at six.

The key came off the singer. The two who carried nothing had, it turned out, carried one thing between them: sewn flat into the celebrant's robe, wrapped in silk gone stiff with age, a knife of black glass — @[obsidian](the-obsidian-knife), knapped in a style @[Aevren](aevren)'s letter would later call older than the empire that thinks it invented the road — with an edge that took no interest in rope, or wood, or the ball of @[Iratius](iratius)'s thumb, and turned to any glass in the room like a compliment. @[Darz](darz) could not be in the same house as it. “It is not louder,” he said, from the yard, hat to his chest, “it is more certain.” They tested it once, on a cellar shard the size of a coin: the black edge went down through the glass the way a good pen goes through cheap paper, and the two halves did not fall apart. They opened. There is a difference, and every person watching saw it, and nobody has ever successfully described it to anyone who was not there. And then @[Corvyn](corvyn), who had been staring at the silk wrappings with a face going carefully to stone, went to his own pack without a word and came back and laid a second knife beside the first. Its twin. Black glass, knapped in the same elder style, edge for edge and curve for curve — the dagger the party had pulled from the neck of a ruined bear under Triboar, in another life, in the first cave of this whole long story, and had carried through every chapter since on the ancient principle that you do not sell a thing you cannot explain. It had ridden in his kit for a year. Nobody had ever known what it was for. They knew now, and the knowing rearranged the early chapters behind them like furniture: the Sharrans had carried keys to the doors they grew, all along, and the party had owned one, unread, since the beginning.

@[Aelendir](aelendir) gave them the country before they went, because he is the only one of them who had ever stood anywhere adjacent to it, long ago, in the way of his people's long errands. He gave it in the voice he saves for true things. @[The gray](the-gray-country) does not hate you; hatred is warmth, and warmth is what it lacks and wants. It will not attack your body first. It will lean on your heart — gently, endlessly, the way winter leans on a door — and what it takes it does not give back across its own borders. So: bank your feelings like coals. Do not blaze. Do not mourn while walking; mourning is the richest fuel there is. Hold your memories instead — memories are structure, not heat; the country has no use for them and no purchase on them. He looked around the fire at the five packed bags and his own. “Remember everything,” he said. “Feel nothing you cannot afford. And when we cannot manage it — because we will not be able to manage it — hum. @[The tune](the-tune) is a rope-line. Keep one hand on it and walk.” Nobody at that fire missed where they had seen a rope-line in a gray blizzard before, and nobody said so, which was itself good practice.

The staying was harder to settle than the going — the going had settled itself. @[Osric](bettany-and-osric) would hold the shrine and the pattern-work; six winters of it did not end because one working died, and somebody sanctioned ought to be above ground if the seven-pointed arithmetic went wrong. @[Darz](darz) would stay because the door would need an ear — “if it changes its mind down there, I will know before it finishes changing it” — and because, he did not say, a man who sleeps in graveyards had found a town that needed exactly that man. And @[Bettany](bettany-and-osric) came to the billet on the last evening and demanded passage, in the tone of a woman who has crossed nine years and a moor and is not stopping for a door, and @[Saeron](saeron) gave her the refusal he owed her: plainly, without cover. We do not know what is there. We do not know that anyone is there. If he is there — and he did not have to say who — I will not come back without knowing it. She stood in the doorway a long time. Then she took the cord from her hair and gave him, from around her neck where it had hung nine years, a key: an iron door-key, worn bright at the shoulder, to a house in Iriaebor that burned the night everything did. “If you find him,” she said, “he will know it. And if you find only where he was —” and she did not finish, and did not need to, and Saeron put the key in the breast pocket where the cords ride, over the heart, which is where this party keeps other people's unfinished sentences. I keep a list of objects I would not have dared invent. The key is third.

They gave the knife to @[Gorvach](gorvach) on the last morning, and the record wants the reasoning on the page, because it was @[Tirig](tirig)'s and it was perfect: he keeps things. The truce, when keeping it cost him a geas. The letters, when the words would not stay for him and he made them stay. The word on the shield. You do not leave a key with the cleverest person in the town; keys are not riddles. You leave it with the one who, told to hold a door for a winter, will be discovered in the spring, holding the door. Gorvach took the black knife in his gray fist and looked at it exactly as long as he looks at anything, and said, “Which word do I carve when you are through,” and @[Saeron](saeron) said, “WAIT,” and the orc nodded once, as though confirming an order he had already given himself.

They laid the mirror back together on the cellar floor. That sentence cost a night's work in gloves and it costs nothing here, which the record apologizes for: every shard they could find, from the sliver-dust up to the fogged hand's-width piece that had carried the note, fitted seam to seam on the swept stone like a puzzle nobody wanted the prize for, until a mirror the size of a barn door lay in the floor of the cold room, dead and black and creased with a hundred silver scars, giving back their six lanterns and nothing else. It did not hum. @[Darz](darz), in the doorway, hat down, confirmed it: “It still counts, though. Under the quiet. It's counting.” Nobody asked him what it was counting. There are questions you save for the far side of a door.

They crossed at dusk, because Aelendir said such doors prefer it and nobody wanted to learn what a door's preferences do when ignored. The plan was spoken at the door's edge, before, not after — @[Amyl](amyl) insisted on the order, and the record thinks he was right to — so that the parting was scheduled as a reunion with an errand in the middle. We go for our friend. We come back by another road if another road offers, but we cross home through this gate, all of us, together — and “all of us,” said in that cellar, was visibly, defiantly, a number larger than six. @[Gorvach](gorvach) listened to the whole plan with his arms folded. Then he unslung the shield, and knelt, and set it down facing the glass, propped against the wall where the lantern found it — so that the first thing anyone coming up out of that door will ever read, in new letters an inch deep, is KEEP.

Then @[Saeron](saeron) drew the black knife down the mirror's long seam — one stroke, wrist-steady, a craftsman's stroke with no anger in it — and handed the knife back out of the circle to the orc, and the glass opened. Not broke: opened. The scars let go of one another; the whole dark lay of it swung downward on nothing, like the surface of a well deciding to be a stair; and where a floor had been there was a going-down, gray on gray on gray, and the cold that is not cold came up the steps of it to meet them like a servant sent ahead.

They went in order, because order was all the ceremony anyone could carry: @[Tirig](tirig) first, her hand rising once to a throat bare since the Spine — old compasses lead too; then @[Corvyn](corvyn) with the violin case slung crosswise like a soldier's kit; then @[Amyl](amyl), staff first, the way you enter water; then @[Iratius](iratius), who paused at the lip and looked back at Darz and Osric and Bettany and the shield and got it all in one look, the way he banks everything now; then @[Aelendir](aelendir), humming, one hand trailing the rope of it behind him for the others; and @[Saeron](saeron) last, the key over his heart, backing down the first step so that the final thing he saw of the sunlit world was his friends' lamplit faces over an orc's word. The glass took the light of Nesmé the way evening takes a window. Then it was a floor again, and Gorvach sat down against the wall beside his shield, facing the door, and began — the town swears this and the record believes the town — to wait in a way that was visible from across the room.

And on the far side of the first step there was no far side, and then there was: a cellar. The same cellar — and not. The cistern's bones stood around them in colors that had given up partway — not gray, exactly; brown that could not remember being brown, stone the shade of a bruise a week old — and the air was the dream's air, the cold that is not cold, the hush like a held breath, but the place was no flat dreamt country. The place was a place. They came up the stair with their lanterns burning small and reluctant, and stepped out into @[Nesmé](nesme) — and Nesmé had fallen. Here, it had fallen. The wall they had defended stood breached in three places nobody had breached; the Wet Ram's roof lay open to a sky like a shut eyelid, lightless without being black; the streets they had walked two hours ago in lamplight ran empty and roofless under a wind that moved nothing. It was the town's other ending — the one they had paid to prevent, kept here anyway, the way a shadow keeps the shape of a thing that has already turned away. @[Aelendir](aelendir) stood in the ruined market square and said, very quietly, the lesson none of his books had managed to make him feel: “It is the world. The @[world's shadow](the-gray-country). Everything casts one — towns, moors, endings. We are going to walk through everything we know, and it will all be like this, and we should start now, because I do not think this street is good for us.” And far off, past the broken gate, out over the shadow of their own moors — all six of them felt it at once, the way you feel a fire behind you — the emptiness had a direction, and the direction had been waiting, and they walked.
