# XXXIII. The Hollow Sovereign
*In which a collection is walked, and the collector is not at home, and a promise is read through glass*

@audio: vault-of-glass fade=8
No door opposed them. The record wants that on its own line, because everything after it happens inside that fact: they walked up the drowned processional, between the drowned colonnades, to an entrance that stood at the scale of weather, and there was no gate to force and no watch to talk past and no lock to pick and no voice to answer to. The doorway simply continued being open, the way it had been open, evidently, for longer than some of the buried architecture had been buried, and the six walked into the horizon they had mistaken it for, and the gray outside became a gray inside without so much as a change of temperature. Keep count, if you have been keeping count, of how many times this book has now told you that the house was not empty. It was not empty now. Nothing received them. Both of those facts held hands the whole way in.

The inside kept the outside's promise. Halls at the scale of canyons, unlit and not dark; ceilings that resolved, when the eye insisted, into further architecture doing something patient overhead; no dust — the record notes it because @[Tirig](tirig) noted it, hunter's eyes running the floor: no dust, no tracks, no cobweb, no wear, a cleanliness that was not maintenance but something prior to the possibility of mess. Their bootsteps went out into the halls and did not come back. @[Aelendir](aelendir) hummed the first bar of the rope-line and the sound stood close to his mouth like a person unwilling to walk ahead. And everywhere — this arrived slowly, the way the fortress itself had arrived, by being mistaken at first for architecture — everywhere, in files and avenues and long receding aisles, stood the glass.

Panes. Not cases, not caskets, not boxes — the record refuses every container word, because the things contained nothing in any way a box contains: tall @[standing panes of dark glass](the-glass), frameless, footless, upright on the air the way the mirror in the cistern had stood on it, arranged in aisles that ran out of sight in every direction the halls offered. And behind each pane — at some depth the glass declined to specify, the way deep water declines — a face. A person. Not an image of a person: the party stopped arguing with themselves about this within the first aisle, because images do not breathe, and these did — slowly, at some other rhythm than time, a breath to the minute, a breath to the year — men and women and children and the very old, of every people the world keeps and some it has stopped keeping, each alone behind their own glass, eyes closed, held mid-heartbeat in an expression the glass had chosen: this one kept at the instant of laughing; this one kept at the instant before weeping; this one kept mid-word, lips parted around a syllable that has been arriving for a hundred years. @[Amyl](amyl) walked the first aisle with his staff held off the floor, the way you carry a thing through a sickroom, and said what the record adopts as the technical description: “It is not a prison. Look at their faces. It is not a prison, and that is worse.”

Deeper in, the aisles opened into the galleries, and the galleries are where the party understood whose house this was. Vast rooms, each holding one thing: not a person behind glass but an event inside it — whole hours of the world, kept at full scale, walled in panes the size of Nesmé's gates and lit from within by their own remembered light. They walked into a hall that held a midsummer bonfire dance in a village none of them could place and no map has kept: the fire mid-leap, three hundred dancers mid-turn, dogs and children weaving the edges, every face open with the specific joy of people who do not know they are being kept — and the room smelled, faintly, impossibly, of woodsmoke. A hall that held a harbor city meeting a wave taller than its bell-towers, kept at the exact crest of the instant before, all its small figures turned to look up together, terror and awe fused into one thing with no name in any tongue the party owned. A hall that held a single room at dawn — a birth, the child just crying, the mother just laughing, the light just arriving — and @[Saeron](saeron) stood in that one until @[Iratius](iratius) took his arm. A hall that held a burial in deep snow with one mourner, and the mourner's back, and the mourner's stillness, and nothing else for acres. Events chosen the way a collector chooses: not the largest hours of the world but the fullest — the hours when feeling ran clean. I have dressed sets for a table of seven for two years, and I want it on the record that I could not have built these rooms. I lack the taste. I mean that in both directions.

It was @[Corvyn](corvyn) who said the true thing about the architecture, and he said it in a mason's whisper with his hat off, which is how his family says church things. “Nothing here is mortared,” he said. “Look. Nothing touches. Every pane stands alone; every hall keeps one thing; nothing leans on anything.” And then, because he had stood in a Remembrance where a village sang its dead into a wall course by course, shoulder to shoulder, feeling mortared to feeling on purpose: “It is a memory-wall. It is a memory-wall built by someone who learned the lesson backward.” Nobody improved on that, then or since, and the record is not going to try now.

The disciplines strained and held. The galleries are a cellar of vintages and the six were lamps; @[Aelendir](aelendir) rationed them room by room, called the rope-line's bar at every threshold, and kept them from lingering anywhere the smell of woodsmoke got under the tune — because the fortress was not attacking them, which was the trouble. It was exhibiting. Twice they saw wardens: tall figures far down a crossing aisle, stitched out of the old descriptions, moving with the deliberation of curators — and both times the warden paused, and averted itself, turning its face away with a courtesy that had law in it, and the party remembered what the scholars say about the gaze of such things, and returned the courtesy at speed. And under everything, always, the chimes: small bell-tones sounding in the far halls, unhurried, exact — and once, close by, a chime and then a phrase of music, @[seven notes](the-tune) the party knew better than their own names, arriving out of the air of an empty aisle a full breath before Aelendir, white-faced, stopped humming. @[The Vault](the-vault) had their song. The Vault had, by the sound of it, more of their song than they had played yet.

They found the mother before they found the man they had come for, and they did not know her for who she was, and the record will keep to what they saw. In a quiet aisle off a quiet hall: a pane holding a small warm room in Ormath — lamplight, a good rug worn to threads, a woman at a table across from a visitor the glass kept only as a gray-eyed shape at the lamplight's edge. The woman was kept mid-sentence, chin lifted, bargaining — you can tell bargaining at any distance, in any light — and she was beautiful in the worn, deliberate way of people whose beauty is a trade, and there was something in the set of her jaw, in the particular geometry of cheek and brow, that made @[Amyl](amyl) slow, and frown, and file it, familiar-shaped and unplaceable, like a word in a language adjacent to one you speak. The glass had kept one detail with special care, the way collectors keep provenance: her hand, flat on the table, palm down, in the gesture of a woman closing terms. They walked on. The record walks on too. Some panes are keeping their chapter.

The direction that had led them across two countries ended at an aisle like every other aisle, and at the end of it, small against the glass, stood their friend. The record has waited eleven chapters to write this paragraph and finds, now, that it wants to be accurate more than it wants to be anything else. @[Sov Thimron](sov) stood facing a pane the way a man stands at a window in winter — close, still, arms at his sides — and he had been standing there, by every sign the six could read, since he had arrived: the gauntness of his face was the gauntness of days, not hours; his lips were cracked; his knees had the lock of a man who has forgotten he is standing on anything; and on the floor around his feet the fortress's placeless light fell in a shape that suggested it had gotten used to him. He did not turn when they entered the aisle. He did not turn when @[Amyl](amyl) said his name, or when @[Tirig](tirig) did, or when @[Saeron](saeron) did — and it was Saeron, watching the stillness of the shoulders, who raised a hand and stopped the others, because he had stood his own vigils, and he knew the difference between a man who will not turn and a man who cannot afford to.

Behind the glass was @[a young man asleep](torrin). The record will describe him once, carefully, because he has been a name in a graveyard for the whole length of this book and he deserves the ink: broad through the shoulder and gentled through the face, a workman gone to sleep mid-breath — hair the color of wheat with copper in it, a fair face freckled by outdoor summers, a jaw carrying a few days of red-gold stubble; forearms wrapped in a mason's linen, the wraps gone soft with washing; a leather apron still on, as though sleep had taken him between one task and the next, with a small round medal pinned at the bib, polished by a thumb's worrying. He slept the way the kept sleep — a breath at some rhythm other than time's — and one arm lay curved on the empty air beside him, holding its shape around an absence exactly the size of someone. The six looked at the arm, and then at their friend's face, and understood the arithmetic of the vigil without one word being spent on it.

“He is a promise,” @[Sov](sov) said at last. His voice had days of disuse in it, and no greeting, and no surprise, as though the six had merely stepped into a conversation he was already holding. “I do not know whose. I do not know for what.” His eyes never left the glass. “I have been trying to read the terms.” And then, in the smaller voice of a man reporting the one fact he is sure of from the far side of an emptied memory: “I do not know him. Understand that. I do not know his name, or his trade, or his town, and I do not know my own — do not tell me it; not yet; it does not fit yet — and everything in me that is still standing has been standing here since I arrived, because whatever was taken out of me, the shape of it is on the other side of this glass, sleeping, with its arm around where I go.”

They did not argue with him, because there was nothing in it to argue with; every word of it was the truth, told from underneath. They did the other thing. @[Amyl](amyl) came and stood at his shoulder, at vigil-distance, and said nothing at all, and @[Sov](sov)'s eyes filled and ran over without his face knowing why, and he let it happen the way you let weather happen. @[Aelendir](aelendir) sat down cross-legged in the aisle, unlatched the case at last — the first time on the far side of any door — and played @[the tune](the-tune), once, quietly, all the way through: and Sov's breath fell into its meter by the fourth bar, the way a tired man's step falls into a road he has walked all his life, and his hands, at his sides, began without him to move through the grammar of a knot. And @[Tirig](tirig) unwrapped the long bundle from her pack and held it out across both palms, hilt forward, the way you return a thing to its craftsman. Sov looked down at it for a long time. Then he took it — not the way a man takes a weapon; the way a man takes his own plane off a stranger's bench, certain of the balance before his fingers close — and something in his shoulders came down half an inch, and he said, to the sword, in mild wonder: “It was easy.” And did not know why he said it, and did not need to, and neither does anyone reading this who has been paying attention.

It was @[Amyl](amyl) who asked to see, because Amyl asks with his hands, and @[Sov](sov) — who could not have said why he trusted the small rot-scholar with the harpoon, only that everything in him did — unlaced his shirt without argument. The wound was there. Through the chest, exactly where the whole party's memory had it — and closed. Not healed: closed — sealed edge to edge with a seam of pale glass, cool under Amyl's fingers, faintly translucent, breathing light the way the panes breathed, and the flesh around it neither angry nor knitting: simply accepting it, the way old trees accept a fence-wire grown through. Amyl read it a long time with his fingertips and gave the party the only diagnosis this record has ever heard him refuse to soften. “This is not healed,” he said. “This is held.” And the record, which has walked you through this whole house of kept instants, will say the rest plainly, because the party spent the walk out arriving at it and deserves the words: he is alive. His death is also alive. They are wearing the same body, and the glass is the border.

And the record must set one more thing in this aisle, because leaving it out would be the record's own almost. In the party's baggage, wrapped in oilcloth, lay a @[knife that opens glass](the-obsidian-knife). Every one of them thought of it. The record has taken testimony, and it was every one of them, and none of them said it aloud. @[Corvyn](corvyn)'s hand — the owner's hand — went once to his pack-strap, and stopped, and came away; because a cut is not an asking; because a stranger's sentence stood in the doorway of every mind in that aisle — it does not keep what it is not given — and because whatever the glass was holding for the sleeping man and the standing one, opening it by edge would make of it the one thing it must never be. Stolen.

The asking almost happened. The record includes it because leaving it out would be a lie of omission, and this book has rules. @[Sov](sov) turned back to the pane, with the sword in his hands and his friends around him, and raised one hand to the glass — to ask; there is no other reading; to ask the house for the sleeping man the way the house had, evidently, at some hour, been asked for him — and before his palm touched, the glass fogged. Once. From the inside. A small bloom of breath-mist at exactly the height of a sleeping mouth, and gone. Sov held his hand where it was, an inch from the pane, for the length of three chimes from three far halls. Then he lowered it. “No,” he said, softly, to himself, or to the glass, or to the house — “you do not wake a man to ask him to wait.” And he stepped back from the pane, one step, which those present rank among the strongest things they have ever watched anyone do.

They left the way they had come, and the house let them, which is not the same as the house doing nothing. The party walked out of the aisle with @[Sov](sov) in their middle — gaunt, nameless to himself, drinking in small sips from @[Amyl](amyl)'s skin because his body had to be reminded of its own terms — and as they passed down the last long corridor of standing panes, every pane they passed turned. Slightly. A degree, two degrees, frameless dark glass pivoting on nothing with a sound like a held breath being held better — not toward the six, the record must be exact: toward him — a corridor of the kept inclining toward the retrieved, all the way to the door, which every one of them understood to be a message, and none of them chose to translate in front of the others.

And one more thing went untranslated, at the very door. As they passed out under the entrance that stands at the scale of weather, with the gray beyond it and the colonnades drowned in their patient sea, the seam in @[Sov](sov)'s chest fogged. Once. From the inside. A small bloom of breath-mist under the pale glass, at the pace of the panes, gone between one stride and the next. He did not break step. The party did not speak of it for three days. Nobody translated it, and everybody carried the translation.

Outside, the gray took them back with something that was almost tact. Nobody spoke until the drowned colonnades. Then @[Iratius](iratius), who counts what can be counted, said the accounting aloud: they had walked into the stronghold of a power none of them could name, and taken back what it had taken, and paid nothing, and been stopped by no one — and @[Aelendir](aelendir), looking up at the birds turning their slow silent wheels against the walls' far weather, finished the ledger in the voice he saves for true things: “We were stopped by no one because we took nothing it considers taken. It does not keep what it is not given — and it has let us walk out with the asking still owed. Nothing in that house is done with us. We have been issued.” And somewhere behind them, deep in the patient dark, a chime rang — unhurried, exact, early — and this time nobody asked what for. The Vault keeps its own schedule. Somewhere in the pages ahead of this sentence, something has already been rung.
