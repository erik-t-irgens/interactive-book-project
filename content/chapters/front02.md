# On the Doors
*Six Roads Out of Harvest — where the party would have gone, and whose story waited on each road*

@audio: door-threshold fade=8
Everything before this page happened. It happened at a table, on ordinary evenings, with dice and cold tea and people talking over each other, and I have tried to set it down faithfully. Everything after this page is interpretation. The campaign ended where the Harvest ended; the road beyond it was built and never walked. What follows is that road as it was prepared — the events as they were meant to find you, told as one telling among the many the table would have made of them. Where I have had to choose what you would have done, I have chosen what I know of you. Argue with me. That is what the blank pages at the back are for.

Part One scattered its evidence, and you will have felt it gathering: a @[crystal that leans north](the-heart-of-winters-veil) in a closed fist. @[A sword that warms](volcaris) toward something that will not hold still. Black feathers laid straight as compass needles on sills and graves. A summons read aloud into a gold-lit hall. An invitation with an invoice folded inside it. A page taken from a dead place, folded smaller every evening. And under all of it, @[a tune the whole company knew](the-tune) and none of you could source. None of these read as doors at the time. Pulls rarely do. A pull is a door heard through a wall — a call to action that arrives before the understanding does, and is answered on credit.

There are @[seven doors](the-doors) in this book. Six of them stand in the country ahead — the North and the West and one place that is neither — and each of the six belongs to one of you: bought with what you carried out of the gray, addressed by name, answerable by nobody else. The seventh is not missing. It waits deeper in the book than the others, for reasons the book will earn.

The doors did not open in the order of persons, and the chapters that follow keep the road's order, not the heart's. This is because doors, like most true things, have prerequisites. Some could only be reached through another — one door opens onto the very country where the next one stands. Some could only be answered at the proper hour — there is a night in the northern year when the veil between the living and the dead wears thinnest, and one door would open on that night or not at all. And some had to come first simply because their keeper was called first, and the company does not leave a member to answer alone. You will recognize the logic as you go. It is the logic of a journey, which is the only logic any of this ever had.

A word on the shape of what follows. Most of these chapters walk together, all seven of you in them, and speak of the company as the company. But when the road reaches a door, the chapter turns and speaks to its keeper — to you, singular, by right. Those chapters were written to be read the way letters are read: privately first, and then, if you can bear it, aloud. And each of them closes with a showing — the gray's, not mine. You stood in that dreamscape together at the very beginning, before any of you knew what you were standing in. In these chapters it begins, at last, to show you why. I will not explain the showings. They explain themselves, the way weather does, given time.

One promise, and then the road. Nothing in this part is punishment, and nothing in it is decoration. Every door asks its keeper the one question that follows them, and behind every door is something that was kept — not hidden; kept. The difference between those two words is, more or less, the entire second half of this book.
